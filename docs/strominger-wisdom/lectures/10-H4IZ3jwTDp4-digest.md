# Digest — Lecture 10: Soft Hair on Black Holes

- **Title:** Soft Hair on Black Holes (Lecture 10 of 10, *Infrared Structure of Gravity and Gauge Theory*, Harvard)
- **Speaker:** Andrew Strominger
- **Video id:** `H4IZ3jwTDp4` — https://youtu.be/H4IZ3jwTDp4
- **Duration:** 01:44:14
- **Source file:** `refs/strominger-videos/10-H4IZ3jwTDp4.md` (auto-captions; secondary source, not L3 ground truth)
- **Written companion:** arXiv:1703.05448 §"Black Holes" / §"Soft Hair" (`refs/arxiv-1703.05448/soft_Arxiv_update.tex` ll. 2657–3022)

**Arc.** The final lecture applies the semester's machinery to the information paradox: charge
conservation forces a horizon contribution, so black holes carry infinitely many charges; an explicit
shock wave implants a soft photon and then a soft graviton on the horizon; supertranslated Schwarzschild
carries classical superrotation charge. All of that is in the written notes. The side channel is
different and, for our purposes, more useful: a sustained defence — under repeated audience pressure —
of *why* it is legitimate to choose a gauge and tell an interior story at all; a plain statement of what
is assumed rather than proved (matching conditions, unitarity); an $\hbar$-counting aside that justifies
the clean split between classical formation and quantum evaporation; two named open problems he says he
tried and failed to solve; a technical argument that AdS is the wrong place to pose this problem; and a
carefully hedged personal bet on how far this programme gets.

The methodological answer to an unfalsifiable worry. Not a proof strategy, a research strategy.

### 5. The one-step argument for soft hair, compressed
The whole conclusion in a sentence, and it is a sentence the written notes do not quite say. Once you
accept (a) black holes are labelled by angular momentum and (b) BMS is a symmetry, then because there is
no preferred angular momentum — the BMS generators differ by conjugation with supertranslations and are
all on the same footing — infinitely many charges follow immediately.

> "once you say the words black holes are characterized by angular momentum and uh BMS symmetry it
> immediately follows that the black holes must be carry carry an infinite number of different kinds of
> charges"

`[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 00:12:43]` — tags: soft-hair, charges, black-holes, supertranslations

### 6. An $\hbar$-counting aside that licenses the whole split-slice argument
Set as a live exercise, then worked out: the power radiated goes as $\hbar^2/M^2$, quanta carry energy
$\sim\hbar/M$, so the waiting time for the *first* Hawking quantum is $\sim M/\hbar$. Everything
classical — formation, transients, ringdown — happens on timescales with no $\hbar$ in them. That is why
one may cleanly separate classical formation from quantum evaporation, which the later argument needs.

> "we have to wait a time of 1 over H bar just to see one Hawking Quantum which means that the kind of
> classical period of the black hole is very very long … we have a clean division a very clean division
> … between the classical black hole formation and the onset of of Hawking evaporation"

`[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 00:22:19]` — tags: methodology, black-holes, soft-hair

### 7. Gauge choices are intuition-generating devices, and different choices teach different things
The charge identity $Q=\int d\varepsilon\wedge\star F + \int \varepsilon\,\star J$ holds for *any*
$\varepsilon$ going to one at infinity. He stresses that all such choices are equally correct and that
one picks the one that reveals the physics one is after — you could even choose $\varepsilon$ to vanish
at some charges and not others.

> "this is a true formula for any Epsilon we could choose Epsilon to vanish at some charges and not at
> others … but in this case clearly it's a good idea to choose Epsilon to be constant everywhere … it
> gets us a better idea of of the of the local physics"

`[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 00:25:26]` (continues at `00:26:29`) — tags: methodology, charges, large-gauge

### 8. …with the caveat attached in the same breath
He does not oversell the interior picture even while constructing it.

> "ultimately in quantum gravity the metric fluctuates and local physics probably is not well defined"

### 9. The cleanest statement of the surface-integral / volume-integral divide
Under sustained audience pushback ("I just don't want anything to change if I make a gauge
transformation"), this is where he lands. The gauge-invariant content is the boundary statement that
charge flows into the hole; the moment you want a story about *where* it is, you have chosen a gauge.

> "if you're not going to choose a gauge you're only going to be able to discuss the surface integral if
> … the minute you want to discuss a volume integral you must choose some gauge"

### 14. A second named gap: the finite non-linear supertranslating shock wave
He believes an exact non-linear solution describing a shock wave that generates a supertranslation is
probably constructible, and reports that as far as he knows nobody has written it down.

> "it's probably possible to analytically solve for a finite non-linear solution corresponding to a
> shock wave that generates a super translation [supertranslation] but exactly that formula doesn't seem
> to be in the literature anywhere that I know of"

`[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 01:18:21]` — tags: open-problems, supertranslations, soft-hair

### 15. Deliberate isolation strategy: throw away the zero mode to expose the new structure
Asked why he restricted to a net-neutral, angle-dependent shell, the answer is a piece of research
craft — the $\ell=0$ piece only reproduces something already known, so it is discarded to make the new
phenomenon visible.

> "I would learn that if I throw charge into a black hole I get a Charged black hole I think we knew
> that before so I wanted to just throw that piece away and study the new interesting phenomena which is
> what happens when you have these angular dependent charts [charges]"

`[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 00:53:23]` — tags: methodology, soft-hair, large-gauge

### 16. Gravity is harder than electromagnetism for a simple physical reason: there are no zero-energy waves
A crisp asymmetry that explains why the supertranslation version of the construction is messier than the
photon one. You can send in a shell with zero net charge but non-zero charge moments; you cannot send in
a shell with zero net energy.

> "unlike in the case of a charge here there is no escaping the zero mode you can't send in a zero energy
> shock wave you can send in a zero charge shock wave which has charge moments but there's no such thing
> the laws of physics do not allow us to have zero energy waves"

> "this greens function has come up before in Sasha's lecture on gravitational memory this is the green

### 18. He raises the natural objection to soft hair himself

> "you might worry about the following thing there's there's different kinds of black holes which differ
> by different number of soft photons but how do we know you know how could we tell from the outside uh
> the difference between them you know do they evaporate differently"

### 19. …and the answer is emphasised as *deterministic*, not statistical
The word choice is the point. Charge conservation relates the evaporation products of $|M\rangle$ and
$Q_\varepsilon|M\rangle$ exactly, not on average.

> "this is gives me an infinite number of deterministic relationships which relate the outcome … I have
> a deterministic formula not a probabilistic formula for the final evaporative outcome of the black
> hole with one extra soft Photon"

### 20. Gravity beats electromagnetism on measurability of the hair
A framing contrast he draws at the end: electromagnetic soft hair shows up as phases, needing a quantum
interference experiment; supertranslation hair shows up as a *classical* superrotation charge, because
supertranslations and superrotations do not commute.

> "unlike the electromagnetic case where the charges were the effect was to um introduce faces [phases]
> which could only be measured in Quantum experiments here we actually see that the um actual super
> rotation charges of the black hole are changed under classically under super translation"

### 21. Where the interior slice can go, and why he prefers it not to
Asked whether the charge integral must terminate on the horizon, he says no — you can push it through
the interior and call the result the black hole's supertranslation charge — and then gives his reason
for not doing so.

> "you don't have to end it there you could extend it in … it ended there because it's a dicey thing to
> talk about the inside of a black hole but yes we could just take it right through the black hole"

`[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 00:14:50]` — tags: methodology, charges, black-holes

### 22. A modelling choice made on taste, said out loud
He could have run the whole argument with eternal black holes acted on by large gauge transformations.
He does not, for a reason he is happy to admit is aesthetic.

> "we could have eternal black holes which we then Super translate … yeah somehow I don't like having
> white holes so I prefer to do it this way"

`[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 01:15:09]` — tags: methodology, black-holes

### 23. Historical positioning: previous "quantum hair" was always finite in number
Answering a question about earlier quantum-hair literature (discrete gauge symmetries, $B$ fields). Those
gave a small finite number of labels; the novelty claimed here is the infinity, both classical and
quantum.

> "they were usually some small finite number and there was some additional small finite number of types
> of quantum hair but but the Infinity of classical and Quantum hair is a new a new thing"

`[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 01:34:32]` — tags: history, soft-hair, black-holes

### 24. AdS is the wrong laboratory for the information paradox — the sharp technical reason
Not just "AdS is different". The specific claim is that the flat-space vacuum degeneracy has no AdS
analogue at fixed energy: acting with Virasoro in AdS$_3$ *changes* the energy, so the degenerate-states
observation simply does not apply there.

> "it is not true in ads [AdS] that there is an infinite number that there that there is a large or
> infinite degeneracy of black holes at every energy level acting with the virusaro [Virasoro] algebra
> changes the energy so if we keep the energy fixed this observation is not relevant"

`[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 01:39:19]` — tags: black-holes, open-problems, celestial, methodology

### 25. …and the accompanying opinion about where the field should go
A judgment on twenty years of effort, delivered mildly.

> "there is no sharp information problem in in ads because black holes don't evaporate … it is a context
> in which the information problem is hard to pose and I don't think we've made a lot of you know
> significant progress on it so I think it's time for a new approach and certainly flat space is the
> most natural home for the information problem"

`[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 01:38:15]` — tags: open-problems, black-holes, history, methodology

### 26. The personal bet, hedged in both directions
The most calibrated sentence in the lecture, and a good model for how to state a research position
without overclaiming. He thinks the old arguments are broken and that this is progress; he does not
think it is the answer; he sees no obstacle and no imminent resolution.

> "personally my personal Prejudice is I doubt that everything I've taught you in this course is is
> qualitatively enough to solve the information problem … I think we've turned the problem upside down
> and uh seen that the old arguments are wrong … and there I don't see an obstacle to solving the
> information problem through this but neither do I think it's around the next corner"

`[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 01:41:26]` — tags: open-problems, black-holes, methodology, history

### 27. The correction is aimed at a textbook fact, and he says which one
Rhetorically pointed, and useful for gauging how much of the claim is meant to be surprising.

> "so it's not true what you've been hearing since you were babies that that there are only a three
> parameter family of black holes there's an infinite number of inequivalent uh Quantum states of black

`[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 00:41:29]` — tags: soft-hair, black-holes, history, pitfalls

### 28. What the horizon soft photon does and does not change
A compact operational characterisation of a soft mode: it costs no energy, commutes with the
Hamiltonian, but shifts angular momentum by a unit, so the resulting state must be orthogonal.

> "it won't change the energy it commutes with the hamiltonian we know that because we can write it as a
> boundary integral up here but um it will for example it's a photon so it will change the angular
> momentum by one unit"

## Deltas vs the written lectures (arXiv:1703.05448)

The technical content of this lecture is the §"Soft Hair" material of the notes, and the closing
two-flaws-in-Hawking's-argument summary appears in §"Discussion" nearly verbatim. The differences are
these.

- **A methodological defence that the notes only gesture at.** The notes say in one sentence that
  extending an asymptotic symmetry inward "is gauge dependent" and that a gauge choice "can be useful in
  developing a picture and intuition". The lecture spends roughly ten minutes on this under audience
  challenge, and arrives at a much sharper formulation: the surface integral is gauge invariant, the
  volume integral requires a gauge, and the interior story is a chosen picture rather than a result
  (insights 7–12). This is directly transferable methodology for the campaign — it is the argument for
  why a frame-dependent construction can still carry frame-independent content.
- **Assumptions are named as assumptions.** Neither the matching-condition assumption under non-linear
  dynamics (insights 1–2) nor the assumption of unitarity (insights 3–4) is flagged this explicitly in
  the notes; `unitarity` occurs twice in the TeX and not in this role. The spoken "if we don't assume
  there's some kind of matching conditions we just don't have a theory" has no written counterpart.
- **The $\hbar$-counting exercise is not in the notes.** The notes assert the clean separation between
  classical formation and quantum evaporation; the lecture derives the timescale $M/\hbar$ live and uses
  it to justify treating $\mathcal{H}^< \cup \mathcal{I}^{+<}$ as effectively Cauchy (insight 6).
- **Two open problems named on the spot, with failed attempts admitted.** Harmonic-gauge analysis of
  soft-hair implantation (insight 13) and the exact finite non-linear supertranslating shock wave
  (insight 14). Neither is listed as an open problem in the notes.
- **The AdS comparison is much sharper spoken than written.** The notes discuss AdS/CFT only as
  motivation for flat-space holography. The lecture gives an explicit technical disanalogy — no infinite
  degeneracy at fixed energy in AdS$_3$ because Virasoro shifts the energy — plus a judgment that the
  paradox cannot be sharply posed there (insights 24–25).
- **"Deterministic, not probabilistic" is an emphasis the notes do not make.** The notes speak of
  "exact correlations"; the lecture repeatedly stresses that the relation between the evaporation
  products of $|M\rangle$ and $|M\rangle$-plus-a-soft-photon is a deterministic formula (insight 19), and
  it arrives as the answer to a self-posed objection (insight 18).
- **The memory cross-link is explicit here.** The lecture points out aloud that the Green's function
  needed for the supertranslating shock wave is the one from the memory lecture (insight 17). The notes
  present both formulas but do not draw the reader's attention to their identity in this direction.
- **Reverse deltas (in the notes, absent from the lecture).** Three points worth knowing are written but
  not spoken: (i) the argument that horizon soft modes are simultaneously "too many and too few" to give
  $S_{BH}$ — too many for lack of a UV cutoff, too few because of a flavour problem where many infalling
  configurations give the same hairdo; (ii) the suggestion that soft hair may be a description of edge
  modes; (iii) the footnote that Planck-scale localisation may render the number of hairs finite. The
  lecture's live version of "what would it mean to solve the information problem" at `01:42:30` matches
  the notes closely, including the "not a squiggly line" phrasing, so it is *not* a delta.
