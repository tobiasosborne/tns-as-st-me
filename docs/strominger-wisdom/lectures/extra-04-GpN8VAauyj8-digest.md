# Digest — Infrared Divergences in QED and Quantum Gravity

- **Title:** Infrared Divergences in QED and Quantum Gravity
- **Speaker:** Andrew Strominger
- **Venue/date:** **Strings 2017, Tel Aviv** (he thanks the organisers "in beautiful Tel Aviv"; the conference
  ran 26–30 June 2017). He refers to a paper of his own from "the last week" — arXiv:1706.00436, *Black Hole
  Information Revisited* — and to "a very interesting paper last week by these authors" on decoherence from
  tracing over soft quanta (Carney–Chaurette–Neuenfeld–Semenoff, arXiv:1706.03782, is the likely referent).
- **Video id:** `GpN8VAauyj8` — https://youtu.be/GpN8VAauyj8
- **Duration:** 00:34:47 (talk to ~00:29:02, then questions)
- **Source file:** `refs/strominger-videos/extra-04-GpN8VAauyj8.md` (auto-captions; secondary source, not L3 ground truth)
- **Written companion:** arXiv:1703.05448 §"Infrared Divergences"; arXiv:1706.00436

**Arc.** A tight conference talk in two halves. The first recovers Faddeev–Kulish from the conservation laws:
Bhabha scattering of undressed charges violates the matching condition, therefore its amplitude must vanish,
and infrared divergences are how quantum field theory enforces that; dress the charges to satisfy the
constraint and the amplitude is finite. The second half spends the reinterpretation on three conjectures —
physical (undressed-in, dressed-out) states are infrared finite; the same holds in non-abelian gauge theory;
and black hole purity is restored by correlations between hard Hawking quanta and the soft quanta the
conservation laws force to accompany them. The side-channel worth harvesting is the *framing* of infrared
divergences as a penalty rather than a pathology, a firm correction to how everyone pictures a
Faddeev–Kulish cloud, and a Q&A in which he says plainly that the Faddeev–Kulish basis is probably
incomplete, may fail to cluster, and that entanglement entropy across a cut of $\mathcal{I}$ may not even be
well defined.

## Insights

### 1. Infrared divergences are a penalty, not a pathology
The organising idea of the talk, stated twice and worth quoting in full. If a process violates one of the
infinitely many conservation laws, the amplitude must vanish; the divergences are the machinery by which the
theory enforces that, exponentiating to $e^{-\infty}=0$. On this reading there are no "real" infrared
divergences in QED at all.

> "the role of infrared divergences in QED is if you try to compute something that is forbidden by the ...
> conservation laws[,] QFT is much cleverer than you and it will set that amplitude to zero by introducing
> infrared divergences which just shut it down"
> … "there are no real infrared divergences in QED[;] they're just a penalty that we pay if we are so foolish
> [as] to try to compute something that must be zero due to an elementary conservation law"

### 2. A firm correction: the Faddeev–Kulish cloud does not travel with the particle
The most useful warning in the talk. The standard mental picture — a charge dragging a mysterious photon
halo — is wrong, because photons reach null infinity while massive charges reach timelike infinity. The cloud
disperses; its function is to satisfy a long-distance constraint, not to accompany anything.

> "if you look at the old papers of [Faddeev] and [Kulish] ... one gets the impression that what happens is
> that you have a charged particle going off to infinity surrounded by some mysterious cloud of photons but
> of course that doesn't happen[.] photons go to null infinity and ... massive particles [go] to time like
> infinity[,] so this cloud of photons doesn't stick with the particle[,] it disperses out to n[ull]
> infinity[,] and its real goal in life is to satisfy the conservation law[.] it's a long distance effect"

`[GpN8VAauyj8 @ 00:16:06]` — tags: dressing, pitfalls, ir-divergences

### 3. …and the cloud is highly ambiguous — only its pole structure matters
The corollary, and the licence for the first conjecture. Nothing about the cloud's location is physical; what
must be right is the $1/\omega$ behaviour, so that the Coulomb field is shielded and the constraint holds.

> "the [Faddeev]-Kulish clouds are highly ambiguous[;] [the] only thing that matters about them is[,] as they
> emphasize[,] is that they have the right pole structure[.] they don't have to be near the particle[,] they
> just have to shield the Coulomb field so that the conservation law is satisfied"

`[GpN8VAauyj8 @ 00:17:10]` — tags: dressing, ir-divergences, charges

### 4. Faddeev–Kulish read as an advanced-potential prescription
A framing of the first conjecture that the written version does not use. What Faddeev and Kulish did amounts
to solving for the radiative modes with the advanced potential everywhere; the physical prescription is to use
the natural one for incoming and outgoing pieces separately, which is what moving the cloud from the in-state
to the out-state accomplishes.

> "it's like the difference between solving the scattering problem with the advanced ... potential[.] what
> [Faddeev] [and] kulish effectively did is they ... use the advanced potential to find the radiative modes
> for all incoming things and the ones for the outgoing things[.] so the conjecture is that if you just use
> the one which is a more physical one for ingoing and outgoing that the infrared divergences will cancel"

### 6. The non-abelian conjecture, offered as a pure extrapolation
He is explicit that the reasoning is by analogy — the non-abelian infrared structure is much more
complicated, and the conjecture is that the conservation laws remain the whole story anyway.

> "it's natural to conjecture that even in non abelian gauge Theory[,] where the structure of the infrared
> divergences is a lot more complicated[,] that as long as we satisfy all the conservation law[s] there will
> be no infrared divergences"

`[GpN8VAauyj8 @ 00:20:26]` — tags: ir-divergences, open-problems, charges

### 7. Every Hawking quantum is hard — that is what makes the argument bite
The hinge of the black hole conjecture, stated more plainly than in print: the thermal distribution goes like
$\omega^2$ at small energy, so it has no pole; a state of purely hard quanta cannot satisfy the conservation
laws; therefore soft quanta must accompany the radiation.

> "we can think of all of the Hawking quanta as hard quanta because their densit[y] goes to zero with Omega[,]
> there is no pole"
> … "it is impossible to make a bunch of hard quanta without also there being a bunch of soft quanta whose
> distributions have poles at Omega equals zero"

`[GpN8VAauyj8 @ 00:21:30]`, `[GpN8VAauyj8 @ 00:23:38]` — tags: black-holes, soft-theorems, ir-divergences

### 8. A retrospective judgment on Page's picture
He does not soften it: on his reading each of Page's states individually violates a conservation law, so the
correlations that restore purity cannot be the ones Page proposed. His alternative puts the correlations
between hard and soft rather than between early and late.

> "here the purity is restored not as Pa[g]e conjectured between the late time [and] the early time hard
> quanta but between the hard quanta[,] which are completely uncorrelated with one another[,] and the soft
> quanta which must appear due to the conservation law"

`[GpN8VAauyj8 @ 00:24:41]` — tags: black-holes, open-problems, soft-hair

### 9. …and the honest hole in it, named without prompting
He states that the argument only *almost* works: two exactly collinear gravitons of half the energy carry the
same supertranslation charge. He rescues it with a measure-zero argument and with superrotation charge, which
sees the spin.

> "we could have two gravitons with half the energy going to exactly the same angle and that would give the
> same [supertranslation] charge[,] so there's a ... [subtlety] there[,] but the probability of ... two
> exactly collinear hard particles is zero in the Hawking distribution ... now this doesn't see the graviton
> spin but there's a second conserved quantity[,] the super rotation charge[,] which does see ... the
> graviton spin"

### 11. The Faddeev–Kulish basis is probably incomplete, and may not cluster
The sharpest technical opinion in the recording, given in answer to a question about unitarity. Three separate
judgments: incomplete (it excludes the physically prepared states), unitarity complicated by superselection
sectors, and — the interesting one — the possibility that the theory restricted to a charge sector is unitary
but fails to cluster.

> "the question of whether the [Faddeev]-[Kulish] basis is a complete[,] unitary basis[,] I don't think it's
> been fully addressed[,] but I would say it's incomplete because ... it doesn't allow you just a plus and
> min[us]. at the LHC we don't scatter [Faddeev]-[Kulish] States ... whether or not you want to say it's
> unitary ... [is a] slightly subtle question because there['re] super selection sectors associated with the
> conserved charges ... it might be that it's unitary but doesn't cluster properly"

`[GpN8VAauyj8 @ 00:30:05]` — tags: dressing, charges, open-problems, ir-divergences

### 12. Entanglement across a cut of $\mathcal{I}$ may not be well defined
Asked why his mechanism and Page's are mutually exclusive, he declines to rule Page's out — "that would be a
fourth conjecture" — and then raises a prior worry: the soft graviton Hilbert space may not admit the
factorisation the question presupposes, and any candidate cut has to behave properly under supertranslations.

> "I'm not sure that entanglement entropy on scr[i] in flat space ... across a cut ... is well-defined[.]
> there are issues with dividing the hilbert space of the soft gravitons and ... it has to transform properly
> under super translations[,] and I wrote a paper with [Raclariu?] and [Kapec?] just pointing out that there
> are a lot of subtleties in that ... I don't know what it means to say late [or] early"

## Deltas vs the written sources (arXiv:1703.05448, arXiv:1706.00436)

Much of the derivational content — Bhabha vanishing, the dressed shock-wave solution, the Faddeev–Kulish
cancellation, the conjecture that all charge-conserving amplitudes are infrared finite — is in the written
lectures (`soft_Arxiv_update.tex` ll. 1772–1855), which also footnote that the QED conjecture was
subsequently proven by Akhoury and Choi. The deltas are therefore framing, warnings, and Q&A.

- **"Penalty, not pathology" (insight 1) is a spoken formulation.** The notes say "IR divergences arise only to
  enforce charge conservation". The talk's version — quantum field theory is cleverer than you and shuts the
  amplitude down; there are no real infrared divergences — is stronger, more memorable, and states a position
  rather than an observation. `penalty` occurs zero times in the notes.
- **The correction to the standard mental picture of a dressing cloud (insights 2–3) has no written
  counterpart.** The notes explain what the clouds *do*; only the talk says explicitly that the usual picture
  of a cloud accompanying the particle is wrong, and why (photons and massive charges reach different
  boundaries).
- **The advanced-potential reading of Faddeev–Kulish (insight 4)** does not appear in the notes, which describe
  the same manipulation operationally (omit the incoming dressing, act with its conjugate on the out-state).
- **The non-abelian conjecture (insight 6)** is stated here as a conjecture; the notes' conjecture is the QED
  one.
- **Q&A material that no paper carries:** the judgment that the Faddeev–Kulish basis is incomplete and may fail
  to cluster (11); the doubt about entanglement entropy across a cut of $\mathcal{I}$ and about the phrase
  "early and late" in this context (12); the restatement that the S-matrix remains the only observable (13).
  Of these, (11) and (12) are the most useful — they are places where the speaker's own programme is put under
  pressure and he concedes ground rather than defending.
- **The "four infinities of charges" count (14)** is an aside with no written analogue in that phrasing.
- **Reverse delta (in the notes, absent here):** the memory corner, the symmetry corner in any detail, the
  massive-particle case, colour memory, and the proof status of the QED conjecture (Akhoury–Choi appears in the
  notes as a footnote; the talk predates or ignores it).
