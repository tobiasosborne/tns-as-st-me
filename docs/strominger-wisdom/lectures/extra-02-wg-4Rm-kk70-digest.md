# Digest — Soft Hair on Black Holes

- **Title:** Soft Hair on Black Holes (part 1 of a two-part talk; Malcolm Perry gave part 2)
- **Speaker:** Andrew Strominger
- **Venue/date:** **Strings 2016, Beijing** — he thanks the organisers and names the conference in the first
  sentence. The talk is billed as 25 minutes.
- **Video id:** `wg-4Rm-kk70` — https://youtu.be/wg-4Rm-kk70
- **Duration:** 00:30:53
- **Source file:** `refs/strominger-videos/extra-02-wg-4Rm-kk70.md` (auto-captions; secondary source, not L3 ground truth)
- **Written companion:** arXiv:1601.00921 (Hawking–Perry–Strominger) and arXiv:1703.05448 §"Black Holes"

**Arc.** A compressed conference talk: the antipodal matching condition for the Liénard–Wiechert field, the
infinite family of conservation laws that follows, the identification of those laws with the soft photon
theorem and of the symmetry with large gauge transformations, vacuum degeneracy, and then the claim that
black holes must carry soft hair because the conservation laws cannot be waived when a black hole is
present. Because the format is tight, the side-channel is concentrated in two places: the opening framing
(a terminology complaint about the word "asymptotic", and an admission that he only recently noticed a
century-old feature of the Liénard–Wiechert field), and five minutes of questions at the end about whether
any of this is visible at an accelerator. The single most-quotable line is his refusal to claim more than he
has.

## Insights

### 1. "We have not solved the information paradox. I can't say this often enough."
Said at the end of the talk, unprompted, and phrased as something he has to keep repeating — which tells you
how the work was being received. The claim he does make is narrower and stated precisely: a flaw in the
argument for information loss, plus concrete next steps.

> "recent developments uh trying to understand the infrared structure of gravity have revealed a flaw
> underlying the argument that information is los[t]. We have not I can't say this often enough. We have not
> solved the information paradox."

### 2. "Asymptotic" is a bad word for an exact symmetry
A terminology complaint he makes in several talks, always in the same terms: the name suggests approximate
validity, and the symmetries are exact. The word only refers to where the data are most conveniently
described.

> "the word asmtoic [asymptotic] is a little bit unfortunate because it kind of suggests that they're not
> exact symmetries that they're only symmetries in some limit. In fact, they are exact symmetries. It's just
> uh that they are most easily understood uh in terms of the data at the asmtoic boundaries of a space time"

`[wg-4Rm-kk70 @ 00:02:14]` — tags: asymptotic-symmetries, pitfalls, methodology

### 3. He only recently noticed that the boosted-charge field is not single valued at spatial infinity
An admission of the "this was hiding in plain sight" kind, made about a formula that has been in Jackson for
a century. The whole programme rests on it.

> "I'm sure that Maxwell and Leonard [Liénard] and Vcart [Wiechert] knew this, but I only became fully aware
> of it myself a year or two ago that this formula has the bizarre property that um, it is not single valued
> as you go to spatial infinity."

`[wg-4Rm-kk70 @ 00:05:28]` — tags: history, charges, methodology

### 4. The infinity of conservation laws is trivial; the surprise is elsewhere
He is careful about which step is hard. Given the antipodal identification, the infinite family of conserved
charges follows with no work at all — he says so twice. The non-trivial content arrives only when you ask
what those laws say about quantum amplitudes.

> "I can uh take the antipital [antipodal] function and integrate it over the future of the past and those
> two things are equal because these are equal. It's completely trivial."

`[wg-4Rm-kk70 @ 00:08:40]` — tags: charges, methodology

### 5. …and an explicit disclaimer of novelty
Having derived a bizarre-looking infinite family of relations among S-matrix elements, he immediately says
they are not new. The contribution is the derivation, not the result.

> "But in fact, it's well known. We haven't discovered anything new here. We've just said something that's
> been known for 50 or maybe even 100 years, the s[oft] photon theorem. We've just derived it in a different
> way from uh starting from a conservation law."

`[wg-4Rm-kk70 @ 00:13:56]` — tags: soft-theorems, methodology, history

### 6. "Hair" means a distinguishing feature, nothing more
A short definitional aside worth keeping, because "hair" invites a picture of structure sitting near the
horizon. His usage is weaker and cleaner: a label that distinguishes otherwise identical states.

> "we can describe this by saying that the vacuum in gauge uh and gravitational the[o]ries has soft hair that
> is it has distinguishing features uh associated with soft particles uh zero energy particles"

`[wg-4Rm-kk70 @ 00:17:15]` — tags: soft-hair, vacuum-degeneracy, methodology

### 7. Memory is an experimental subject, and it is not what LIGO reported
Two sentences of framing that separate the DC effect from the oscillating signal. He is precise about the
condition: the shift is there *if the detectors are inertial*.

> "the so-called memory effect uh which is uh has been largely uh developed as an experimental uh subject.
> It's a long distance effect of gravity distinct from what's measured at LIGO. at LIGO they ... looked at
> the oscillating uh pendulums but uh if you uh analyze it very carefully according to general relativity
> there's a DC effect where at the end of the day if they are inertial detectors there will be a finite
> shift a memory of what went past."

`[wg-4Rm-kk70 @ 00:03:18]` — tags: memory, experiment

### 8. The soft-hair argument turns on *exact*, not average, conservation
The load-bearing word in the black hole part of the talk. Everyone already assumes angular momentum is
conserved quantum by quantum in Hawking evaporation; the argument is that there are infinitely many more
charges in exactly the same position.

> "it is always assumed that angular momentum in the process of Hawking radiation is exactly conserved. Not
> just on the average but exactly conserved."

`[wg-4Rm-kk70 @ 00:20:31]` — tags: black-holes, charges, soft-hair

### 9. Why the deep infrared was missed: every real measurement has an infrared cutoff
His answer to a questioner who objects that soft theorems cannot constrain anything actually measured at the
LHC. He first insists the soft theorems are load-bearing for collider calculations, then concedes the real
point and turns it into an explanation of the field's blind spot.

> "of course the soft theorems constrain the LHC amplitudes ... you can't do an LHC calculation without
> using the soft theorem. Now I think what you're getting at is that ... any actual measurement involves an
> infrared cut[o]ff and so um some of the deep infrared physics will be very hard to see ... I think that is
> part of the reason why uh this deep infrared structure has ... been missed or not studied"

`[wg-4Rm-kk70 @ 00:25:59]` — tags: ir-divergences, history, experiment, methodology

### 10. A concrete open question: how small must the infrared cutoff be to be sure no information escaped?
The best question in the recording, and he treats it as genuinely open and quantifiable. His guess — that the
cutoff may need to be below the inverse of the longest time scale in the problem — is offered as a guess.

> "it's a logical possibility that uh information can flow into the deep infrared and we need to quantify by
> how much that happens ... how how small do we need to make our infrared cut off? And it might be that we
> need to make it smaller than the longest time scale in the problem in order to be sure that we hadn't uh
> lost any any information."

`[wg-4Rm-kk70 @ 00:28:06]` — tags: open-problems, ir-divergences, experiment, black-holes

### 11. What he would bet on about singularities
Asked what happens if the spacetime has a singularity rather than the regular slicing used in the soft-hair
papers, he gives a clean conditional opinion: unitarity and a genuine singularity are hard to hold together,
so he expects the singularity to be resolved.

> "if unitar[ity] is preserved um which I think most people are assuming now ... it is tantamount to saying
> that there isn't a singularity[,] that higher derivative[,] stringy effects or quantum effects um somehow
> eliminate the singularity and allow information to go in there and and come back out"

`[wg-4Rm-kk70 @ 00:29:19]` — tags: black-holes, open-problems, methodology

### 12. A joke about how long this will take
Opening the talk, on the two-part format with Malcolm Perry. It reads as a joke and is also an accurate
forecast of a multi-year programme.

> "I'm giving part one of the talk. Malcolm will give part [two]. And parts three, four, and five will be at
> strings 2017. We we hope."

## Structure of the argument (thin skeleton)

- `00:01:07` The triangle in three sentences: soft theorems, asymptotic symmetries, memory.
- `00:05:28` The boosted point charge; the Liénard–Wiechert field is not single valued at $i^0$; antipodal
  matching.
- `00:08:40` Infinitely many conservation laws follow trivially; $\epsilon=$ const gives ordinary charge
  conservation, general $\epsilon$ gives a soft photon term.
- `00:12:51` Quantum version: the charge commutes with the S-matrix; this is the soft photon theorem.
- `00:13:56` Symmetry side: large gauge transformations, antipodally identified; vacuum degeneracy and soft hair.
- `00:19:23` Black hole information: the no-hair argument, the flaw, exact conservation of infinitely many charges.
- `00:25:59` Q&A: LHC observability and the infrared cutoff; singularities and unitarity.

## Deltas vs the written sources (arXiv:1601.00921, arXiv:1703.05448)

- **The anti-overclaiming line is the delta.** The written record states the soft-hair result and its
  limitations in the usual measured prose. Here he says "we have not solved the information paradox" and adds
  "I can't say this often enough" (insight 1) — a piece of information about the reception of the work that
  no paper carries.
- **A personal-discovery admission (insight 3).** The notes present the antipodal matching condition as a
  property of the retarded solution. The talk records that he had not noticed it until a year or two before,
  despite the formula's age. Compare the same admission in the Simons Center talk
  (`extra-05-F42XbX45Dzw`, `[00:11:16]`), where it is phrased as "I didn't realize until the last few years".
- **Explicit grading of which steps are hard (insights 4, 5).** The conservation laws are called "completely
  trivial" and the soft photon theorem is disclaimed as not new. The written lectures do not editorialise this
  way, and the grading is useful: it locates the actual content in the *translation* between corners rather
  than in either corner.
- **The infrared-cutoff question (insight 10) is not in the notes in this form.** The written record discusses
  infrared divergences and Faddeev–Kulish states at length, and mentions the LHC twice, but does not pose the
  quantitative question of how far below the longest time scale a detector's cutoff must sit before you can
  claim no information was lost. Posed here as an open problem worth quantifying.
- **A stated bet on singularity resolution (insight 11).** The notes assume regularity where convenient and do
  not commit. The talk gives a conditional prediction.
- **Reverse delta (in the notes, absent here):** the memory corner is mentioned in one sentence and never
  developed; there is no discussion of the horizon supertranslation charges, of Bondi gauge, or of the
  Faddeev–Kulish story. Part 2 of the talk (Perry) carried that material and is not in this recording.
