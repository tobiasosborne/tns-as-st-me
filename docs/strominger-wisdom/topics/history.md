## BMS and the fifty-year impasse

- **BMS were looking for the Poincaré group and expecting general relativity to reduce to special relativity in the infrared. They failed, people kept trying for years, and we now know why.** "General relativity has a spectacularly rich infrared structure that is just not there in special relativity, and BMS were the first to get a hint at this though I don't think they understood the full import of what they found." `[Yx7PWgfC1dw @ 00:20:34]`, setup at `[Yx7PWgfC1dw @ 00:18:29]` — [L7](../lectures/07-Yx7PWgfC1dw-digest.md); the compressed version at `[F42XbX45Dzw @ 00:05:59]` — [extra-05](../lectures/extra-05-F42XbX45Dzw-digest.md)
- **BMS did not think they had a symmetry, and he is candid that he does not understand how they *did* think of it.** `[Yx7PWgfC1dw @ 01:24:05]` — [L7](../lectures/07-Yx7PWgfC1dw-digest.md)
- **They never managed to construct the charges, and rival candidate groups — Newman–Unti among them — could not be excluded without more work.** `[swrNwD0ypaE @ 00:31:57]` — [L3](../lectures/03-swrNwD0ypaE-digest.md)
- **Forty years separated the BMS algebra from the BMS charges (Wald–Zoupas)** — "they're not the same thing, it's very hard to go from one to the other". `[7S3Eg4VlZf0 @ 00:42:39]` — [extra-03](../lectures/extra-03-7S3Eg4VlZf0-digest.md)
- **The community actively tried to *kill* BMS symmetry with a boundary condition at spatial infinity, and all those attempts had problems.** He includes his own discomfort in the account. `[6kH2pl_zvMA @ 00:17:52]` (continues `00:18:53`) — [cstq](../lectures/cstq-6kH2pl_zvMA-digest.md)
- **Retrospective verdict in six words: "the BMS boundary conditions were both too strong and too weak".** `[6kH2pl_zvMA @ 00:34:37]` — [cstq](../lectures/cstq-6kH2pl_zvMA-digest.md)
- **A group that large ought to have constrained every scattering process in Minkowski space, "yet we've heard very little about it"** — the anomaly in the literature that motivated him. `[7S3Eg4VlZf0 @ 00:02:13]` — [extra-03](../lectures/extra-03-7S3Eg4VlZf0-digest.md)
- **The van der Burg joke, and the point behind it: "you should never have a last name without a clear initial, otherwise you will go 50 years and you will be completely forgotten".** `[7S3Eg4VlZf0 @ 00:00:09]` — [extra-03](../lectures/extra-03-7S3Eg4VlZf0-digest.md)

## Where the field was wrong

- **The 1960s–1980s literature on the large-`r` expansion was substantially wrong, and that wrongness is why the present story looked impossible before Christodoulou–Klainerman.** `[lvyyxkjwSCg @ 01:22:45]` — [L1](../lectures/01-lvyyxkjwSCg-digest.md)
- **"The whole school of relativity, starting from the 60s up until Christodoulou and Klainerman in the 90s, had it wrong in a very important way"** — with the methodological moral drawn himself, and then hedged: maybe they finally solved it. `[OXl7IXT7_Cw @ 01:11:14]`, `[OXl7IXT7_Cw @ 01:12:15]` — [L6](../lectures/06-OXl7IXT7_Cw-digest.md)
- **"Hundreds of papers … almost all of which were completely wrong"** on the behaviour at the edges of scri. `[Yx7PWgfC1dw @ 00:12:04]`, `[Yx7PWgfC1dw @ 00:13:08]` — [L7](../lectures/07-Yx7PWgfC1dw-digest.md)
- **Penrose's asymptotic simplicity was too strong — it roughly forces all the BMS charges to vanish — and the belief that going beyond it was merely technical was "completely wrong".** `[Yx7PWgfC1dw @ 01:26:13]`, `[Yx7PWgfC1dw @ 01:27:20]` — [L7](../lectures/07-Yx7PWgfC1dw-digest.md)
- **The peeling theorems in every general relativity textbook fail for two scattering protons.** The example is deliberately mundane — not black holes, protons. `[Yx7PWgfC1dw @ 01:28:23]` — [L7](../lectures/07-Yx7PWgfC1dw-digest.md)
- **The email anecdote: around 1992–93 he wrote to Christodoulou pointing out the contradiction and was told the answer was in an unpublished Cetraro lecture note, after Lemma 46** — "he sent me a copy but the world didn't learn about it … he's a character". Oral history with no written counterpart. `[Yx7PWgfC1dw @ 01:32:43]` — [L7](../lectures/07-Yx7PWgfC1dw-digest.md)
- **Christodoulou–Klainerman's contribution, in his telling, is the explicit falloffs rather than the global existence theorem** — the `|u|^{-3/2}` news kills the logarithms and makes `C` well defined at the edges. `[Yx7PWgfC1dw @ 01:30:33]` — [L7](../lectures/07-Yx7PWgfC1dw-digest.md)
- **Penrose's programme tried to put all of physics at null infinity and got stuck on massive fields — "nothing useful was ever really found within that program".** `[xZ2FG1kLb9I @ 00:26:01]` — [L4](../lectures/04-xZ2FG1kLb9I-digest.md)
- **Sociology, told without malice: mathematical relativists "shudder" at Bondi quantities and will only discuss coordinate-invariant objects** — the point being that invariants alone cannot solve the scattering problem. `[Yx7PWgfC1dw @ 01:19:48]` — [L7](../lectures/07-Yx7PWgfC1dw-digest.md)
- **…and the stronger claim that follows: the scattering problem "was never even posed, it was never asked in a way that made any sense".** `[Yx7PWgfC1dw @ 01:21:56]`, `[Yx7PWgfC1dw @ 01:23:00]` — [L7](../lectures/07-Yx7PWgfC1dw-digest.md)
- **Rigour is better in gravity than in electromagnetism or Yang–Mills — the reverse of what one might expect.** `[lvyyxkjwSCg @ 01:24:51]` — [L1](../lectures/01-lvyyxkjwSCg-digest.md)

## Why the gauge-theory side took a century
