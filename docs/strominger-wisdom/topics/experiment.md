## What a detector has to be

- **The detector is defined by a protocol, not by a formula:** two bodies carrying clocks *synchronise*, the wave passes, they re-measure by exchanging light signals and compare. The calibration is inside the definition of the measurement. `[8Aml1ToQtt4 @ 00:05:20]` — [L9 (guest)](../lectures/09-8Aml1ToQtt4-digest.md)
- **Without the prior agreement, the observable does not exist — "the word calibrated is extremely important here".** `[8Aml1ToQtt4 @ 01:50:18]` — [L9 (guest)](../lectures/09-8Aml1ToQtt4-digest.md)
- **The "BMS detector" is an admitted fiction:** its elements sit at fixed `(z,z̄)` in a chosen Bondi frame, which requires acceleration — a little rocket on each one — and he does not know how to build it. `[8Aml1ToQtt4 @ 00:46:04]` — [L9 (guest)](../lectures/09-8Aml1ToQtt4-digest.md)
- **Displacement memory is frame-robust; clock desynchronisation is not.** Accelerated and freely falling observers agree on the displacement; only the accelerated ones see clocks drift. `[8Aml1ToQtt4 @ 01:11:34]` — [L9 (guest)](../lectures/09-8Aml1ToQtt4-digest.md)
- **The signal has memory; the instrument does not.** `[8Aml1ToQtt4 @ 00:33:18]` — [L9 (guest)](../lectures/09-8Aml1ToQtt4-digest.md)
- **Strominger locates the content elsewhere — in the phase space, not the apparatus: "no, you don't care about experimental device … the flat connections on the sphere at infinity are physical".** `[F42XbX45Dzw @ 01:02:06]` — [extra-05](../lectures/extra-05-F42XbX45Dzw-digest.md). **TENSION** with the two entries above; see [campaign-hooks.md](../campaign-hooks.md#h1).
- **The measuring-device criterion for physical inequivalence:** two supertranslated graviton configurations, one departing fifty years early and the other a hundred years late, are diffeomorphic and distinguishable by an instrument. `[6kH2pl_zvMA @ 00:16:49]`, `[6kH2pl_zvMA @ 00:17:52]` — [cstq](../lectures/cstq-6kH2pl_zvMA-digest.md)
- **Detector limitations are not an argument against wanting an S-matrix.** `[WxCY-K-Jb4A @ 01:27:18]` — [L5](../lectures/05-WxCY-K-Jb4A-digest.md)

## Gravitational memory

- **Why LIGO is blind to it, mechanistically:** suspended mirrors give a ~100 Hz sensitivity band and memory is a zero-frequency effect, so the published waveforms already miss it by construction of the instrument. `[8Aml1ToQtt4 @ 00:12:45]` — [L9 (guest)](../lectures/09-8Aml1ToQtt4-digest.md)
- **Strominger's version: memory is "a DC effect … *if they are inertial detectors*", distinct from the oscillating signal LIGO reported.** `[wg-4Rm-kk70 @ 00:03:18]` — [extra-02](../lectures/extra-02-wg-4Rm-kk70-digest.md)
- **Free-flying detectors are the natural home for a DC observable — a space-based constellation such as LISA.** `[8Aml1ToQtt4 @ 00:13:48]` — [L9 (guest)](../lectures/09-8Aml1ToQtt4-digest.md)
- **He expects memory to be measured, probably by pulsar timing arrays, because the `1/R` suppression is survivable at those fluxes.** `[CdFyoaNv8GI @ 00:30:22]` — [extra-01](../lectures/extra-01-CdFyoaNv8GI-digest.md)
- **Late-period status, with named programmes:** LIGO, pulsar timing arrays and others are trying to verify it, and it is worth doing because it is "a qualitative prediction of the Einstein equations that has not been experimentally verified". `[6kH2pl_zvMA @ 00:21:00]`, `[6kH2pl_zvMA @ 00:30:30]` — [cstq](../lectures/cstq-6kH2pl_zvMA-digest.md)
- **Gravitational wave energy at null infinity is unambiguous, and LIGO is the proof — it moved the mirrors.** `[Yx7PWgfC1dw @ 00:54:26]` — [L7](../lectures/07-Yx7PWgfC1dw-digest.md)
- **The horse in the desert:** near-field motion produces no memory; the effect is dominated by enormous fluxes at large angles. `[8Aml1ToQtt4 @ 01:01:07]` — [L9 (guest)](../lectures/09-8Aml1ToQtt4-digest.md)

## Electromagnetic memory

- **An operational protocol:** align two charges' phases, separate them, let the large gauge transformation act, bring them back and interfere. His favourite version is a short Susskind note using an array of SQUIDs connected by resistors. `[F42XbX45Dzw @ 01:01:02]` — [extra-05](../lectures/extra-05-F42XbX45Dzw-digest.md)
- **It is *harder* than the gravitational case, because a phase is a quantum measurement.** `[8Aml1ToQtt4 @ 01:50:18]` — [L9 (guest)](../lectures/09-8Aml1ToQtt4-digest.md)
- **…and that asymmetry is his explanation for why the gravitational case was found first.** `[F42XbX45Dzw @ 01:01:02]` — [extra-05](../lectures/extra-05-F42XbX45Dzw-digest.md)
- **In gravity the hair is measurable classically, because supertranslations change the superrotation charge.** `[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 01:32:28]` — [L10](../lectures/10-H4IZ3jwTDp4-digest.md)

## Colliders and cutoffs

- **Soft theorems do constrain LHC amplitudes — "you can't do an LHC calculation without using the soft theorem"** — but every actual measurement has an infrared cutoff, which is part of why the deep infrared went unstudied. `[wg-4Rm-kk70 @ 00:25:59]` — [extra-02](../lectures/extra-02-wg-4Rm-kk70-digest.md)
- **Inclusive cross sections are fine for the LHC but have no symmetries — "even energy conservation is not a symmetry of those things".** `[OXl7IXT7_Cw @ 00:56:04]` — [L6](../lectures/06-OXl7IXT7_Cw-digest.md)
- **How small must the cutoff be to be sure no information escaped?** Treated as genuinely open and quantifiable; his guess is below the inverse of the longest time scale in the problem. `[wg-4Rm-kk70 @ 00:28:06]` — [extra-02](../lectures/extra-02-wg-4Rm-kk70-digest.md)
- **Memory is what makes a soft graviton measurable, and the unmeasurability had bothered him** — "people are building detectors". `[CdFyoaNv8GI @ 00:34:36]` — [extra-01](../lectures/extra-01-CdFyoaNv8GI-digest.md)
