## Why the vacua are degenerate, and why moving between them is cheap

- **"Untethered": the angles at null infinity are not tied to one another, which makes the problem behave like 1+1 dimensions and makes vacuum change cost no energy.** A global Mexican-hat symmetry has to be moved everywhere at once; a large gauge parameter can have compact support in one patch of the sphere. `[WxCY-K-Jb4A @ 00:52:52]`, `[WxCY-K-Jb4A @ 00:41:44]` — [L5](../lectures/05-WxCY-K-Jb4A-digest.md)
- **The causal version of the same point: null infinity is ultralocal, so nothing may tie the sphere points together.** `[CdFyoaNv8GI @ 00:37:46]` — [extra-01](../lectures/extra-01-CdFyoaNv8GI-digest.md)
- **…and that is exactly how this differs from ordinary spontaneous symmetry breaking:** global Goldstone vacua are not allowed to vary with angle, so the structure is rigid, whereas here the angles are liberated from one another and you can move around the moduli space at finite energy. `[CdFyoaNv8GI @ 01:17:17]` — [extra-01](../lectures/extra-01-CdFyoaNv8GI-digest.md)
- **Superselection sectors are the hinge: with them there are no useful Ward identities, without them there are** — and here there are none, because it costs a finite amount of energy to move between vacua. `[WxCY-K-Jb4A @ 00:42:47]`, `[WxCY-K-Jb4A @ 00:43:50]` — [L5](../lectures/05-WxCY-K-Jb4A-digest.md)
- **The degenerate photon vacua have the same energy but different angular momentum** — another way to see that they are genuinely different states. `[WxCY-K-Jb4A @ 01:29:28]` — [L5](../lectures/05-WxCY-K-Jb4A-digest.md)
- **A horizon soft photon costs no energy and commutes with the Hamiltonian, but shifts angular momentum by one unit, so the resulting state is orthogonal.** `[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 00:39:12]` — [L10](../lectures/10-H4IZ3jwTDp4-digest.md)
- **The vacuum as a storage medium — "like a big hard drive"** — with the claim downgraded from "perfectly possible" to "not ruled out" inside the same sentence. `[F42XbX45Dzw @ 00:44:58]` — [extra-05](../lectures/extra-05-F42XbX45Dzw-digest.md)
- **General relativity does not reduce to special relativity because special relativity has no infinite vacuum degeneracy to match the infinite zero-energy structure at long distances.** `[F42XbX45Dzw @ 01:05:22]` — [extra-05](../lectures/extra-05-F42XbX45Dzw-digest.md)

## Labelling which vacuum you are in

- **Supertranslation charges cannot label the degenerate vacua** — the group is abelian, so all the supertranslation vacua carry zero supertranslation charge; what distinguishes them is `ΔC` and the angular momentum. Anyone building a "which vacuum am I in?" observable should note this first. `[CdFyoaNv8GI @ 01:12:01]` — [extra-01](../lectures/extra-01-CdFyoaNv8GI-digest.md)
- **The extra integration function `C|_{scri⁺₋}` is "the point that everybody would have missed"** — the free data are not just the radiative field, and the missing datum is exactly where the vacuum label lives. `[Yx7PWgfC1dw @ 01:08:32]`, context from `[Yx7PWgfC1dw @ 01:07:29]` — [L7](../lectures/07-Yx7PWgfC1dw-digest.md)
- **Fixing the vacuum frame is a one-time choice:** gauge-fix `C=0` on the initial slice and the Einstein equations determine the supertranslation frame at every later time. `[8Aml1ToQtt4 @ 00:32:13]` — [L9 (guest)](../lectures/09-8Aml1ToQtt4-digest.md)
- **Radiation through null infinity is a domain wall separating two BMS-inequivalent vacua**, flat on either side. `[CdFyoaNv8GI @ 01:01:17]` — [extra-01](../lectures/extra-01-CdFyoaNv8GI-digest.md)
- **"Hair" means only a distinguishing feature** — a label that separates otherwise identical states, not structure sitting near a horizon. `[wg-4Rm-kk70 @ 00:17:15]` — [extra-02](../lectures/extra-02-wg-4Rm-kk70-digest.md)
- **A student's tunnelling analogy corrected: these are transitions in *space* along the slice on which the Hilbert space is defined, not transitions in time.** `[WxCY-K-Jb4A @ 00:46:14]` — [L5](../lectures/05-WxCY-K-Jb4A-digest.md)

## The Goldstone dictionary — three positions, in tension

- **Strominger's pedantry with a point: strictly it is the *zero mode* that transforms inhomogeneously, and calling the whole field the Goldstone boson "caused a lot of confusion".** `[WxCY-K-Jb4A @ 00:28:53]`, `[WxCY-K-Jb4A @ 00:35:26]` — [L5](../lectures/05-WxCY-K-Jb4A-digest.md)
- **…and the word "soft" in "soft photons are the Goldstone bosons" is a communication choice, kept because listeners follow better — "it's a free world".** `[WxCY-K-Jb4A @ 00:34:24]`, `[WxCY-K-Jb4A @ 00:35:26]` — [L5](../lectures/05-WxCY-K-Jb4A-digest.md)
- **Strominger hedging on semantic grounds:** asked whether a soft photon really is a Goldstone boson, he calls it a question of semantics, restates that only the zero mode transforms, and says "I don't know if it's a good terminology or not". `[F42XbX45Dzw @ 01:10:31]` — [extra-05](../lectures/extra-05-F42XbX45Dzw-digest.md)
- **The guest lecturer's open dissent: "this dictionary I think is not completely well defined … probably any statement that can be made can be made without saying the word Goldstone boson".** `[8Aml1ToQtt4 @ 01:43:55]` (continues `01:44:56`) — [L9 (guest)](../lectures/09-8Aml1ToQtt4-digest.md)
- **…with a named reason: there is no counting theorem for spacetime symmetries.** For internal symmetries the number of Goldstone bosons is fixed by the number of broken generators; for a crystal the phonons are outnumbered by the broken generators, and no analogue is known here. `[8Aml1ToQtt4 @ 01:44:56]` (continues `01:45:58`) — [L9 (guest)](../lectures/09-8Aml1ToQtt4-digest.md)
- **…and an honest closing verdict on it: "it's all very confusing … I find the discussion to be quite preliminary at the moment".** `[8Aml1ToQtt4 @ 01:45:58]` — [L9 (guest)](../lectures/09-8Aml1ToQtt4-digest.md)
- **TENSION, recorded rather than resolved.** The written notes have a subsection built on the Goldstone/SSB language and use it throughout; the CSTQ colloquium presents it as a vocabulary equivalence ("the words in particle physics is that supertranslation symmetry is spontaneously broken", `[6kH2pl_zvMA @ 00:27:17]`); lecture 5 uses it while correcting its imprecision; lecture 9's guest lecturer declines to endorse it at all. Three different confidence levels across two speakers and one written source.
