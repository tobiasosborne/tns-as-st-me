## What is assumed

- **Unitarity is assumed, not derived, and he says so unprompted:** "symmetries imply conservation laws only if you have unitarity, so I'm not proving here that information is [preserved] — I'm assuming unitarity, I'm not trying to prove unitarity". `[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 00:29:40]` — [L10](../lectures/10-H4IZ3jwTDp4-digest.md)
- **…with the research strategy that follows from it:** nobody can prove nothing crazy happens in the middle of the spacetime, so "the way to proceed is to make some reasonable plausible set of assumptions and try to build a consistent theory". `[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 00:30:46]` — [L10](../lectures/10-H4IZ3jwTDp4-digest.md)
- **The matching conditions are the other assumption, and without them there is no theory.** `[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 00:28:37]` — [L10](../lectures/10-H4IZ3jwTDp4-digest.md)
- **An `ħ`-counting aside licenses the clean split between classical formation and quantum evaporation:** the waiting time for the first Hawking quantum is `~M/ħ`, while formation, transients and ringdown have no `ħ` in them. `[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 00:22:19]` — [L10](../lectures/10-H4IZ3jwTDp4-digest.md)
- **A modelling choice made on taste, said out loud:** he could have used eternal black holes acted on by large gauge transformations, but "somehow I don't like having white holes". `[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 01:15:09]` — [L10](../lectures/10-H4IZ3jwTDp4-digest.md)
- **He would bet on singularity resolution:** if unitarity is preserved, that is "tantamount to saying that there isn't a singularity". `[wg-4Rm-kk70 @ 00:29:19]` — [extra-02](../lectures/extra-02-wg-4Rm-kk70-digest.md)

## What is at stake

- **The determinism riff:** if Hawking is right the claim is not that a prediction fails but that there are no laws — "we thought our job was to discover what the laws of nature are, but it's saying that there aren't laws". `[F42XbX45Dzw @ 00:40:46]` — [extra-05](../lectures/extra-05-F42XbX45Dzw-digest.md)
- **…with the alternative left genuinely open:** indeterminism should stay on the table, the beginning of the universe is a reason it might be true, and the case rests on nobody having built a replacement. `[F42XbX45Dzw @ 00:41:48]` — [extra-05](../lectures/extra-05-F42XbX45Dzw-digest.md)
- **A head count, offered as his own impression:** of the hundreds or thousands who have thought about the paradox, he believes only two now admit to accepting indeterminism — and Hawking himself changed his mind. `[F42XbX45Dzw @ 00:42:50]` — [extra-05](../lectures/extra-05-F42XbX45Dzw-digest.md)
- **The no-hair theorem is a correct mathematical theorem whose physical interpretation was wrong** — the diffeomorphisms it quotients by include ones that change the physical state. `[F42XbX45Dzw @ 00:36:29]` — [extra-05](../lectures/extra-05-F42XbX45Dzw-digest.md)
- **The correction is aimed at a textbook fact and he says which one:** "it's not true what you've been hearing since you were babies that there are only a three parameter family of black holes". `[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 00:41:29]` — [L10](../lectures/10-H4IZ3jwTDp4-digest.md)
- **Previous "quantum hair" was always a small finite number of labels; the infinity of classical and quantum hair is what is claimed to be new.** `[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 01:34:32]` — [L10](../lectures/10-H4IZ3jwTDp4-digest.md)

## Where the argument gets to, and where it stops

- **"We have not solved the information paradox. I can't say this often enough."** Said unprompted, and phrased as something he has to keep repeating. The claim he does make is narrower: a flaw in the argument for information loss, plus concrete next steps. `[wg-4Rm-kk70 @ 00:23:42]` — [extra-02](../lectures/extra-02-wg-4Rm-kk70-digest.md)
- **The personal bet, hedged in both directions:** "I doubt that everything I've taught you in this course is qualitatively enough to solve the information problem … I think we've turned the problem upside down and seen that the old arguments are wrong … I don't see an obstacle to solving the information problem through this, but neither do I think it's around the next corner." `[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 01:41:26]` — [L10](../lectures/10-H4IZ3jwTDp4-digest.md)
- **Soft hair is unlikely to be the whole story**, said before any question forced it. `[F42XbX45Dzw @ 00:58:42]` — [extra-05](../lectures/extra-05-F42XbX45Dzw-digest.md)
- **The concession underneath the whole picture: it is not obvious the Hilbert space factorises** — "if you're asking me, Steve, how do I get from here to a complete solution of the information paradox, the answer is I don't know". `[F42XbX45Dzw @ 01:07:26]` — [extra-05](../lectures/extra-05-F42XbX45Dzw-digest.md)
- **The quantum-computer challenge, and the concession it produces:** asked why gravity's infrared is special when entanglement transfer is familiar elsewhere, he ends up conceding that the difference is not that the conservation laws fail elsewhere — it is that elsewhere there is nothing to solve. `[F42XbX45Dzw @ 01:07:26]`, `[F42XbX45Dzw @ 01:08:28]` — [extra-05](../lectures/extra-05-F42XbX45Dzw-digest.md)
- **A retrospective judgment on Page's picture:** on his reading each of Page's states individually violates a conservation law, so the correlations restoring purity cannot be the ones Page proposed — his alternative puts them between hard and soft rather than early and late. `[GpN8VAauyj8 @ 00:24:41]` — [extra-04](../lectures/extra-04-GpN8VAauyj8-digest.md)
- **…and the honest hole in it, named without prompting:** two exactly collinear gravitons of half the energy carry the same supertranslation charge; he rescues it with a measure-zero argument and with superrotation charge, which sees the spin. `[GpN8VAauyj8 @ 00:26:53]` — [extra-04](../lectures/extra-04-GpN8VAauyj8-digest.md)
- **What neither he nor Page can compute:** "I haven't solved the information problem … neither did Page give any proposal for how you compute the `c_α`s". `[GpN8VAauyj8 @ 00:27:59]` — [extra-04](../lectures/extra-04-GpN8VAauyj8-digest.md)
- **Does soft hair contribute to black hole entropy? "I can't answer."** Work in progress, with the distinction that the soft modes contribute differently to black hole entropy than to vacuum entropy. `[CdFyoaNv8GI @ 01:04:30]` — [extra-01](../lectures/extra-01-CdFyoaNv8GI-digest.md)
- **Where the hair actually *is* is not clear to him** — "it's a little confusing where this stuff is; it certainly is long wavelength". `[CdFyoaNv8GI @ 01:07:45]` — [extra-01](../lectures/extra-01-CdFyoaNv8GI-digest.md)

## AdS is the wrong laboratory

- **The sharp technical reason:** there is no infinite degeneracy at fixed energy in AdS₃, because acting with Virasoro *changes* the energy — so the degenerate-states observation simply does not apply. `[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 01:39:19]` — [L10](../lectures/10-H4IZ3jwTDp4-digest.md)
- **…and the accompanying judgment on twenty years of effort:** "there is no sharp information problem in AdS because black holes don't evaporate … I think it's time for a new approach, and certainly flat space is the most natural home for the information problem". `[H4IZ3jwTDp4 @ 01:38:15]` — [L10](../lectures/10-H4IZ3jwTDp4-digest.md)
- **The methodological version:** "if you were to study quantum gravity by putting it in a box and taking that box out to infinity you would miss all of this". `[CdFyoaNv8GI @ 01:07:45]` — [extra-01](../lectures/extra-01-CdFyoaNv8GI-digest.md)
- **AdS seems not to have a version of the triangle "because it's gapped".** `[CdFyoaNv8GI @ 00:04:31]` — [extra-01](../lectures/extra-01-CdFyoaNv8GI-digest.md)
- **The holographic principle, he says in passing, begins with the Bekenstein–Hawking area entropy formula.** `[6kH2pl_zvMA @ 00:59:13]` — [cstq](../lectures/cstq-6kH2pl_zvMA-digest.md)
- **A datum about shifted emphasis:** soft hair and the information paradox — central to the written notes — are nearly absent from the late CSTQ overview, where black holes appear only via the Bekenstein–Hawking remark. — [cstq](../lectures/cstq-6kH2pl_zvMA-digest.md)
