Stop 01  ·  why

Why a scattering law, a symmetry, and a late detector record know about one another

In the standard asymptotic formulations of QED and gravity, a leading soft theorem, its Ward identity, and a memory observable are related under explicit boundary and regularity assumptions. Three lines of blackboard fix what that means before this route tries to use any of it somewhere else.

One — the leading soft factor (literature)
\[ \langle{\rm out}|\,a^{\rm out}_{+}(\vec q\,)\,{\cal S}\,|{\rm in}\rangle \;=\; e\left[\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{Q^{\rm out}_{k}\,p^{\rm out}_{k}\!\cdot\varepsilon^{+}} {p^{\rm out}_{k}\!\cdot q} -\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{Q^{\rm in}_{k}\,p^{\rm in}_{k}\!\cdot\varepsilon^{+}} {p^{\rm in}_{k}\!\cdot q}\right] \langle{\rm out}|\,{\cal S}\,|{\rm in}\rangle \;+\;{\cal O}(q^{0}) \]
Weinberg’s leading soft photon theorem, copied from refs/arxiv-1703.05448/soft_Arxiv_update.tex:1075-1082. The \(Q_k\) are the charges of the hard particles, outgoing entering with the opposite sign to incoming; the statement is the leading term of a Laurent expansion as the photon momentum \(q^{\mu}\to0\), with corrections of order \(q^{0}\). Not a result of this campaign.
Two — its symmetry reading (literature)
“The standard soft photon formula reduces to the Ward identity following from invariance under large gauge symmetries, or equivalently, the matrix element of the charge conservation law.”

Three — the hinge this route actually uses
\[ \tilde\varepsilon_{\rm sub}(\omega)=\frac{i{\cal A}_*}{\omega+i0}+{\cal O}(1) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \lim_{u\to\infty}\varepsilon_{\rm sub}(u)\;=\;{\cal A}_*\;=\; \lim_{\omega\to0}\bigl[-i\omega\,\tilde\varepsilon_{\rm sub}(\omega)\bigr] \]
Stated for the synchronized retarded response derived at stop 04, with the transform convention \(\tilde f(\omega)=\int du\,e^{i\omega u}f(u)\), the retarded \(\omega+i0\) prescription, and no additional nondecaying physical homogeneous components — the endpoint conditions of the reconstruction select the physical member of the growing homogeneous sector. Those hypotheses are the content: a simple pole at zero frequency does not by itself make a response settle, since other real-axis poles or nondecaying pieces can prevent the late limit from existing at all. This route uses the identity exactly once, at stop 04, in the case just stated.

Each corner of that triangle, as usually stated, needs massless asymptotic particles, a spacetime with a boundary at infinity, and detectors whose rulers do not change while the measurement is being made. The question here is narrower, and can be settled by computation rather than by analogy:

Does the soft/DC-to-late-response edge survive when the ruler is a thermal correlator — when the detector’s own reference clock is part of the thermal state, and therefore erodes while you watch?

The system this route uses is two SYK copies, or dots, prepared in a thermofield double state, with a compact source acting on the right copy. No coupling term between the dots is introduced anywhere on this route. Their low-energy behaviour is governed by a single soft mode — the Schwarzian reparametrization of boundary time. The next three stops build the apparatus: what may be measured, what may be compared, and what the soft mode actually does. Stop 05 reports what was found.

Stop 02  ·  how

What counts as a detector

Before asking whether the system remembers anything, we have to say exactly which measurements are allowed. Without that, the question has no content — and the campaign learned this the hard way.

“this question cannot be correctly answered unless you specify the physically allowed observables clearly. until then you will get nonsense.”
the directive that produced theory/lanes/syk-jt/observables-spec.md

The specification has three layers. At the bottom sits the thing that is actually operational: for two dots of N Majorana fermions each, the algebra of allowed observables is the fermion-parity-even part of the CAR algebra, a finite-dimensional matrix algebra. Parity superselection is in force, so a single Majorana operator is not an observable at all. Detectors are POVMs whose effects live in that even algebra.

The middle layer is what this calculation actually uses: uniformly normalized smeared bilocals, together with the two boundary Hamiltonians. In particular the detector below, and the two exactly conserved energies. The top layer — the emergent von Neumann algebra of the strict large-N limit — is the correct mathematical home for some of these questions, but nothing from it is load-bearing here.

The detector
\[ G_{LR}(u_L,u_R)\;=\;{\cal N}_\Delta\left[\frac{t'_L(u_L)\,t'_R(u_R)}{\bigl(t_R(u_R)-t_L(u_L)\bigr)^{2}}\right]^{\Delta} \]
One two-sided correlator of a matter primary of dimension \(\Delta>0\), read at equal boundary times. \(t_L\) and \(t_R\) are the two boundary clocks; the normalization \({\cal N}_\Delta\) cancels from every comparison made on this route. Equation (0.4) of the proof shard; kernel derived at GravityReparametrizationsRevised.tex:645-675.

What is not a detector matters just as much. The reparametrization field itself, any representative of it, and any relative frame label are scaffolding. So are the Schwarzian symmetry charges beyond the energy: they are bookkeeping in the effective theory, and no measurement protocol for them has been exhibited. Any conclusion phrased in frame language that does not reduce to the allowed algebra is treated in this campaign as a fatal error, not a stylistic one.

Stop 03  ·  how

What counts as a fair comparison

A detector reading only means something against a reference run. The reference is admissible only if it agrees with the pulsed run on every quantity that is exactly conserved — otherwise the pair is not comparable in the sense of D32, so its difference cannot support the memory verdict at issue.

This is not a stylistic preference; it is written into the definition the campaign uses for the word memory. Two protocols are comparable when they agree on every exactly conserved allowed charge of the specification, and D32 reserves the word memory for a comparable pair. A difference between protocols that are not comparable may still be perfectly measurable; it simply cannot license memory in that register.

Here that bites immediately. A source acting on the right boundary changes the right energy. The comparator originally used in this lane put both clocks at the new temperature — which silently changed the left energy too, even though nothing acted on the left. No source on the right can do that. So that comparison was not between two preparable states with the same conserved charges, and every detector conclusion drawn from it was retracted.

The admissible sector
\[ \Delta E=\int_{u_i}^{u_f}P(u)\,du,\qquad \kappa_f=\sqrt{\kappa_i^{2}+\frac{2\Delta E}{C}},\qquad \text{retained:}\;\;\Delta E=0 \]
\(P\) is the boundary power delivered by the source and \(C\) is the Schwarzian stiffness. Setting \(\Delta E=0\) — an energy-balanced pulse, which pushes and then pulls — leaves both energies where they were, so the pulsed run and the untouched equilibrium run are comparable. Equation (1.1) of the proof shard.

The balanced sector is the sector for which this shard constructs a same-charge reference protocol. A general balanced pulse is only required to return the net energy to its initial value; the explicit family used in the analytic proof raises the temperature, holds it, and lowers it back. That explicit family still produces a nonzero leading calibrated susceptibility: the two clocks come out of the history offset relative to one another even though every conserved charge returns to its old value.

What survives is therefore narrower than what was first written down, and the narrowing has a name and a date. In the numerical campaign, six of eight computed parameter points use unbalanced pulses; after review, all six were demoted to formal diagnostics of the Schwarzian kernels, and only the two energy-balanced rows carry operational conclusions. Stop 05 shows only those two.

Stop 04  ·  how

The soft clock mode and its zero-frequency weight

At leading order the entire late response is carried by one soft mode — a slow wobble of the boundary clock — and its zero-frequency weight is fixed by when the energy went in, not by how the pulse was shaped.

Write the right boundary clock as a thermal clock with a small correction, keep the source power fixed and let the stiffness C grow. The equation of motion linearizes to a fourth-order retarded equation for that correction, and its Fourier transform has a double pole at zero frequency. For an energy-balanced pulse the source transform starts at first order in \(\omega\), so one power of the pole is cancelled and what is left is a simple pole — exactly the situation stop 01 described. Its residue is the pulse’s first moment: the dwell time between pushing and pulling, weighted by power.

The result this route is built around
\[ \Delta u_{\rm rel}^{(1)} \;=\;\frac{1}{2C\kappa^{2}}\int_{u_i}^{u_f}(u-u_*)\,P(u)\,du \;=\;\lim_{\omega\to0}\,{\cal S}_{\rm Sch}(\omega;P_{\rm sub}) \]
Left: the late reading of the calibrated relative-clock shift. Right: the zero-frequency residue of the same invariant soft attachment, normalized with no fitted coefficient. Equations (4.9) and (4.13) of the proof shard; the normalizing factor \(1/(2\kappa)\) is \(\Delta/[-\partial_T\log G_{\rm eq}]\) at late time, and the answer is independent of the detector dimension \(\Delta\), which cancels between the vertex and the detector Jacobian.

That is one sentence with two halves that were computed in different ways: a late-time reading of a detector, and a zero-frequency property of a soft mode. This establishes the soft/DC-to-late-response edge at leading \(O(C^{-1})\) in the balanced Schwarzian scope. The 31-leaf object further down is a faithful compression of the argument; the linked shard is the full proof.

Does the record persist?

The absolute detector difference decays to zero for every operator dimension. The calibrated shift does not. Those are two different questions about the same experiment, and in this system they have different answers — which is the whole result.

The two readouts, as defined
\[ \Delta u^{\rm lin}_{\rm rel}(T)=\frac{R_g(T)-1}{2\Delta\kappa\tanh\bigl(\kappa(T-u_*)\bigr)} \;=\;-\frac{\delta G_{LR}}{\partial_T G^{\rm eq}_{LR}} \qquad\bigl(|R_g-1|\ll1\bigr) \] \[ G^{\rm pulse}_{LR}(T,T)=G^{\rm eq}_{LR}\bigl(T-\Delta u^{\rm NL}_{\rm rel},\, T-\Delta u^{\rm NL}_{\rm rel}\bigr) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Delta u^{\rm NL}_{\rm rel}(T)=(T-u_*)-\frac{1}{\kappa}\operatorname{arcosh}\frac{D_g(T)}{2} \qquad(D_g\ge2) \]
