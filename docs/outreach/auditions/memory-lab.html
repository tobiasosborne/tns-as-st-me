The Memory Lab

Three things that are often called the same thing — a permanent record, a calibrated susceptibility, and a scattering time delay — come apart in a controlled Schwarzian gravitational-clock calculation: a numerically integrated pulse fixes a fitted post-pulse frame, and proved closed forms determine its late detector response. You can operate the bench, and every curve on this page says where its numbers come from.

Why — the question

After the wave has passed, what would have to remain different for you to say that the apparatus remembers it?

The honest answer is that the question is not yet well posed. “Something is different afterwards” is not a measurement until you say which quantity you read, against what you read it, and when. Whether the ring above kept a record depends on whether the rulers you compare it against also moved.

So before any gravity appears, it is worth spending five minutes on a bench where every answer can be written down in closed form.

The bench — an exactly solvable toy

A detector, a ruler, and one pulse

One degree of freedom relaxing toward equilibrium at rate γ. You run it twice: once undisturbed, once with a compact pulse. Then you ask what is different afterwards.

\[ \dot x(t) \;=\; -\gamma\,\bigl[x(t)-x_{\rm eq}\bigr] \;+\; f(t), \qquad f(t)=F \ \text{ for } 0\le t\le w, \ \ 0 \text{ otherwise.} \]

Both runs start at x(0)=xeq+x0; the reference run has f≡0. The bench below fixes the numerical convention xeq = 0 and x0 = 1 and evaluates every formula at those values. Integrating the linear equation once gives, for every t ≥ w,

\[ x_{\rm ref}(t)=x_{\rm eq}+x_0e^{-\gamma t},\qquad x_{\rm pulse}(t)=x_{\rm eq}+(x_0+K)\,e^{-\gamma t}, \]
\[ K=\int_0^{w}\! e^{\gamma s}f(s)\,ds=F\,\frac{e^{\gamma w}-1}{\gamma} \qquad\bigl(\to Fw \text{ as } \gamma\to0\bigr). \]

Two registers can be built from this, and they are not the same quantity:

\[ \underbrace{A(t)=x_{\rm pulse}(t)-x_{\rm ref}(t)=K\,e^{-\gamma t}}_{\text{absolute}} \qquad\qquad \underbrace{R=\frac{x_{\rm pulse}(t)-x_{\rm eq}}{x_{\rm ref}(t)-x_{\rm eq}}-1=\frac{K}{x_0}}_{\text{calibrated against the same ruler}} \]

A(t) decays whenever the ruler decays. R is exactly independent of t. At γ=0 the two read the same number only because x0=1 has been declared: in general A = K and R = K/x0. Move the ruler’s decay rate below and watch which one survives.

Absolute register A(t) — Difference of the two runs in the fixed observable x.
Calibrated register R — Ratio of the same two runs to the same decaying ruler, minus one. Independent of t.
Shots for 10% error on A(t) — Assume i.i.d. additive readout noise of fixed variance σ² on x, so the standard error of a mean of N samples is σ/√N. Then N ≥ (σ/ηK)² e2γt with σ=1, η=0.1. That noise model is assumed here, not derived, and this is the cost of A(t) alone: R is a different estimator whose variance is not modelled on this bench.

What the bench shows. With a persistent ruler the two registers read the same number: a pulse leaves an offset you can read directly, and calibrating changes nothing. Turn on the ruler’s own decay and they part company. The fixed difference decays with the ruler, while the calibrated ratio remains nonzero. No memory label follows from this toy: it has no soft-theorem or DC datum of any kind, so it establishes neither D33 nor any Schwarzian conclusion. What it does show is that the readout cost of A(t) grows exponentially in the read time, under the noise model declared above.

Two registers, then, with different names and different accounting. Which name attaches to which, and what has to be declared before either question means anything, is the subject of the next section.

What this bench is not

A classical, linear, one-degree-of-freedom relaxation model, solved in closed form above. It is here to make one question precise: which register you were reading. It is not quantum, it is not a black hole, and it establishes nothing whatever about the system in the next two sections. Nothing below is inferred from it. Every claim from here on stands on its own proof or its own computation, cited to file and line.

How — what has to be declared first

Five things, then the two names

These are the ingredients an operational memory question needs. They are ordered: each one is meaningless until the one before it has been fixed.

1A fixed observable
Name the quantity you will read, in advance, with a bound on its norm that does not grow with the detector time. Without this, “something is different” can always be arranged after the fact by choosing a new observable.
2A reference run you are allowed to compare with
The pulsed run and the reference run must agree on every exactly conserved quantity the specification admits. If the pulse changed one of them, you are comparing two different experiments, and their difference is not an admissible D32/D33 memory comparison — it may still be reported as a formal diagnostic. This is a real constraint, and one of this campaign’s earlier claims was retracted for violating it.
3A detector time
The reading happens at a time T, and T has to be stated. “Late” is not a time.
4An order of limits
A system with two large parameters gives different answers depending on which limit is taken first. The convention used throughout the computation below is stiffness first: C → ∞, then κT → ∞ with T/C → 0; equivalently 1 « κT « κC.
5A measurement cost
How many repetitions does the reading at time T need, for a stated relative error? A quantity whose readout cost grows exponentially in T is not a practical record even when its limit is nonzero. The bench above already showed this happening.

Only now, the two names

Two registers are built on exactly that operational data, and the campaign keeps them apart by name. Compressed to what you actually have to check:

Faithfully compressed from definitions.md § D32 and § D33; both are quoted in full below. Satisfying D33 does not license the unqualified word memory, and a system may satisfy D33 while failing D32.

The limit order, stated as a constraint on claims

“Permanent” requires a declared limit order. Convention (adopted from critic r1, Objection 6): C → ∞ FIRST, then 1 « κT « κC (invariantly: κT → ∞ with T/C → 0). Taking T → ∞ first at fixed C or fixed N is NOT supported by the sources; any permanence claim must state this iterated limit, a finite-C error or maximum usable detector time, and the detector’s sample cost at that time.

Both definitions are quoted in full from the repository. They share the same operational data — the five ingredients above.

D32 — memory, absolute-response register

Fix an operational specification: a C*-algebra A of allowed observables (for two coupled systems: with its stated superselection rules and one-sided subalgebras), a family of implementable protocols (preparation, evolution, at most one compact perturbation), each protocol P defining states ω_T^P at detector time T, and a declared asymptotic window (the order in which large-parameter limits are taken BEFORE T, and the admissible T-range within stated validity). Two protocols ω^pulse, ω^ref are comparable when they agree on every exactly conserved allowed charge of the specification. A comparable pair exhibits memory (unqualified) iff there exists a single allowed observable O ∈ A, fixed in advance with T-independent norm bound, such that lim_T [ω_T^pulse(O) − ω_T^ref(O)] exists in the declared window and is nonzero. The word “memory” without qualifier is reserved for this register throughout the campaign. (Relation to D26/D27: those are charge-ledger/counting definitions; D32 is the detector register. The reconstruction obligation connecting them is tracked, not assumed.)

definitions.md § D32 (memory, absolute-response register — TJO ruling 2026-08-31)

D33 — calibrated relational memory susceptibility

Same operational data as D32. A calibrated relational memory susceptibility is a detector functional F_T — a ratio or finite algebraic combination of finitely many allowed expectations in the two comparable protocols, with its measurement cost (shot count for a stated relative error at time T) declared as part of any claim — such that: (i) lim_T F_T exists in the declared window and is nonzero; (ii) F_T is invariant under the specification’s gauge redundancies (simultaneous reframings acting trivially on A); (iii) the limit equals the declared zero-frequency (DC) datum of the corresponding soft-theorem statement, with normalization fixed by measured injected charge, nothing fitted. Satisfying D33 does NOT license the unqualified word “memory” (D32). A system may satisfy D33 while failing D32 — the adjudicated instance is the two-boundary Schwarzian setting (claims JT-B-susc and JT-noplat-abs), where the divergence has a structural cause worth reporting in its own right: a thermal reference frame erodes its own record, and the readout cost grows exponentially in detector time. Gravitational-wave strain is the paradigm D33 functional that ALSO passes D32 because its calibrating denominator does not decay; the two registers coincide exactly when the ruler is persistent.

What — the Schwarzian station

The same two registers, on a real computation

Now the bench is replaced by an actual calculation, and the toy is left behind.

The system, in one paragraph. Jackiw–Teitelboim gravity enters here in exactly one role: an effective description of two gravitational boundary clocks. The Schwarzian action governs their soft time-reparametrisation mode, with a stiffness C — large C means a stiff clock. SYK is the microscopic many-body system whose low-energy clock mode this effective theory describes; you do not need it to read the panels. The detector is a two-sided correlator GLR(T,T): an allowed expectation in the two-copy algebra, not a frame label. Nothing on this page needs a wormhole picture, holographic duality, or celestial vocabulary.

Balanced Schwarzian rows, leading O(C⁻¹), C→∞ first:

  1. the calibrated relative-delay detector approaches a nonzero susceptibility, and its extrapolated DC residue agrees with the independently normalised soft vertex to 0.011% or better — Figure 2
  2. the allowed absolute correlator has no nonzero late plateau — Figure 1
  3. the pure butterfly shift has zero plateau — Figure 3

No uniform finite-C bound and no practical permanence is claimed — the decisive numbers.

The detector, written out. Both boundary clocks are dressed by their own SL(2) element; the reference run uses the identity, the pulsed run uses the matrix fitted from the integrated trajectory:

\[ G_{LR}(T)=\mathcal N_\Delta\left[\frac{\lvert t_L'(T)\,t_R'(T)\rvert}{\bigl(t_L(T)-t_R(T)\bigr)^{2}}\right]^{\Delta}, \qquad t_X = g_X\!\cdot\! x_X, \qquad x_L=-e^{-\kappa_L T},\quad x_R=e^{\kappa_R T}. \]

The numerical convention used by the results file, and by every number this page evaluates, is 𝒩Δ = 1. In the equal-κ chart of the proof shard this is equation (3.2), with Dg(T) = a x + (b+c) + d/x and x=eκT̄. The detector sees only three combinations of the relative matrix: a, b+c, and d.

\[ \delta G_{LR}(T)=\mathcal N_\Delta\,\kappa^{2\Delta} \Bigl[D_g(T)^{-2\Delta}-(x+x^{-1})^{-2\Delta}\Bigr] \qquad\quad \Delta u^{\rm lin}_{\rm rel}(T)=\frac{R_g(T)-1}{2\Delta\kappa\tanh(\kappa\bar T)} \]

Epilogue — where this sits

One corner of an older triangle

In gauge theory and in gravity, three statements are known to be different faces of one structure: a soft theorem constraining low-frequency emission, an asymptotic symmetry, and a memory effect at late times. This page tested the late-response corner of that triangle for a 0+1-dimensional thermal system and found a calibrated D33 susceptibility, not D32 memory. It has touched one edge only — the one joining the late detector reading to the zero-frequency datum, at leading order. The map is an epilogue here, not an introduction.

What is open

The unbalanced sector is held, not proved and not refuted: no implementable reference protocol with the same measured unequal pair of one-sided energies has been exhibited, so six of the eight computed points license no operational detector conclusion. The finite-C window is a scaling convention rather than a uniform bound. The microscopic finite-N window and the effective-theory window do not yet overlap. And the leading-order equality between the calibrated susceptibility and the DC residue is exactly that — leading order; exact finite-amplitude equality to a single-soft vertex is excluded, not merely unproven.

The result that survives all of this is small and, we think, worth stating plainly: in this system, at leading order and in the balanced sector, the calibrated relational susceptibility exists and matches the soft datum, the absolute record does not survive, and the butterfly shift is not a record at all. A thermal reference frame erodes its own ruler.
