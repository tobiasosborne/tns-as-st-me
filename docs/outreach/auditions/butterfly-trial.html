The Butterfly Effect on Trial

A shock hits one side of a two‑sided black hole and the two boundary clocks stop agreeing. The claim under examination is that this shift is a memory effect. You are the referee. Three cross‑examinations, each settled by an equation or by data that is on this page.

The finding, before the argument for it
  1. In the Schwarzian effective theory, the pure butterfly translation has zero late plateau.
  2. The fixed two‑sided correlator also decays.
  3. A different object — a balanced, calibrated, large‑\(C\)‑first susceptibility — survives, and equals a zero‑frequency soft residue.

Those three sentences are the result. The three votes below are the route by which you can check them against the equations and the computed points; they are not a device for withholding the answer. Each sentence carries a scope, and the scopes are stated where the sentence is proved.

Exhibit A

Two late effects. Only one of them is a record.

Before anything about black holes, a question you can settle with ordinary physics: what does it take for a disturbance to leave something behind? Two familiar answers sit side by side below. They look alike, and they are not.

For this case we need two operational registers that ordinary language can blur: a nonzero late difference in a fixed observable, which the campaign writes down as D32; and a calibrated ratio with a nonzero late limit tied to a zero‑frequency datum, which it writes down as D33. Both registers describe real effects. Only the first is a record, and on this page the whole case turns on which of the two is in play.

A — 1   A delay in ordinary scattering

Page-evaluated

Restrict, exactly, to one isolated s‑wave Breit–Wigner resonance at \(E_r\) of width \(\Gamma\) with no background phase. The resonant phase, taken on its continuous branch so that it rises by \(\pi\) across the resonance, is

\[ \delta(E)=\frac{\pi}{2}+\arctan\frac{E-E_r}{\Gamma/2},\qquad 2\hbar\,\frac{d\delta}{dE} =\frac{\hbar\,\Gamma}{(E-E_r)^{2}+(\Gamma/2)^{2}}. \] The right‑hand equality is one differentiation of the left‑hand definition, and the curve below is that closed form evaluated in this page. Taking the principal branch of \(\arctan[(\Gamma/2)/(E_r-E)]\) instead would put a jump at \(E=E_r\), and its derivative would not be this Lorentzian.

That combination is a delay because of one stationary‑phase line. The outgoing s‑wave carries phase \(kr+2\delta(E)\); the packet’s peak sits where \(\partial_E[\,kr+2\delta(E)-Et/\hbar\,]=0\), that is at \(t=r/v+2\hbar\,\delta'(E)\). The second term is the shift relative to free propagation, and it is what the curve shows.

Why nothing survives, computed rather than asserted. Take the free run first, in one dimension with a Gaussian packet of initial width \(\sigma\) and group velocity \(v\). The free evolution is exactly solvable:

\[ |\psi_t(x)|^{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma_t} \exp\!\left[-\frac{(x-vt)^{2}}{2\sigma_t^{2}}\right],\qquad \sigma_t=\sigma\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\hbar t}{2m\sigma^{2}}\right)^{2}}. \] The standard free Gaussian, written out so that the next line is arithmetic. Displayed, not evaluated by this page.

Now fix, in advance, any observable \(O\) supported in a bounded region \(B\) of length \(L\) near the detector, with \(\|O\|\le1\). Then \(|\langle\psi_t,O\psi_t\rangle|\le\int_B|\psi_t|^{2}\,dx\le L/(\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma_t)\), which falls like \(1/t\). The packet leaves, so the reading goes to zero. Restriction, stated rather than hidden: for the scattered run we assume what the resonance picture already assumes — no bound‑state component, and a state that is asymptotically a free outgoing packet. The delay then only replaces \(t\) by \(t-\Delta t\) in the same bound, which does not change the limit. So both runs go to zero and the limit of their difference is zero. This is an assumption about the toy, made explicit; it is not proved here, and nothing later on this page rests on it.

A — 2   A displacement at null infinity

Recorded quote

Two inertial detectors sit near future null infinity in a region with no Bondi news at early and at late times. Gravitational radiation passes between them. Integrating the geodesic deviation equation gives a DC effect: the initial and final separations differ by

\[ \Delta s^{\bar z}=\frac{\gamma^{z\bar z}}{2r}\,\Delta C_{zz}\,s^{z}. \] Quoted from refs/arxiv-1703.05448/soft_Arxiv_update.tex:2594 (Strominger, Lectures on the Infrared Structure of Gravity and Gauge Theory).
Record1703.05448:329

“This is a subtle DC effect, in which the passage of gravitational waves produces a permanent shift in the relative positions of a pair of inertial detectors.”

The thing being compared here — the separation of two freely falling masses — does not itself decay. \(\Delta C_{zz}\) need not vanish, because flatness does not require \(C_{zz}=0\). A fixed observable retains a nonzero difference as \(t\to\infty\).

A preliminary vote · settles nothing, on purpose
Which of the two deserves the word memory?

Both are effects of a disturbance. Both are still there after the disturbance is gone, in some sense of “there”.

The two registers, written down

The campaign settled this by writing two definitions instead of arguing about one word. They are quoted here because every later ruling on this page turns on which of the two is in play.

D32  /  D33   the two registers

Recorded quote

D32 — memory, absolute register. Two protocols are comparable when they agree on every exactly conserved allowed charge of the specification. A comparable pair exhibits memory (unqualified) if and only if there is a single allowed observable \(O\), fixed in advance with a \(T\)‑independent norm bound, such that \(\lim_{T}\left[\omega_T^{\rm pulse}(O)-\omega_T^{\rm ref}(O)\right]\) exists in the declared window and is nonzero.

D33 — calibrated relational memory susceptibility. A detector functional \(F_T\) — a ratio or finite algebraic combination of finitely many allowed expectations in the two comparable protocols, with its measurement cost declared as part of any claim — whose limit exists, is nonzero, is invariant under the specification’s gauge redundancies, and equals the declared zero‑frequency datum of the corresponding soft‑theorem statement, with normalization fixed by measured injected charge, nothing fitted.

A **calibrated relational memory susceptibility** is a detector functional `F_T` — a ratio or finite algebraic combination of finitely many allowed expectations in the two comparable protocols, with its measurement cost (shot count for a stated relative error at time `T`) declared as part of any claim — such that: (i) `lim_T F_T` exists in the declared window and is nonzero; (ii) `F_T` is invariant under the specification's gauge redundancies (simultaneous reframings acting trivially on `A`); (iii) the limit equals the declared zero-frequency (DC) datum of the corresponding soft-theorem statement, with normalization fixed by measured injected charge, nothing fitted. The docket

What is actually accused, and of what

One page of setup. No holography is required to follow it, and none is used below.

Take two copies prepared in the thermofield‑double state. In the Schwarzian regime each boundary has a trajectory \(t_L(u)\) or \(t_R(u)\) with stiffness \(C\). A simultaneous \(SL(2,\mathbb R)\) Möbius transformation is redundant; the allowed two‑sided correlator depends on their relative configuration. SYK supplies a microscopic realization of this effective regime, but no finite‑\(N\) conclusion is used here. Its own separate verdict is in the open dockets at the end.

A binding specification fixes what may be measured. The readout is the two‑sided correlator \(G_{LR}\) between the two boundaries. From it the record builds exactly two quantities, and the entire case is about telling them apart.

The two boundaries, and the two readouts

The calibrated ratio.

“At every finite \(T\), the quantity … is an allowed detector functional: its numerator is a difference of two Layer‑1 two‑sided expectations and its denominator can be calibrated by a finite difference of equilibrium expectations. It is not itself a new operator…”

The shock, as a transfer matrix

Proved formula

In this language the celebrated Shenker–Stanford shock is one specific element of the post‑pulse Möbius transfer matrix — a pure horizon translation \(\tilde v=v+\alpha\):

\[ g_{\rm shock}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \alpha\equiv\frac{E}{4M}e^{R t_w/\ell^{2}}. \] theory/lanes/syk-jt/t1-analytic.md (3.14); the shift and \(\alpha\) from refs/arxiv-1306.0622/shock.tex:196. Displayed, not evaluated here.

In the Shenker–Stanford double‑scaling regime \(\alpha=(E/4M)e^{Rt_w/\ell^{2}}\); at fixed small \(E/M\), inserting the perturbation earlier produces exponential boost sensitivity. The late detector question is separate from that growth. It is not whether \(\alpha\) is large — it is — but whether the shift it produces in the boundary clocks is a record in the sense of D32.

The charge

“The black‑hole butterfly effect is a memory effect.” Three things had to be settled before that sentence could be ruled on, and the campaign’s adversarial record settled each of them the hard way. They are the three cross‑examinations that follow. In each, one vote is yours, and each vote is decided by something displayed on this page — a conservation law, a fit, or a line of code.

Cross‑examination I

Can the two runs be compared at all?

D32 does not begin with a limit. It begins with a precondition: the pulsed run and the reference run must agree on every exactly conserved allowed charge. Otherwise the difference between them is not a response — it is a change of sector.

Here the exactly conserved allowed charges include the two boundary energies. For a thermal clock of exponent \(\kappa\), the measured energy is \(E=C\kappa^{2}/2\). A right‑only pulse changes the right clock from \(\kappa_i\) to \(\kappa_f\) and leaves the left clock alone.

The first submission compared that pulsed run against a reference in which both clocks had been reset to \(\kappa_f\). The two runs then carry different left energies. Before reading the objection, look at the ledger the specification actually demands.

Cross‑examination II

Does the fixed observable approach a constant, or decay?

Comparability granted, D32 asks one question of the absolute observable \(\delta G_{LR}\): does its late‑time limit exist and is it nonzero? Two theorems answer it. Then the computed points are shown, as a check on the theorems rather than a substitute for them.

The butterfly law

Proved formula
\[ \Delta u_{\rm rel}^{\rm NL}(T)=-\frac{\alpha}{\kappa}e^{-\kappa\bar T}+O(e^{-2\kappa\bar T}), \qquad \delta G_{LR}=O\!\left(e^{-(2\Delta+1)\kappa\bar T}\right), \qquad \bar T=T-u_*. \] theory/lanes/syk-jt/t1-analytic.md ⟨1⟩3 (3.15). PROVED; claim row JT‑bfly‑decay.

Scope, in full. For the pure Shenker–Stanford horizon translation \(g_{\rm shock}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\alpha\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), in the energy‑balanced, \(u_*\)‑synchronized Schwarzian future chart, in the large‑\(C\)‑first late window. For that translation the Möbius coefficient is exactly \(a=1\), so the constant term vanishes exactly and the leading detector response is \(-\alpha\kappa^{-1}e^{-\kappa(T-u_*)}\).

The general balanced law

Proved formula

Cross‑examination III

Was the zero‑frequency datum obtained independently?

One statement survives all of the above: the balanced calibrated susceptibility equals the \(\omega\to0\) residue of the same soft attachment. That is a leading‑order identity between the calibrated D33 susceptibility and the zero‑frequency soft residue — but only if the residue was computed without being told the answer.

This is the objection a careful reader should raise about any numerical “confirmation” of a final‑value identity, and in this campaign it was raised, and it was correct. Here is the transform as it stood in the first submission. The routine is handed the late detector value late_delta, and appends an analytic tail:

Why the two registers came apart here

Not because anything is wrong with the calculation, and not because the butterfly effect is small. Both \(\delta G_{LR}\) and the equilibrium calibration \(\partial_TG_{LR}^{\rm eq}\) decay as \(e^{-2\Delta\kappa(T-u_*)}\); their ratio can tend to a constant while neither absolute signal does. In the gravitational‑wave case the calibrating denominator — the separation of two freely falling masses — does not decay, so the ratio register and the absolute register agree, and the word memory is safe. Here the ratio is well defined and equals the soft‑theorem datum; what it is not is a record you could come back and read.

That is a finding about thermal reference frames, and it is worth having in its own right. It is also a smaller claim than the one on the docket, which is the honest outcome.
