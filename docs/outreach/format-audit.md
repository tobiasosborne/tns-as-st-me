# Format audit: how to make the SYK/JT result impossible not to forward

The audience is a traditional physicist, not a holographer and not a web
designer. The relevant test is therefore severe: does the format let that
physicist *discover or interrogate the physics*, or does it merely decorate an
explanation? Scores are from that chair. “Feasibility” means feasibility from
the campaign's existing assets, not generic ease of production. Totals break
ties only; the rank also reflects whether the format has an audience-facing
idea rather than just a delivery channel.

## 2. Failure analysis of the leading eight

### 1. Two-registers lab — the discovery is visibly baked in

The fatal version is a pedagogical puppet theatre: every detector the reader
touches behaves exactly as the labels “absolute” and “relational” predict, and
the final SYK/JT panel arrives by analogy. A professor will enjoy it for a
minute and then correctly ask what was actually established. The magic survives
only if the toy produces a genuine surprise *and* then gives way to the real
allowed observables, order of limits, error terms, and readout cost. The toy may
teach the question; it may not carry the conclusion.

### 2. Executable paper — the execution is cosmetic

The most likely failure is a beautifully animated surrogate: parameters slide
continuously, a graph moves, and “verify” checks a canned answer. That is worse
than a static figure because it borrows authority from computation without
exposing its domain. A serious physicist will test an edge case immediately.
The experience has to distinguish precomputed samples, mathematically justified
interpolation, and genuinely rerun checks; outside the certified region it must
say **not computed**, not draw a persuasive curve.

### 3. Detective game — it becomes a gotcha

The game dies if the winning move is spotting a hidden textual qualifier or
guessing what the author wants. The actual prize must be a physical inference:
for example, distinguishing a decaying absolute signal from a plateauing ratio,
or noticing that an unbalanced comparison changes a conserved charge and hence
is not a valid reference experiment. If the player loses because the interface
withheld a premise, the campaign's honesty is turned into trickery.

### 4. Adversarial review as content — the process eats the result

The repository has enough objections to make a compelling scene and far more
than an audience should consume. Publishing the transcript faithfully would be
a category error: `FAIL(8)` is provenance, not pacing. The magical version
compresses the trajectory into the two or three objections that changed the
physics—the invalid unbalanced comparator, the non-circular DC extraction, and
the permanence/readout-cost fence—and leaves the complete record one click
away. Otherwise the reader remembers the campaign machinery and forgets the
zero plateau.

### 5. AI canvas lecturer — trust collapses before the physics lands

This audience will deliberately ask the lecturer an awkward limit-order or
scope question. One smooth but wrong answer poisons every earlier animation;
one obviously scripted refusal reveals a branching slideshow wearing an AI
mask. The lecturer therefore has the highest ceiling and the most brittle
credibility of any candidate. Its wow is rented from model behavior, while the
result's wow ought to come from the detector and the equations.

### 6. Claims DAG as publication — graph shock at the front door

Dropping a reader into dozens of colored nodes asks them to learn the research
program before they know why any node matters. The visual will be parsed as
dependency-management software. A single guided question must precede the
graph; only after the first result should the page pull back and reveal that
every sentence has a navigable proof, computation, criticism, and status. The
reveal is magical. The graph as homepage is clerical.

### 7. Referee-mode toggle — honesty is mistaken for apology

If skeptic mode merely injects more parentheticals, red warnings, and
NOT-CLAIMED boxes, it makes the work look fragile. The toggle has to change the
reader's *task*: enthusiast mode asks “what was found?”, while referee mode
asks “what observation, assumption, or limit licenses this exact sentence?”
The latter should expose evidence and dependency, not just multiply caveats.

### 8. Negative results serialized — the ending never arrives

Serial form naturally rewards suspense, but a sequence of retractions and HOLD
verdicts can feel like a campaign forever promising the theorem in the next
episode. Each installment needs a closed scientific payoff. “The butterfly
shift has zero plateau” is one; “the two memory registers coincide iff the
ruler persists” is another. A HOLD is a sober boundary after a result, not a
cliffhanger marketed as a result.

## 3. Recommendation — exactly three picks

### Pick 1 — **The Memory Lab** (bundle: 10 + 7 + 11)

**Audience-facing identity, in one sentence.** An interactive experimental
bench where the reader pulses a detector, changes whether its ruler persists,
and discovers—on the campaign's actual computation—that a permanent record, a
calibrated susceptibility, and a butterfly time delay are three different
things.

**Why it wins on wow for this audience.** This is the only candidate that can
make the central conceptual result feel experimentally inevitable and the
numerical result feel physically surprising. The reader does not begin by
learning the terms D32 and D33. They watch an absolute signal vanish, rescue a
stable datum by calibrating it against the same decaying ruler, and then see
that datum land on the independently extracted DC soft residue to 0.011%. For
a physicist, the magic is not the animation. It is that changing what counts as
the ruler changes the honest answer to “did the system remember?” The final
**verify** action turns the page from a demonstration into an instrument.

**The why → how → what journey.** The opening screen asks one question over a
familiar gravitational-wave sketch: *after the wave has passed, what would
have to remain different for you to say that the apparatus remembers it?*

- **Why:** The reader first operates a persistent ruler and a decaying ruler.
  The same pulse can leave a readable offset in one and only a calibrated
  ratio in the other. No SYK vocabulary appears yet. The discrepancy creates
  the need for two registers.

- **How:** The bench reveals the operational ingredients one at a time: a
  fixed observable, a reference protocol with the same conserved charges, a
  detector time, an order of limits, and a measurement cost. A low-frequency
  dial then connects the late response to the zero-frequency scattering datum.
  Only now are the labels **absolute response (D32)** and **calibrated
  relational susceptibility (D33)** attached to what the reader has already
  found.

- **What:** The apparatus switches to the real balanced-pulse Schwarzian
  calculation. The absolute two-sided correlator difference dies; the
  calibrated relative-clock ratio plateaus in the large-\(C\)-first window;
  an independently computed DC residue converges to the same value. A second
  button injects the pure Shenker–Stanford shift and shows its exponential
  tail reaching zero: time delay, yes; memory, no. Only after this result does
  the view pull back into the infrared triangle, which acts as an epilogue and
  navigation map rather than unexplained homepage iconography.

**On-ramp from QM/GR/QED.** Begin with objects the reader can already reason
about: a two-level clock, a harmonic detector with and without a stable
reference oscillator, a scattering time delay, and the reported LIGO-era
picture of gravitational-wave memory as a lasting interferometer displacement.
Use the QED soft-photon limit to introduce the idea that a zero-frequency
scattering datum can control a late response. Then introduce JT in one narrow
role—an effective theory of two gravitational boundary clocks—and the
Schwarzian in one sentence—as the stiffness-controlled action for their soft
time-reparametrization mode. SYK is the microscopic many-body realization
behind that low-energy clock theory, not prerequisite lore. No wormhole,
AdS/CFT, or celestial vocabulary is needed to operate the bench.

**Kill-risk and mitigation.** The kill-risk is false executability: a toy
analogy followed by sliders that draw plausible curves outside the actual
calculation. The mitigation must be architectural. Give the toy and the
Schwarzian station visibly different provenance; label each parameter region
as exact formula, computed sample, or not computed; never interpolate across a
scope boundary; print the large-\(C\)-first window and shot-cost growth beside
the plateau; and make **verify** rerun a fixed, identified subset of the actual
tests. The interface should become less glossy at the decisive moment: values,
equations, residuals, and scope should take over the screen.

**Existing campaign assets consumed.** This pick directly consumes
`numerics/results/schwarzian_memory.json`,
`numerics/test/test_schwarzian_memory.jl`, the balanced-pulse analytic result in
`theory/lanes/syk-jt/t1-analytic.md`, the numerical report and repair records,
claim rows `JT-B-susc`, `JT-bfly-decay`, and `JT-noplat-abs`, definitions D32
and D33, the readable operational account in labbook section 19, and the
claims DAG for the final triangle reveal. It also exploits the existing
negative finite-size ED result as an optional “change microscope” panel that
says, honestly, why the microscopic and effective-theory windows do not yet
overlap.

### Pick 2 — **The Butterfly Effect on Trial** (bundle: 18 + 8 + 16)

**Audience-facing identity, in one sentence.** A twenty-minute scientific trial
in which the reader serves as referee for the claim “the black-hole butterfly
effect is a memory effect,” tests the decisive evidence, and delivers the
campaign's real verdict.

**Why it wins on wow for this audience.** “The butterfly effect is not memory”
is the campaign's cleanest plot twist, and the reason it fails is substantive:
the celebrated shift is a transient relational time delay with zero plateau.
This format turns the negative result into the motor of the story rather than
an apologetic footnote. It also lets a traditional physicist inhabit the role
they trust most—the skeptical reader. The real proposer/critic history gives
the drama a texture that no invented quiz can match: an attractive stronger
claim failed, an inadmissible comparison was retracted, a non-circular DC test
survived, and a narrower result became sharper.

**The why → how → what journey.** The trial opens with the accusation, not a
SYK lecture: *a shock shifts the relative boundary clock; when is that shift a
record?*

- **Why:** Exhibit A contrasts a scattering time delay with a permanent
  interferometer displacement. The reader votes on which deserves the word
  memory and is immediately shown that “a late effect” is not yet an
  operational definition.

- **How:** Three authored cross-examinations expose the load-bearing physics.
  First, can the pulsed and reference experiments actually be compared without
  changing an exactly conserved charge? Second, does the fixed observable
  approach a nonzero constant or decay? Third, was the DC residue extracted
  independently of the purported late plateau? The reader handles a small
  amount of real data and one equation at each stage; the prover, critic, and
  adjudicator voices supply context, objection, and ruling.

- **What:** The verdict card separates three statements. The pure butterfly
  shift has zero plateau and is **not memory**. The absolute two-sided
  correlator also has no late plateau. A balanced pulse nevertheless has a
  calibrated large-\(C\)-first susceptibility equal to the DC soft datum at
  leading order. The final “case history” shows the honest trajectory from r1
  `FAIL(8)` through retraction and repair to r2 PASS, with open microscopic and
  finite-coupling questions entered as open dockets, not sequels baited as
  imminent victories.

**On-ramp from QM/GR/QED.** Start from Wigner time delay in scattering and a
pair of ordinary quantum clocks; neither requires holography. Contrast that
transient with gravitational-wave displacement memory, where a stable ruler
carries a before/after difference. Recall the QED idea that the soft limit
constrains long-time response. Only then identify the two clocks with the two
boundaries of the low-energy JT system, explain the Schwarzian as their soft
clock dynamics with stiffness `C`, and identify the two-sided correlator as the
allowed detector readout. The reader needs no prior picture of SYK beyond “a
many-body quantum system whose low-energy clock mode is described by this
effective theory.”

**Kill-risk and mitigation.** The kill-risk is a courtroom gimmick that rewards
button-clicking and fetishizes review process. Remove scores, avatars, badges,
and branching for its own sake. There should be exactly three consequential
decisions, all settled by displayed physics; a wrong vote triggers the missing
observable, conservation law, or fit comparison, never a canned “try again.”
Cap the main path at twenty minutes. Reduce the eight first-round objections to
the three that altered claim content, while preserving the full transcript as
an evidence drawer. The adjudicator must sound like a terse referee report, not
a game-show host.

**Existing campaign assets consumed.** This pick is almost entirely an edit of
material already present: `theory/verdicts/syk-jt-joint-critic-r1.md` and
`syk-jt-joint-critic-r2.md`; the two repair responses; the retracted unbalanced
claim and surviving balanced theorem in the analytic lane; exponential-versus-
constant fit data and independent DC extraction in
`numerics/results/schwarzian_memory.json`; the real regression suite; the three
adjudicated claim rows; HOLD verdicts from the finite-size ED and finite-
coupling rungs; and the labbook's readable synthesis. The transcripts supply
voices, but equations and recomputations remain the exhibits.

### Pick 3 — **The Proof Atlas** (bundle: 6 + 9 + 12 + 14 + 15)

**Audience-facing identity, in one sentence.** A guided map of the argument in
which every plain-language physics claim opens into its assumptions, Lamport
proof, numerical witness, adversarial history, exact status, and nearest honest
boundary.

**Why it wins on wow for this audience.** A physicist has seen interactive
figures; almost none has seen an entire scientific claim made inspectable as an
object. The forwardable moment is the pullback: after following one clean
argument, the reader discovers that every node in the result carries a live
epistemic contract—PROVED, SKETCH, CONJECTURE, REFUTED, or HOLD; dependencies;
scope; computation; and criticism. This is not blockchain-flavored “trust.” It
is a publication that exposes exactly where trust is and is not required. The
campaign already owns the unusually expensive substrate, so the wow comes from
scientific architecture rather than web ornament.

**The why → how → what journey.** The default view is a seven-stop authored
route, never the full graph.

- **Why:** It begins with the shared infrared puzzle from QED and gravitational
  memory: why do a low-frequency scattering law, a symmetry constraint, and a
  late detector record know about one another—and can that triangle survive in
  a finite thermal quantum system?

- **How:** The next nodes introduce the operational detector, comparable
  protocols, the soft clock mode, the DC extraction, and the permanence test.
  At every node, enthusiast mode states the physical move and shows only its
  one load-bearing equation. Referee mode replaces that view with “what licenses
  this sentence?” and exposes assumptions, limit order, falsifier, criticism,
  and NOT-claimed boundary. Collapsible Lamport levels give a flyover first and
  the complete proof only when requested.

- **What:** The route lands on the three adjudicated SYK/JT statements and the
  D32/D33 split, then pulls back to reveal the full triangle and its unresolved
  edges. Claim-level permalinks make each result citable without pretending the
  whole graph has one status. Subscribable changes say “this row moved from
  HOLD to PROVED” rather than advertising a vague paper update.

**On-ramp from QM/GR/QED.** Each unfamiliar object gets a two-line translation
rail: one familiar role and one explicit non-equivalence. The soft Schwarzian
mode plays the role that a low-frequency collective coordinate plays in an
effective theory, but is not a microscopic particle. The Ward statement plays
the same logical role as a QED symmetry constraint on a soft amplitude, but the
system is 0+1-dimensional. The two-sided correlator is an allowed many-body
detector expectation, while its calibrated ratio is not an absolute record.
Gravitational-wave strain supplies the case where the ruler persists and both
registers agree. A glossary is secondary; the route should teach through these
controlled comparisons and flag precisely where each analogy stops.

The “ask” box is a navigation aid inside this atlas, not an autonomous lecturer.
It may return approved claim text, definitions, dependencies, short extracts
from proofs, and exact source links. When asked beyond scope, it should point to
the relevant CONJECTURE, REFUTED, or HOLD node and explain the missing edge. It
must not synthesize a new derivation. That constrained behavior makes refusal
useful rather than merely cautious.

**Kill-risk and mitigation.** The kill-risk is that the atlas looks like Jira
for theorem proving: status colors, arrows, and metadata before physics. The
mitigation is a strict reveal order. Open on one question and one guided path;
show no node whose first line is not a plain-language physical statement; hide
machine metadata until requested; and reveal the global DAG only after the
reader reaches the first verdict. Referee mode must foreground evidence, not
spray red caveat boxes. The AI component must be extractive and source-linked,
with its answer vocabulary generated from the status manifest rather than
allowed to improvise physics.

**Existing campaign assets consumed.** This is the natural publication skin
for `claims/CLAIMS.md` and its dependency/status fields, the hierarchical proofs
under `theory/lanes/syk-jt/`, the numerical JSONs and test suites, the r1/r2
verdicts and repair trail, definitions D32/D33, the 202-page labbook, and the
676-entry Strominger remarks database. The rigorous/blackboard voice split can
appear inside a node when useful, but it remains supporting annotation rather
than a fourth recommended format. Claim permalinks and machine-readable status
manifests are infrastructure under the atlas identity, not a separate product.

## 4. Explicit cut

### Strongest idea cut: the AI canvas lecturer (#5)

This is the most seductive proposal not selected. In a flawless demo, a
lecturer that notices where an individual physicist is confused, asks the right
Socratic question, and draws the next diagram live would be astonishing. It
also matches the why → how → what arc almost perfectly.

I would still cut it. Its magic is borrowed from the behavior of the AI, not
from the physics, and this audience will stress-test it exactly where it is
weakest: order of limits, conservation sectors, the distinction between an
absolute observable and a ratio, and what the effective theory does not license
microscopically. One confident scope error contaminates the whole artifact. If
the lecturer is constrained tightly enough never to make that error, it becomes
a costly, slower branching video whose “Socratic” choices are visibly canned.
It also makes the experience non-reproducible: two colleagues following the
same scientific argument may receive different explanations of the claim.

The narrow part worth saving is already absorbed into the Proof Atlas: a
source-linked interlocutor that navigates frozen claims and refuses to derive
new physics. It does not control the story and is not asked to be charismatic.
That sacrifice is correct. The thing a GR professor should forward is “you can
make the detector forget while the calibrated soft datum survives,” not “look
how fluently an AI explained holography to me.”
