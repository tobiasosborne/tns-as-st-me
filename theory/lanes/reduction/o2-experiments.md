# O2 — quantum-mechanical experiments for the campaign quantities

This lane uses only operations and measurements on physical spins.  An MPS
transfer matrix or virtual operator may be used to predict a result, but it is
never treated as an apparatus observable.  Every extrapolation below is made
from finite chains, finite windows, and finite-time experiments.

Status guard: the protocols below do not change any campaign status.  In
particular, WI, A1, M-INDEX-fin, and S2-2body-S retain their `PROVED` status in
`claims/CLAIMS.md`; asymptotic TPM statements retain their displayed (LR)
hypotheses; the reconstruction experiment in §5 is `PROPOSED` and its identity
is `CONJECTURED`.  Statements about laboratory feasibility or noise models are
marked `[physical argument]`.

## 1. Measuring the projective class `[ω] in H²(G,U(1))`

The target is the class of the multiplier in

`V(h)V(g)=exp(i omega(h,g))V(hg)`,

not a phase convention for any one `V(g)`.  Two complementary physical-spin
protocols are useful.  The first is a bulk string-selection experiment.  The
second reconstructs the symmetry action on a physical open-chain edge.  The
string route is especially direct for finite abelian groups; for a general
group, commuting-pair data need not determine every class in `H^2`, so the edge
process-tomography route supplies the missing relator data.

### 1A. String-order selection

**Preparation.** Prepare many copies of the same injective, unbroken,
`G`-symmetric MPS vacuum on a chain much longer than a string of length `L`.
Calibrate the on-site matrices `u(g)` using one-spin process tomography.  Choose
commuting `g,h in G`.  On endpoint blocks of at least the injectivity length,
choose a basis of physical operators `O_L,O_R` and resolve it into `h`-charge
channels.  Define the right-end convention by

`U_{B_R}(h) O_R U_{B_R}(h)^dagger = chi_R(h) O_R`.

The endpoint blocks, rather than single sites, are essential: injectivity only
guarantees that a sufficiently padded block spans the full endpoint operator
space.

**Operation.** On each copy apply, or coherently control, the physical string

`U_[1,L](g)=prod_{x=1}^L u_x(g)`

between the two endpoint blocks.  Repeat over all endpoint charge channels and
over a set of commuting pairs that generates the commuting-pair invariants of
the group.  A control qubit may be encoded in an additional addressable
physical spin; no virtual leg is addressed.  `[physical argument]` For a finite
on-site symmetry this is a parallel pulse.  For a compact Lie group it is a
calibrated finite-angle pulse generated by the on-site charge.

**Measurement.** Measure the complex string correlator

`S_L(O_L,O_R;g)=Tr[rho O_L U_[1,L](g) O_R]`.

Its real and imaginary parts can be obtained by a Hadamard/Ramsey test of the
controlled physical-spin unitary, or by decomposing the non-Hermitian product
into Hermitian Pauli strings.  The local source states the relevant endpoint
selection condition as follows:

> “the conditions for the SOP not to vanish are: (i) `\rho({\cal E}_u)=1`;
> (ii) `{\rm tr}[\Lambda V^\dagger {\cal E}_y(\1)]`,
> `{\rm tr}[\Lambda {\cal E}_x(V)]\ne 0`.”

This is the selection rule in
`refs/arxiv-0802.0447/StringOrder-v10.tex:253-255`.  The same file defines the
measured physical string `x tensor u^{tensor N} tensor y` at lines 112-122.

**Readout formula.** For commuting `g,h`, define the gauge-invariant endpoint
commutator

`c_g(h):=V(h)V(g)V(h)^(-1)V(g)^(-1)
       =exp(i[omega(h,g)-omega(g,h)]).`

With the right-end convention above, covariance of the endpoint trace gives

`S_infinity(O_L,O_R;g) != 0  only if  chi_R(h)=c_g(h)`.            (1.1)

Reversing the endpoint or using the conjugation convention
`U(h)^dagger O U(h)` replaces both sides by their reciprocals.  Thus the
nonvanishing physical charge channel reads out `c_g(h)` without choosing a
matrix representative for `V`.  For finite abelian `G`, the table of `c_g(h)`
is the alternating bicharacter normally used to label the projective class.
For a group whose
`H^2` contains classes invisible to commuting-pair commutators, (1.1) is only a
partial invariant and must be combined with §1B; it is not advertised as a
complete classifier by itself.

**Error and convergence.** Let the two endpoint blocks have fixed total width
`ell`.  D1 gives, for every `lambda_tilde in (lambda_E,1)`,

`|S_L-S_infinity| <= C_(lambda_tilde,O) lambda_tilde^(L-ell)`.       (1.2)

For `M` independent shots of a bounded interferometric readout, the statistical
standard error is at most `1/sqrt(M)` before readout-error mitigation.  If the
smallest deliberately excited nonzero channel has magnitude `A_min`, a safe
classification condition is

`C lambda_tilde^(L-ell)+z/sqrt(M)+epsilon_cal < A_min/2`,

where `z` fixes the desired confidence and `epsilon_cal` is the calibrated
pulse/readout bias.  `[physical argument]` Varying `L` is part of the protocol:
an exponential fit separates a true selection zero from a short-string
accident.

**What failure looks like.** Persistent weight in two incompatible `h`-charge
channels, above (1.2) and the shot budget, falsifies the assumed symmetric
injective-MPS description or the pulse calibration.  Exponential decay of
every tried endpoint correlator does *not* establish a trivial class: it can
mean that the chosen endpoint basis has zero overlap.  Failure of the inferred
`c_g(h)` values to obey their group consistency relations is likewise a failed
preparation/model check, not a new cohomology class.

### 1B. Physical edge process tomography and relator phases

**Preparation.** Prepare the gapped parent Hamiltonian, or a Hamiltonian in the
same symmetric phase, on an open chain.  Resolve a low-energy edge multiplet by
spectroscopy and prepare a tomographically complete set of states in it using
fields supported on physical spins near the two ends.  Keep the two edges much
farther apart than the correlation length.  This route is conditional on the
physical-edge/split hypothesis: the low-energy edge register must be isolated
and locally controllable.  The campaign explicitly does not derive that
realisation from A1 alone (`definitions.md:807-826`).

**Operation.** Apply the global physical symmetry `U_[1,N](g)` for a generating
set of `G`.  Use independently addressable boundary preparation and analysis
pulses to perform process tomography on the two-edge ground space, then factor
the reconstructed action into spatially separated left/right actions using the
two physical boundary algebras.  No invariant right-edge reference state is
assumed; the compensating right action is reconstructed as well.  Denote the
left matrices by `R(g)`.  Their basis and overall phases are deliberately left
free.

**Measurement.** Use only projective or informationally complete POVM
measurements on boundary spins.  Spectroscopy first measures the edge
degeneracy; Ramsey/process tomography then measures transition amplitudes and
relative phases within the edge code space.  `[physical argument]` A robust
multiplet of dimension at least `d_omega` is a useful capacity check, but
degeneracy alone is not a complete class measurement.

**Readout formula.** For every tested product `hg`, form

`omega_hat(h,g) := Arg Tr[R(h)R(g)R(hg)^dagger]`,                 (1.3)

after checking that `R(h)R(g)R(hg)^dagger` is proportional to the identity on
the resolved edge space.  More robustly, take the phase of the closest scalar
unitary in the polar decomposition.  For a finite presentation
`G=<g_i | r_a=e>`, also multiply the reconstructed `R(g_i)` along every relator;
the scalar relator phases, together with (1.3), determine the multiplier class.

Under an arbitrary reconstructed-edge basis change and phase convention,

`R(g) -> exp(i phi(g)) W R(g) W^dagger`,

`omega_hat` changes only by the coboundary
`phi(h)+phi(g)-phi(hg)`, while conjugation by `W` drops out.  Therefore the
reported object is `[omega_hat]`, never the individual table
`omega_hat(h,g)`.  This and `c_g(h)` in §1A are explicitly
representation-independent readouts: neither depends on an MPS gauge, an edge
basis, or a phase choice for `V(g)`.

**Error and convergence.** `[physical argument]` If each physical edge block
has width `ell_B`, a conservative systematic budget is

`epsilon_edge <= C lambda_tilde^min(ell_B,N-2ell_B)
                 + epsilon_split + epsilon_leak`,                 (1.4)

where `epsilon_split` is the spectroscopically measured finite-chain edge
splitting and `epsilon_leak` is leakage out of the selected multiplet.  Full
process tomography has shot error scaling at least as a polynomial in the edge
dimension, schematically `O(d_edge^2/sqrt(M))`; bootstrap the inferred relator
phases rather than assigning an unwrapped phase error by hand.  A class is
resolved only when every admissible cocycle class outside the reported one is
farther away than the combined phase-confidence region.

**What failure looks like.** A product `R(h)R(g)R(hg)^dagger` that remains
nonscalar as `N,ell_B` grow signals leakage, unresolved multiple edge sectors,
symmetry breaking, or failure of the split-edge assumption.  A multiplet that
splits by an amount not decaying with edge separation is not a protected edge
register.  If string selection and edge tomography disagree after their
orientation conventions are aligned, the experiment has failed its common
state/preparation assumptions; one must not choose whichever answer matches a
desired SPT label.

## 2. Preparing and observing the boundary action

The half-infinite operation is implemented experimentally as a sequence of
finite operations, not as a literal infinite product.  Fix the near endpoint at
the bond `(-1|0)` and put the remote endpoint at `(L|L+1)`.

**Preparation.** Prepare an injective `G`-symmetric vacuum on a chain with both
physical ends far from `[-w,L]`.  Choose `g`, calibrate `u(g)`, and reserve the
fixed tomography window `W=[-w,w]` around the near endpoint.  Repeat for an
increasing sequence of `L>w`.  In the unbroken experiment `g in H_alpha`; a
broken `g` is also allowed, but then the limiting state is the physical kink
state with vacuum `alpha` on the left and `g dot alpha` on the right, rather
than an endpoint state in the same vacuum sector.

**Operation.** Apply the sudden finite-region symmetry pulse

`U_[0,L](g)=prod_{x=0}^L u_x(g)`.                              (2.1)

This is a local unitary for every finite `L`.  WI says that it leaves two
endpoint insertions, with `V(g)^(-1)` at `(-1|0)` and `V(g)` at `(L|L+1)`, and
changes the interior vacuum only in the broken case.  `[physical argument]`
The pulse duration should be short compared with the intrinsic Hamiltonian
timescale, or
the known Hamiltonian evolution during the pulse should be included in the
tomographic model; otherwise (2.1) is not the applied unitary.

**Measurement.** Immediately after the pulse, perform informationally complete
tomography on the *physical spins* in `W`.  Product Pauli bases, generalized
Pauli bases for spin `S`, or any calibrated informationally complete POVM are
sufficient.  Reconstruct

`rho_W^(L,g)=Tr_(complement W)[U_[0,L](g) rho U_[0,L](g)^dagger]`.

Also measure a selected set of bounded `O in A_W` directly.  The direct
observables are a lower-cost convergence check; full reduced-state tomography
is the observation of the endpoint datum itself.

**Readout formula.** Define the operational endpoint state by its compatible
finite-window reductions,

`rho_W^(infinity,g):=lim_(L->infinity) rho_W^(L,g)`.             (2.2)

For the unbroken normal-ordered MPS, A1 identifies (2.2) with the reduced state
of the single near-endpoint insertion `V(g)^(-1)@(-1|0)`.  This statement does
not instruct the apparatus to measure `V(g)`; the apparatus reports
`rho_W^(infinity,g)` and all values `Tr[rho_W^(infinity,g) O]`.  Endpoint states
for two insertions `M,M'` are equal exactly when `M'` is a nonzero scalar
multiple of `M`, so the state-level readout lives in the representation-
independent `PGL(chi)` orbit established by A1.

**Error and convergence.** For a fixed `W`, every
`lambda_tilde in (lambda_E,1)` admits a constant `C_(W,g,lambda_tilde)` such
that

`sup_(O in A_W, ||O||<=1)
 |Tr[O(rho_W^(L,g)-rho_W^(infinity,g))]|
 <= C_(W,g,lambda_tilde) lambda_tilde^(L-w)`.                  (2.3)

Equation (2.3) is the campaign's proved transfer-rate form; a bare
`lambda_E^L` is not valid when the transfer map has a Jordan block.  In the
ideal infinite, unbroken canonical MPS, A1 is stronger: the remote endpoint is
exactly invisible once it lies beyond `W`, so the left side is eventually
zero.  The exponential bound remains the conservative finite-chain and broken-
sector laboratory extrapolation (`theory/corner-a-kinks.md:109-133`).

After absorbing the fixed `w` and constant into the tolerance, an endpoint
state is preparable to local accuracy `epsilon` with

`L >= w + ceil(log(C/epsilon)/|log lambda_tilde|)
   = O(log(1/epsilon))`.                                       (2.4)

If `D_W=d^|W|`, add a tomographic confidence radius
`epsilon_tom(M,D_W,delta)` to (2.3); full tomography is exponentially costly
in `|W|`, whereas estimating a fixed list of endpoint observables can scale as
`O(log(number of observables)/epsilon_tom^2)` with suitable randomized
measurements.  `[physical argument]` Pulse infidelity and imperfect initial
state preparation enter as additive trace-distance errors measured by controls
with `g=e` and with two inverse pulses.

**What the non-implementability theorem forbids.** When `V(g)` is nonscalar,
A1(b) proves that the natural vectors
`U_[0,L](g) Omega_A` are not Cauchy: the truncated strings have no strong
operator limit in the vacuum GNS representation.  Consequently there is, in
particular, no norm-convergent global unitary obtained by sending `L` to
infinity.  This forbids interpreting (2.2) as conjugation by a quasi-local
half-chain unitary.  It does *not* forbid any finite pulse (2.1), and it does
not forbid convergence of every expectation value in a fixed window.  The
latter weak/local content, quantified by (2.3), is precisely what the
experiment prepares and observes.

**What failure looks like.** If the trace distance between successive
`rho_W^(L,g)` does not fall at the fitted transfer rate after preparation and
tomography errors are removed, the assumed injective MPS, transfer gap,
on-site symmetry, or finite-pulse model has failed.  A vanishing endpoint
signal is not automatically failure: it is the correct result when `V(g)` is
scalar.  Conversely, claiming a globally converged half-chain unitary from
local convergence would be a category error even if every finite-window fit is
excellent.

## 3. The charge-history memory experiment

This experiment measures a two-time probability law.  It is not simulated by
subtracting two expectation values measured on unrelated, unmeasured copies.

**Preparation.** Prepare many identical copies of a localized kink state and,
when testing a scattering event, an incoming magnon wavepacket.  Choose a fixed
window `W=[a,b]` containing the kink core and a cut `c_0 in W`.  At the two
measurement times the core should be padded from both window edges and the
propagating packet should be outside `W` up to a measured leakage probability
`eta_pkt`.  Form the physical window charge

`Qhat_(W,c_0)=2s(X_W-c_0)
             =sum_(x=a)^b S_x^z+s(a+b-1-2c_0)`.                (3.1)

The scalar background in (3.1) is programmed once and must be identical at the
two measurement times.

**Operation.** At an early time `t_-`, before the packet reaches the kink,
perform a projective measurement of (3.1).  Record its outcome `q_-`; the
post-measurement state is retained.  Evolve that same copy with the physical
Hamiltonian through the collision.  At a late time `t_+`, after propagating
content has left the fixed window, projectively measure the *same* (3.1) and
record `q_+`.  Repeat the whole two-measurement history on fresh copies.

**Measurement apparatus.** `[physical argument]` A QND collective-magnetometry
pulse can resolve the eigenspaces of the sum in (3.1), provided its resolution
is finer than one charge unit and it does not resolve degeneracies within a
fixed total charge.  A quantum-gas microscope can measure every `S_x^z` and add
the results in software.  That is an immediate readout of the correct charge
eigenvalue and is sufficient for the final measurement.  At the *first*
measurement, however, direct site resolution is a refinement of the total-
charge PVM and generally causes extra back-action.  To realize exactly (3.2),
the microscope implementation must coherently sum into a meter and record only
the total (or otherwise erase which-site information before it becomes a
record).  Coarse-graining a stored site record afterwards does not recover the
Lüders instrument.  A deliberately refined first measurement is a different
TPM protocol: its integer support survives, but its history weights and
dephasing map must be reported separately.  In every platform the first
measurement must be nondestructive so that the measured copy can continue to
evolve.  A calibrated POVM followed by confusion-matrix inversion is
acceptable, but calling a noisy analog magnetization trace “projective” without
that calibration is not.

**Readout formula.** Let `Pi_q` be the spectral projector of (3.1), let
`U_(+,-)` be evolution from `t_-` to `t_+`, and let `rho_-` be the state just
before the first measurement.  The laboratory joint law is

`P_W(q_-,q_+)=Tr[Pi_(q_+) U_(+,-) Pi_(q_-) rho_- Pi_(q_-) U_(+,-)^dagger]`.

Bin it by escaped charge `nu:=q_--q_+=-Delta Q_W`:

`p_W(nu)=sum_q ||Pi_(q-nu)(t_+) Pi_q(t_-) Psi||^2`.             (3.2)

D26 implies

`spec Qhat_(W,c_0) subset kappa_(W,c_0)+Z`.

Because both measurements use the same `W,c_0` and background, the offset
cancels in every history:

`nu=(kappa+n_-)-(kappa+n_+)=n_--n_+ in Z`.                    (3.3)

Thus the raw signature is a normalized histogram with peaks on integers.  The
integer support is exact finite-window charge arithmetic, not evidence for a
particular scattering-channel inventory.

The same data give the kink displacement.  With
`Delta Q_W=q_+-q_-=-nu`,

`2s delta x = E[Delta Q_W] = -sum_(nu in Z) nu p(nu)`,

`delta x=-(1/(2s)) sum_(nu in Z) nu p(nu)`.                    (3.4)

Here `p` is an ordered large-time/large-window limit law when D27(LR) is used.
At finite time and fixed `W`, the right side of (3.4) defines the TPM ledger
estimator; it is not yet identified with D13's unmeasured `delta x`.  On a
separate unmeasured ensemble, microscopy gives the independent wall-coordinate
estimator

`delta x_direct=Tr[X_W(rho_+-rho_-)]`.                         (3.5)

The TPM first moment contains the dephased final charge,

`sum_nu nu p_W(nu)
 = <Qhat(t_-)>-<D_(t_-)(Qhat(t_+))>`,                          (3.6)

not in general the naive unmeasured difference.  D27(LR2) is exactly the
asymptotic condition under which the averaged dephasing defect in (3.6)
vanishes, allowing (3.4) and (3.5) to agree.  No commutativity of the two
Heisenberg observables is assumed.

**Error and convergence.** From `M` accepted histories,

`SE(delta x_TPM)=sqrt(Var_p(nu)/M)/(2s)`.                       (3.7)

Bootstrap (3.7) when the observed law has several integer channels.  If `d_W`
is the minimum core-to-edge distance, D13 supplies for every
`lambda_tilde in (lambda_E,1)` a spatial-tail error
`C lambda_tilde^d_W`.  `[physical argument]` For a packet whose escaped charge
magnitude is bounded by `q_max`, a practical total budget is

`|delta x_hat-delta x|
 <= sqrt(Var(nu)/M)/(2s)+C lambda_tilde^d_W
    +q_max eta_pkt/(2s)+epsilon_det+epsilon_relax`.            (3.8)

The campaign supplies no quantitative rate for the D27 time relaxation;
`epsilon_relax` must therefore be bounded empirically by increasing `|t_+-t_-|`
at fixed `W`, using the same Cesaro/time-averaging prescription if the signal
oscillates.  Only after that fixed-window test is stable may `W` be increased.
First-moment tightness must also be checked: probability leaking to larger and
larger integer `|nu|` can preserve normalization while destroying convergence
of (3.4).

Detector validation is performed before rounding.  Report the posterior mass
farther than the calibrated resolution from every integer, and repeat with
several window lengths; changing `W` between the first and second measurement
would replace (3.3) by two different offsets and can fake fractional escaped
charge.  The campaign's microscope-style numerics independently compared a
gradient-centroid wall estimator with the ledger and found agreement below
`0.1` sites (`theory/memory-index.md:780-805`); that is a protocol check, not a
proof of LR.

**What failure looks like.** Persistent off-integer mass after detector
deconvolution and same-window verification means that (INT) or the claimed
charge measurement has failed.  A persistent difference between (3.4) and
(3.5) as times and windows are increased is direct evidence against LR2 or
against packet/core isolation.  Loss of first-moment tightness is failure of
LR3.  By contrast, extra peaks at other *integers* are allowed and may signal
bound, absorbed, or additional propagating channels.  A noninteger value of the
mean `delta x` is also allowed: outcomes `nu` are quantized; their probability-
weighted mean generally is not.

## 4. Observing the soft phase slope `sgn(v_h-v_s)/S`

The observable is the derivative of the *physical outgoing/incoming phase*, not
the phase of an arbitrarily ordered Bethe coefficient.  Keep
`0<|k_h|<pi`, stay away from equal velocities, and select `S_phys` using the
actual velocity ordering, exactly as in D7--D8.

**Preparation.** In the fully polarized vacuum of

`H_S=-J sum_x(S_x dot S_(x+1)-S^2)`,

prepare two well-separated one-magnon packets.  The hard packet has momentum
distribution centered at `k_h` with width `sigma_h`; the soft packet has center
`k_s` and width `sigma_s`.  Put the faster packet behind the slower packet so
that one collision occurs, and choose the initial separation much larger than
both spatial widths.  Repeat with signed soft centers `+k_s` and `-k_s` while
keeping `sgn(v_h-v_s)` fixed.  Independently measure each packet's free
dispersion and group velocity before using the collision data.

**Operation.** Two equivalent physical-spin implementations are available.

1. In the position-shift implementation, evolve the two packets through the
   collision.  On reference copies evolve the soft packet freely, with the
   same preparation and duration but no overlapping hard packet.
2. In the Ramsey implementation, encode two branches in an addressable control
   spin and apply opposite momentum kicks so that the soft packet is centered
   at `k_s+Delta k/2` in one branch and `k_s-Delta k/2` in the other.  After the
   collision, undo the kicks and recombine the branches.  A no-collision run or
   spin echo subtracts the known free dynamical phase.

Both operations act on physical spins.  `[physical argument]` The Ramsey route
is preferable when outgoing packet centers overlap too strongly to locate a
small spatial shift; the position route avoids controlled many-body evolution.

**Measurement.** For the position route, measure the site-resolved outgoing
magnon density and fit its centroid only after the two packets are again
separated.  For the Ramsey route, measure the control spin in two quadratures
to obtain the complex visibility.  In both cases measure leakage into bound or
additional channels and reject data for which the regular two-magnon channel
does not dominate.

**Readout formula.** With the campaign's Fourier convention, multiplication of
a narrow outgoing packet by `exp(i delta_phys(k_s,k_h))` produces the spatial
shift

`Delta x_s=-partial_(k_s) delta_phys(k_s,k_h)`.                 (4.1)

Thus `-Delta x_s`, extrapolated to `k_s=0`, is the desired slope.  The Ramsey
central-difference estimator is

`a_hat(k_s,k_h)=unwrap[delta_hat(k_s+Delta k/2,k_h)
                        -delta_hat(k_s-Delta k/2,k_h)]/Delta k`. (4.2)

Average the estimates at signed `+k_s,-k_s` before extrapolating:

`a_hat_0=(a_hat(+k_s,k_h)+a_hat(-k_s,k_h))/2
       -> sgn(v_h-v_s)/S`.                                    (4.3)

The symmetric average removes the generic term linear in the chosen soft
center.  The theorem being observed is

`delta_phys(k_s,k_h)=sgn(v_h-v_s) k_s/S+O(k_s^2)`,

`partial_(k_s)delta_phys|_0=sgn(v_h-v_s)/S`

(`theory/spin-s-twomagnon.md:29-59`; current claim status `PROVED` at
`claims/CLAIMS.md:46`).

**Error and convergence.** On a compact hard-momentum set away from
`0,+/-pi`, the exact ratio has uniformly bounded derivatives.  For symmetric
momentum envelopes and the signed extrapolation in (4.3), `[physical argument]`
a useful error model is

`|a_hat_0-sgn(v_h-v_s)/S|
 <= C_s(k_s^2+sigma_s^2)+C_h sigma_h^2+C_fd(Delta k)^2
    +epsilon_sep+epsilon_chan+epsilon_phase/Delta k`.          (4.4)

The first two terms are packet averaging, the third is central differencing,
`epsilon_sep` is residual initial/final overlap or finite-time error,
`epsilon_chan` is measured leakage, and a Ramsey phase uncertainty is amplified
by `1/Delta k`.  Equation (4.4) exposes the experimental tradeoff: making
`sigma_s` small makes the packet long (`sigma_x sigma_s >= 1/2`), while making
`Delta k` too small magnifies phase noise.  Convergence therefore requires a
joint scan in packet width, separation, `Delta k`, and signed `k_s`; a single
small-momentum point is not a slope measurement.  The equal-velocity and
`k_h=0,+/-pi` endpoints are outside this budget.

Parts of this protocol are already mirrored by the campaign's own numerics.
The red-capable checker verifies the exact symbolic derivative, finite-momentum
grid values, and independent ring-Hamiltonian eigenvector residuals
(`theory/spin-s-twomagnon.md:353-375`).  The ring extrapolations in
`numerics/results/spin1-bc-falsifier.json` scan
`S=1/2,1,3/2,2` and find the respective `1/S` limits.  The independent
real-space collision calculation in
`numerics/results/spin1-bc-crosscheck.json` uses signed soft packets, spatial-
width extrapolation, and free references: at `k_h=1.5` it reports
`2.0093 +/- 0.0044` for `S=1/2`, `1.0026 +/- 0.0043` for `S=1`, and
`0.6650 +/- 0.0035` for `S=3/2`.  These calculations mirror preparation,
collision, free-arm comparison, and packet extrapolation.  They do not test
laboratory pulse noise, a physical Ramsey ancilla, equal velocities, endpoint
limits, generic many-body channels, or the separate memory conjecture.

**What failure looks like.** A fitted derivative that drifts under increasing
separation or decreasing momentum width is an unresolved packet/finite-time
effect.  A loss of Ramsey visibility or appreciable bound-channel weight makes
(4.2) undefined for the claimed elastic channel.  Failure to reverse the sign
when the velocity ordering is reversed identifies a channel-convention error.
If the extrapolated value remains inconsistent with `sgn(v_h-v_s)/S` after all
terms in (4.4) close and the Hamiltonian is independently verified, the exact
two-body model has been experimentally falsified.  None of these outcomes by
itself proves or disproves the campaign's broader `S-general` or memory claims.

## 5. `PROPOSED`: operational target for reconstruction theorem `tns-25h`

The missing reconstruction theorem should relate two already measurable
objects: a change of state on a *physical boundary algebra* and the physical
charge-history ledger.  “Bond data” is not an acceptable substitute for either
object.

**Preparation.** Choose a physical boundary or interface and a nested family
of boundary-spin windows `B_ell=[0,ell-1]`.  Prepare a localized incoming
excitation and an endpoint state.  Choose `t_-` before the excitation reaches
the boundary and `t_+` after all propagating density has left `B_ell` to a
measured tolerance.  Prepare three matched ensembles: one for tomography at
`t_-`, one for tomography at `t_+`, and one for the TPM ledger of §3.  If the
event is intended to leave permanent memory, repeat the late measurement over
an increasing waiting time rather than selecting a visually favorable time.

**Operation.** Evolve all ensembles with the same physical Hamiltonian through
the transit.  Apply no virtual insertion and perform no tensor-gauge choice.
On the TPM ensemble use the same `W,c_0` and background at both times.  On the
tomography ensembles, do not make the first TPM measurement: this preserves any
boundary coherence whose loss would otherwise be caused by the ledger
apparatus.

**Measurement.** Perform informationally complete physical-spin tomography on
`B_ell` before and after the transit and reconstruct

`rho_(partial,ell)^-=Tr_(complement B_ell) rho(t_-)`,

`rho_(partial,ell)^+=Tr_(complement B_ell) rho(t_+)`.           (5.1)

Measure, on those same boundary spins, the calibrated local boundary charge
`Q_ell^partial`, with its vacuum background fixed so that positive change means
charge gained by `B_ell`.  On the ledger ensemble measure `p_W(nu)` as in
(3.2).  A stronger version of the experiment performs late boundary tomography
conditioned on each recorded TPM outcome `nu`; those data can reconstruct a
charge-labelled boundary instrument rather than only an average state.

**Readout formula.** The field-side/boundary memory datum is the physical
state change

`M_(partial,ell):=rho_(partial,ell)^+-rho_(partial,ell)^-`,      (5.2)

equivalently the functional
`O -> Tr[M_(partial,ell)O]` on `A_(B_ell)`.  Report the full matrix with a
tomographic confidence region, the trace distance
`D_partial=(1/2)||M_(partial,ell)||_1`, and a fixed preregistered list of local
expectation changes.  These are basis-independent properties of a physical
reduced state; no virtual matrix `M`, `V`, or ansatz tensor is the readout.

The minimal candidate reconstruction identity is

`lim_(ell->infinity) Tr[Q_ell^partial M_(partial,ell)]
  ?= lim_(m->infinity) E[Delta Q_(W_m)]
   = 2s delta x
   = -sum_(nu in Z) nu p(nu)`.                                (5.3)

If `B_ell` is oriented as the complementary receiver of escaped charge, the
left side carries the opposite sign; that orientation must be fixed before
data collection.  Equation (5.3) is a **CONJECTURED identity**, not a theorem
in the campaign.  It is the smallest operational bridge from a boundary-state
change to the charge ledger.  Existence and sequence-independence of both
limits in (5.3) are part of the conjectured reconstruction statement; no
infinite-window charge operator is assumed.

The ledger `p(nu)` cannot by itself reconstruct every matrix element of
(5.2): charge-neutral rotations and coherences can change the boundary state
without changing its charge moment.  A genuine full reconstruction theorem
would therefore need additional, experimentally reconstructible
charge-conditioned completely positive maps `T_(ell,nu)` satisfying a limit
of the form

`rho_(partial,ell)^+ ?= sum_nu p(nu)
                            T_(ell,nu)(rho_(partial,ell)^-)`,    (5.4)

together with a stated charge-selection rule for each `T_(ell,nu)` and control
of coherences between charge sectors.  Equation (5.4) is a proposed theorem
target, not an identity smuggled in by defining the maps from the final state.
Conditional process tomography is what would make those maps independently
specified.

**Error and convergence.** Let the reported tomography regions obey
`||rho_hat^+-rho^+||_1<=epsilon_tom^+` and similarly at `t_-`.  For `M_TPM`
ledger histories, define the finite residual

`R_(ell,m):=|Tr[Q_ell^partial M_hat_(partial,ell)]
              -E_hat[Delta Q_(W_m)]|`.

`[physical argument]` A conservative acceptance budget is

`R_(ell,m) <= ||Q_ell^partial||
                 (epsilon_tom^++epsilon_tom^-)
              +sqrt(Var(Delta Q)/M_TPM)
              +C lambda_tilde^r+q_max eta_pkt+epsilon_relax`,  (5.5)

where `r` is the minimum padding/separation scale.  The transfer contribution
has the campaign rate for every `lambda_tilde in (lambda_E,1)`; the time-
relaxation term has no proved campaign rate and must be scanned; `q_max` and
`eta_pkt` are the packet-charge bound and measured leakage probability of §3.
Full tomography has dimension `d^ell` and rapidly becomes the dominant cost.
`[physical argument]` One should therefore use full tomography for the smallest
windows that contain the endpoint and randomized measurements for a fixed
nested operator set at larger `ell`; the latter tests convergence of the
functional but does not silently count as full-state tomography.

**What failure looks like.** A nonzero limiting residual in (5.5), after the
sign and backgrounds are checked, falsifies (5.3) for that preparation.  A
nonzero `M_partial` with a zero ledger identifies neutral or coherent boundary
memory that the scalar charge ledger does not reconstruct.  A nonzero ledger
with `M_partial` decaying to zero at late times means charge crossed the window
but no permanent boundary memory remained.  Agreement of the one charge moment
does not establish (5.4); failure of outcome-conditioned maps or unaccounted
coherences defeats full reconstruction even when (5.3) passes.  These are
scientifically distinct negative results, not reasons to rename a virtual bond
matrix as the missing observable.

## 6. Honest negative: the ansatz gauge matrix `X` is not observable

The specific object is the matrix `X in M_chi` in the ansatz null direction

`N_k(X)^s=exp(ik) A_alpha^s X-X A_beta^s`

and the replacement `B -> B+N_k(X)` of D5(b).  I cannot describe a
quantum-mechanical experiment that measures this `X`.

**Preparation.** There is no preparation that physically singles out `X`.
One may prepare the excitation represented by `B`, but the gauge-related tensor
`B+N_k(X)` represents the same infinite-volume generalized physical vector in
the D12 limits.  Changing the MPS tensor gauge changes the matrix coordinates
again without changing the spin state.

**Operation.** The replacement `B -> B+N_k(X)` is not a unitary, channel,
quench, or measurement on the spin chain.  It is a change of coordinates in an
ansatz.  Therefore there is no physical “apply `X`” branch to compare with an
unshifted branch.

**Measurement.** Every projective measurement or POVM on the physical spins
depends only on the resulting density operator.  Gauge-related representatives
give the same probabilities in the register where the null direction vanishes.
Consequently no physical-spin POVM can distinguish two assigned values of
`X` on that orbit.

**Readout formula — impossibility.** If an estimator `F` extracted `X` from
physical outcome probabilities, gauge equivalence would require both

`F[rho_(Phi(B))]=0`  and
`F[rho_(Phi(B+N_k(X)))]=X`,

while the two density operators are equal.  For nonzero `X` this is a direct
contradiction.  Equivalently, the quantum Fisher information along this gauge
direction is zero.  Only gauge-invariant functions of the physical excitation,
or an explicitly fixed numerical gauge convention, can be read out.

**Error and convergence.** This is exact non-identifiability, not a small
signal with an unfavorable error bar.  On a finite window the formal null
direction leaves two boundary terms
`exp(ik(b+1))|X@(b|b+1)>-exp(ika)|X@(a-1|a)>`
(`definitions.md:324-339`).  Measuring those terms would measure the chosen
finite boundary vectors and truncation convention.  They disappear only in the
declared D12 limits and cannot be used to reconstruct a unique infinite-volume
`X`.  Increasing the shot count therefore does not produce a consistent
estimator for the gauge datum.

**What failure looks like.** Any claimed `X` readout changes when the tensor
gauge, boundary vectors, or finite-window convention is changed while all
physical-spin predictions remain fixed.  That dependence is the diagnostic
that the apparatus has measured scaffolding rather than physics.

Per the campaign directive, this `X` is **not operationally meaningful**.  It
may remain useful as calculational scaffolding for quotienting the excitation
ansatz, but it must not be described as an observable, a memory record, or an
experimental endpoint datum.
