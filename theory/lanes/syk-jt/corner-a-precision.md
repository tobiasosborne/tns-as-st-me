# Corner A in two-sided SYK/JT: the symmetry, charges, and operational gaps

This memo is source-limited and creates no campaign claim.  Its convention is
that an object is not called a charge merely because it is a coordinate on the
Schwarzian saddle: a charge must act on a named algebra of allowed observables.
The local sources themselves motivate this caution.  Maldacena--Stanford--Yang
(MSY) call the boundary map the field variable
(`refs/arxiv-1606.01857/GravityReparametrizationsRevised.tex:497-502`), whereas
Penington--Witten (PW) construct their physical operator algebra only after the
diagonal isometry constraints have been imposed
(`refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:481-515,538-577`).

The source identities were re-verified internally: *Conformal symmetry and
its breaking in two dimensional Nearly Anti-de-Sitter space*, by Juan
Maldacena, Douglas Stanford, and Zhenbin Yang
(`refs/arxiv-1606.01857/GravityReparametrizationsRevised.tex:139-158`);
*Algebras and States in JT Gravity*, by Geoff Penington and Edward Witten
(`refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:211-222`); and *Causal connectability
between quantum systems and the black hole interior in holographic duality*,
by Samuel Leutheusser and Hong Liu
(`refs/arxiv-2110.05497/causal_connectability_PRD.tex:323-344`).

## 0. Objects and operational predicates

### 0.1 The three observable layers

Let \(X\in\{L,R\}\) label the two SYK copies, let
\(\psi_i^X=(\psi_i^X)^*\), \(i=1,\ldots,N\), be their Majorana generators,
let \(H_{X,N}\) be copy \(X\)'s microscopic Hamiltonian, and let
\(\mathcal H_N\) be the finite-\(N\) two-copy Hilbert space.  Define

\[
 \mathfrak A_N:=\operatorname{CAR}
 (\psi_1^L,\ldots,\psi_N^L,\psi_1^R,\ldots,\psi_N^R)^{\rm even}.
\tag{0.1}
\]

Here “even” means fixed by total fermion parity.  Thus \(\mathfrak A_N\) is a
finite-dimensional type-I C*-algebra; after fixing a parity superselection
sector it is a matrix algebra.  This is **Layer 0**.  Its observables are
self-adjoint elements, its detector effects are elements \(0\le E\le1\), and
an operation is **implementable** when it is a finite-\(N\) preparation,
Hamiltonian evolution, compact source, or POVM made from such effects.  These
are the bedrock objects specified in
`theory/lanes/syk-jt/observables-spec.md:20-30`.

For boundary times \(u,v\), define
\(\psi_i^X(u):=e^{iuH_{X,N}}\psi_i^Xe^{-iuH_{X,N}}\).  For a smooth
compactly supported test function \(f\) of \((u,v)\), define the normalized
bilocal sequence

\[
 B^{XY}_N(f):={1\over N}\sum_{i=1}^N\int du\,dv\,
 f(u,v)\,\psi_i^X(u)\psi_i^Y(v),
\tag{0.2}
\]

with adjoints and all operator orderings included.  Define
\(\mathfrak A_{\rm biloc}^{(1)}\), **Layer 1**, to be the algebraic register of
finite products and linear combinations of the sequences (0.2), together
with bounded functional calculus of the two microscopic Hamiltonians
\(H_{L,N},H_{R,N}\).  A *Layer-1 limit observable* means a sequence of Layer-0
measurements whose joint moments converge in the stated thermofield-double
(TFD) protocols; the limiting symbol is shorthand for those finite-\(N\)
statistics, not a new detector.  This is the operational meaning fixed at
`theory/lanes/syk-jt/observables-spec.md:32-40`.  The Hamiltonians themselves
need not be uniformly bounded: at this layer they are specified by bounded
functions such as \(e^{isH_{X,N}}\) and by their spectral POVMs.
For \(X=L,R\), define \(\mathfrak A_X^{(1)}\) to be the one-sided
subalgebra of \(\mathfrak A_{\rm biloc}^{(1)}\) generated by sequences
whose copy labels are all \(X\), together with bounded functions of
\(H_{X,N}\).

Two different weak closures occur in the sources and will not be conflated.
Fix an inverse temperature \(\beta>0\).  Let \(\omega_\beta\) be the
large-\(N\) thermal functional on the one-sided
single-trace/bilocal algebra, let
\((\pi_\beta,\mathcal H_\beta,\Omega_\beta)\) be its GNS representation, and
define

\[
 \mathcal M_R^{\rm LL}:=\pi_\beta(\mathfrak A_R^{(1)})'',\qquad
 \mathcal M_L^{\rm LL}:=(\mathcal M_R^{\rm LL})'.
\tag{0.3}
\]

Let \(B(\mathcal H_\beta)\) denote all bounded operators on
\(\mathcal H_\beta\).  For any operator algebra
\(\mathcal N\subset B(\mathcal H_\beta)\), the prime \(\mathcal N'\) denotes
its commutant in \(B(\mathcal H_\beta)\).

These are the **Layer-2 LL algebras**.  Leutheusser--Liu (LL) define the
large-\(N\) algebra and its GNS representation at
`refs/arxiv-2110.05497/causal_connectability_PRD.tex:663-676`; importantly,
they *conjecture*, rather than prove there, that the two factors are type
\(\mathrm{III}_1\) (`refs/arxiv-2110.05497/causal_connectability_PRD.tex:685-697`).  Their short
summary is that a “type \(\mathrm{III}_1\) von Neumann algebraic structure”
emerges from the finite-\(N\) type-I algebra
(`refs/arxiv-2110.05497/causal_connectability_PRD.tex:425-427`).

Independently, let \(\mathcal H_{\rm JT}\) be PW's physical two-sided JT
Hilbert space after the diagonal \(\widetilde{SL}(2,\mathbb R)\) constraint,
and likewise let \(B(\mathcal H_{\rm JT})\) denote all bounded operators on that
Hilbert space.  Define
\(\mathcal A_X^{\rm JT}\subset B(\mathcal H_{\rm JT})\) to be the
von Neumann algebra generated by bounded functions of the boundary
Hamiltonian \(H_X\) and bounded functions of suitably smeared, gauge-invariant
matter insertions \(\Phi_X(t)\).  PW spell out these generators and the
required smearing at `refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:817-836`.  Define the
two-sided physical algebra

\[
 \mathcal A_{LR}^{\rm JT}:=\mathcal A_L^{\rm JT}\vee
 \mathcal A_R^{\rm JT}.
\tag{0.4}
\]

Here \(\vee\) means the von Neumann algebra generated by the union of its two
arguments.

With matter and PW's stated regularity assumptions,
\(\mathcal A_X^{\rm JT}\) is a type-\(\mathrm{II}_\infty\) factor and the two
sides are mutual commutants; without matter both sides coincide with the
commutative algebra generated by \(H=H_L=H_R\)
(`refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:220-222,748-769,838-852,1233-1240`).  PW's crossed-product
statement is only a high-energy identification: there the boundary algebra
becomes the crossed product of a type-\(\mathrm{III}_1\) bulk-QFT wedge algebra
by modular flow (`refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:907-914`).  It is not, in this source, an
isomorphism between (0.3) and the full background-independent JT algebra.

### 0.2 Observable, affiliated generator, and trivial action

An object is **OBSERVABLE at Layer 0** if it is in \(\mathfrak A_N\) (or is a
spectral outcome of a self-adjoint element of it).  It is **OBSERVABLE at
Layer 1** if it is a limit-observable sequence in the sense above.  A
self-adjoint operator \(Q\) **exists at Layer 2 as a charge for a von Neumann
algebra \(\mathcal N\)** when:

1. \(Q\) is affiliated with \(\mathcal N\), meaning every spectral projection
   of \(Q\) lies in \(\mathcal N\), equivalently
   \(e^{isQ}\in\mathcal N\) for every real \(s\);
2. the unitaries \(e^{isQ}\) generate a strongly continuous automorphism
   group \(\alpha_s(A)=e^{isQ}Ae^{-isQ}\) of \(\mathcal N\); and
3. there are a specified strongly dense *-subalgebra
   \(\mathcal D\subset\mathcal N\) and a dense Hilbert-space core
   \(\mathscr C\subset\operatorname{Dom}Q\) such that
   \(A\mathscr C\subset\mathscr C\) for every \(A\in\mathcal D\), and the
   quadratic-form commutator \(\delta_Q(A)=i[Q,A]\) on \(\mathscr C\) has a
   stated weak or strong closure.

The pair \((\mathcal D,\mathscr C)\) is the *commutator register*.  Stating a
formal commutator without both parts does not establish an unbounded
Layer-2 charge.  A **Layer-1 realization** of the same charge additionally
requires parity-even self-adjoint \(Q_N\in\mathfrak A_N\), or their bounded
unitaries/resolvents, whose conjugation of every chosen bilocal sequence and
whose Ward matrix elements converge to those of \(Q\).  **GAUGE-bookkeeping**
means a variable or generator used before constraint reduction that has not
been shown to satisfy either observable criterion.

For a group \(G\) acting by automorphisms \(\alpha_g\) on a named algebra
\(\mathcal N\), define its trivially acting subgroup by

\[
 K(\mathcal N):=\{g\in G:\alpha_g(A)=A\ \hbox{for every }A\in\mathcal N\},
\tag{0.5}
\]

and define the effective symmetry to be \(G/K(\mathcal N)\).  Thus “modulo
trivial transformations” is meaningless until both \(\mathcal N\) and the
action have been supplied.  Two states \(\omega_1,\omega_2\) are *separated by
\(\mathcal N\)* when some \(A\in\mathcal N\) has
\(\omega_1(A)\ne\omega_2(A)\).

## 1. The group

### 1.1 One Euclidean circle and two Lorentzian boundary curves

Define \(\operatorname{Diff}_+(S^1)\) as the group of smooth lifts
\(f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) with \(f'(\tau)>0\) and
\(f(\tau+2\pi)=f(\tau)+2\pi\), modulo integer \(2\pi\)-translations.  In the
one-boundary Euclidean disk preparing the thermal state, the Schwarzian field
is such an \(f\), or equivalently the projective coordinate
\(t(\tau)=\tan(f(\tau)/2)\).  The Möbius group
\(PSL(2,\mathbb R):=SL(2,\mathbb R)/\{\pm I\}\) acts on \(t\) by

\[
 t\longmapsto {at+b\over ct+d},\qquad a,b,c,d\in\mathbb R,\quad ad-bc=1.
\tag{1.1}
\]

MSY state both that the asymptotic symmetries are “all the time
reparametrizations of the boundary”
(`refs/arxiv-1606.01857/GravityReparametrizationsRevised.tex:190-195`) and that
Möbius-related \(t(\tau)\)'s give the same cutout geometry
(`refs/arxiv-1606.01857/GravityReparametrizationsRevised.tex:367-382`).  Therefore the Euclidean
soft *configuration space*, not yet an operational symmetry group, is

\[
 \mathcal C_E=\operatorname{Diff}_+(S^1)/PSL(2,\mathbb R).
\tag{1.2}
\]

MSY identify its coordinates as Goldstone-like zero modes of the breaking to
\(SL(2,\mathbb R)\) (`refs/arxiv-1606.01857/GravityReparametrizationsRevised.tex:393-406`).  Away from
the conformal limit the Schwarzian action explicitly lifts the non-Möbius
directions (`refs/arxiv-1606.01857/GravityReparametrizationsRevised.tex:490-508`), so (1.2) alone
does not provide conserved charges.

For Lorentzian signature, let \(u_X\in\mathbb R\) be the proper time of boundary
\(X\), let \(T\in\mathbb R\) be global-cover \(AdS_2\) time, and define a
boundary curve by an orientation-preserving map \(T_X(u_X)\), together with
the radial variable fixed by the boundary condition.  PW explicitly describe
the two curves by separate functions \(T_L(t),T_R(t)\)
(`refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:388-400,461-472`).  Define
\(\operatorname{Diff}_+(\mathbb R)\) to be the group of smooth
orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms of the real line.  The maximal *kinematic
candidate* is consequently **[physical argument]**

\[
 \mathcal G_{\rm kin}^{\rm Lor}
 =\operatorname{Diff}_+(\mathbb R)_L\times
  \operatorname{Diff}_+(\mathbb R)_R,
\tag{1.3}
\]

subject to the causal and falloff conditions needed for both curves to bound
one spacetime.  The named sources do not specify those falloffs well enough to
turn (1.3) into a completed asymptotic-symmetry group; this is Gap G1 below.
**[physical argument]** The Euclidean TFD preparation uses one circle, whereas its Lorentzian
continuation has two boundary curves.  Squaring
\(\operatorname{Diff}_+(S^1)\) in Euclidean signature would therefore double
the preparation data and is not licensed by these sources.

### 1.2 What is gauge, and on which algebra

Let \(G_0:=\widetilde{SL}(2,\mathbb R)\), the universal cover of the \(AdS_2\)
isometry group, and let \(G_{0,\mathrm{diag}}\) act simultaneously on the two
curves.  PW require configurations related by this common action to be
equivalent and impose the three total constraints

\[
 J_a=J_a^L+J_a^R+J_a^{\rm matt}=0,
 \qquad a=1,2,3,
\tag{1.4}
\]

where \(J_a^{\rm matt}\) denotes the matter-sector isometry generator and is
omitted in pure JT
(`refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:481-515,636-653`).  On states, the physical
Hilbert space is the coinvariant space, on which \(G_0\) acts trivially
(`refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:538-577`).  On operators, PW say that imposing the constraint
means “restricting to operators that commute with the group of constraints”
(`refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:751-760`).  Thus
\(G_{0,\mathrm{diag}}\subset K(\mathcal A_{LR}^{\rm JT})\) by construction.
This is the precise sense in
which diagonal \(SL(2)\), and not each side's \(SL(2)\) separately, is gauge.

A right-only or left-only Möbius transformation is a *relative* direction.
It is not removed by (1.4): classically the reduced two-sided phase space is
parametrized by the common energy and a relative timeshift
(`refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:535-536`).  At the Schwarzian level a common
Möbius map leaves every invariant bilocal unchanged, whereas a one-sided map
generically changes a mixed bilocal
(`theory/lanes/syk-jt/t1-analytic.md:37-52,267-276`).  Consequently:

* \(G_{0,\mathrm{diag}}\) is **GAUGE** on the physical algebra (0.4);
* relative \(G_0\) data are **physical relational data** when matter is
  present and a two-sided correlator in (0.4) separates them, although the
  coordinate label itself is not an observable; and
* **[physical argument]** no source makes all of diagonal
  \(\operatorname{Diff}_+(\mathbb R)\) gauge.  In fact a general common
  reparametrization is not Möbius and the Schwarzian/matter kernels need not be
  invariant.  Beyond \(G_0\), an automorphic action on (0.3) or (0.4) still has
  to be constructed.

This also locates the shifted-TFD statement.  Let
\(|\mathrm{TFD}\rangle\in\mathcal H_N\) denote the normalized two-copy
thermofield-double state at inverse temperature \(\beta\), and define

\[
 |\mathrm{TFD};s\rangle:=e^{-isH_L}|\mathrm{TFD}\rangle .
\tag{1.5}
\]

Every right observable has the same expectation in (1.5) as at \(s=0\), since
the left and right algebras commute.  LL prove this more generally for
a Hermitian left-algebra operator \(A_L\) and the state
\(e^{iA_L}|\mathrm{TFD}\rangle\) at
`refs/arxiv-2110.05497/causal_connectability_PRD.tex:461-473`.  For the
Hamiltonian choice in (1.5), the left reduced thermal state is also stationary,
so neither one-sided algebra separates the family.  Mixed two-sided
correlators can: LL explain that the relevant future measurement must involve
both sides (`refs/arxiv-2110.05497/causal_connectability_PRD.tex:477-481`).
Define \(G_{LR}(u_L,u_R):=\langle\Phi_L(u_L)\Phi_R(u_R)\rangle\); this explicit
Schwarzian mixed correlator changes under a relative Möbius map
(`theory/lanes/syk-jt/t1-analytic.md:387-414`).  Hence the timeshift is not an
extra one-sided charge; it is relational information in the joint algebra.

### 1.3 Layer verdict for the group

At Layer 0, a fixed finite-\(N\) SYK Hamiltonian supplies exact time evolution
and fermion parity, but the named sources do not construct an exact
\(\operatorname{Diff}\) action on \(\mathfrak A_N\).  At Layer 1,
**[physical argument]** reparametrization covariance of bilocal correlation functions is an emergent
low-energy rule, not yet a sequence of microscopic automorphisms.  At Layer 2,
PW rigorously impose only the diagonal \(G_0\) constraint and construct the
gauge-invariant algebra; LL construct the GNS algebra but do not identify a
full reparametrization automorphism group on it.  Therefore the source-backed
effective group is presently the broken-orbit geometry (1.2), with diagonal
\(G_0\) gauge and relational \(G_0\) directions.  Constructing an action of
\(\mathcal G_{\rm kin}^{\rm Lor}\), determining its kernel, and only then
forming the effective quotient is a problem, not a result.

## 2. The charges

### 2.1 Generators and the classical Schwarzian ledger

Let \(\epsilon\) and \(\eta\) be smooth real functions of boundary target time and let
\(v_\epsilon:=\epsilon(t)\partial_t\) be its vector field.  Define the vector
field bracket by

\[
 [\epsilon,\eta]_{\rm vf}:=\epsilon\eta'-\eta\epsilon'.
\tag{2.1}
\]

On the projective line, the three-dimensional Möbius subalgebra is the span of
\(\epsilon_-=1\), \(\epsilon_0=t\), and \(\epsilon_+=t^2\).  On the thermal
circle the same real subalgebra is the span of \(1,\cos\tau,\sin\tau\), as is
also visible in PW's action on boundary time
(`refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:688-700`).  All other smooth \(\epsilon\)'s
will be called *non-Möbius directions*.

Let \(\bar\phi_r\) be the renormalized boundary dilaton and \(G_N\) the
two-dimensional Newton constant.  Define the Schwarzian coupling
\(C:=\bar\phi_r/(8\pi G_N)>0\), let
\(t=t(u)\) be a monotone Lorentzian boundary map, and let primes denote
\(u\)-derivatives.  MSY define the three classical one-side Noether functions

\[
\begin{aligned}
 Q^-&=C\left({t'''\over t'^2}-{t''^2\over t'^3}\right),\\
 Q^0&=C\left({t t'''\over t'^2}-{t t''^2\over t'^3}-{t''\over t'}\right),\\
 Q^+&=C\left({t^2t'''\over t'^2}-{t^2t''^2\over t'^3}
                    -{2tt''\over t'}+2t'\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}
\]

The source gives (2.2) at
`refs/arxiv-1606.01857/GravityReparametrizationsRevised.tex:885-901` and calls
them the charges of the global \(SL(2)\) symmetry.  It also defines the
boundary-time Hamiltonian

\[
 H={-Q^+Q^-+(Q^0)^2\over 2C},
\tag{2.3}
\]

the quadratic Casimir of the one-side triple
(`refs/arxiv-1606.01857/GravityReparametrizationsRevised.tex:921-929`).  Thus \(H\), which generates
translations of the physical time \(u\), is not a fourth target-time Diff mode:
it is a separate Noether generator expressible as the \(SL(2)\) Casimir.
For the two-sided TFD, define \(Q_X^a\) by applying (2.2) to boundary \(X\).
MSY impose \(Q_L^a=-Q_R^a\), so the diagonal total can vanish although either
one-side triple is nonzero
(`refs/arxiv-1606.01857/GravityReparametrizationsRevised.tex:902-909`).

For matter, define \(q_M^a\) to be its \(AdS_2\)-isometry charge and define the
total diagonal charge \(Q_T^a:=Q^a[t]+q_M^a\).  MSY state this soft/matter split
and the corresponding flux equation at
`refs/arxiv-1606.01857/GravityReparametrizationsRevised.tex:955-972`.  There is a sign warning in the
source itself (`refs/arxiv-1606.01857/GravityReparametrizationsRevised.tex:964-965`).  With the
equation-of-motion convention used in the analytic lane, the unambiguous
integrated ledger is as follows.  Let \([u_i,u_f]\) be the compact support
interval of the source and let \(T_{tz}\) be its boundary matter-flux
component in the MSY coordinates.  Let \(Q_i^a\) and \(Q_f^a\) be the values
of (2.2) immediately before and after that interval; then

\[
 Q_f^a-Q_i^a=-\mathcal J^a,\qquad
 \mathcal J^a:=\int_{u_i}^{u_f}du\;t'T_{tz}(1,t,t^2)^a,
\tag{2.4}
\]

derived directly from (2.2) at
`theory/lanes/syk-jt/t1-analytic.md:73-118`.  Equation (2.4) is a classical
Schwarzian Ward ledger.  It becomes an operational charge law only if both
sides are represented by allowed observables at one of the layers in section
0.

### 2.2 What PW's quantization actually contains

Before reduction, PW's boundary phase space has canonical coordinates
\((T_X,\chi_X)\) and momenta \((p_{T_X},p_{\chi_X})\).  Here \(\chi_X\) is the
renormalized radial/length coordinate defined in their Hamiltonian reduction
(`refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:451-456`).  They construct self-adjoint
operators \(J_a^X\) on the unreduced Hilbert space and derive

\[
 [J_a^X,J_b^X]=i\epsilon_{ab}{}^cJ_c^X,
\tag{2.5}
\]

with \(\epsilon_{123}=1\) and Lie-algebra metric
\(\eta=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1)\)
(`refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:486-505`).  The boundary Hamiltonian is an
affine quadratic Casimir of this triple (`refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:502-505`).  With matter,
the constrained generator is exactly the sum in (1.4).

This construction does **not** put the individual \(J_a^X\) in the physical
boundary algebra.  The total \(J_a\) acts trivially on the coinvariant Hilbert
space (`refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:602-610`), while \(J_a^R\) or \(J_a^L\) alone is not invariant
under the diagonal nonabelian constraint.  By contrast, the Casimir descends:
PW obtain positive physical boundary Hamiltonians, equal in pure JT and
commuting in JT plus matter
(`refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:612-630,662-673`).  In pure JT the conclusion is especially sharp:
the only gauge-invariant one-boundary operators are functions of \(H_X\)
(`refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:751-769`).

Accordingly, PW contain:

* three self-adjoint \(SL(2)\) **constraint generators before reduction**;
* their ordinary \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) algebra (2.5);
* the gauge-invariant boundary Hamiltonian \(H_X\), an unbounded self-adjoint
  operator affiliated with \(\mathcal A_X^{\rm JT}\); and
* with matter, gauge-invariant smeared boundary fields \(\Phi_X(t)\), but no
  displayed \(Q[\epsilon]\) for a non-Möbius \(\epsilon\).

The affiliation statement for \(H_X\) follows from PW's definition of the
von Neumann algebra using \(e^{itH_X}\) and \(e^{-\beta H_X}\); their warning
that the fields themselves are operator-valued distributions and require
smearing is at `refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:817-835`.  It is therefore incorrect to say that
PW's type-\(\mathrm{II}_\infty\) algebra contains three physical \(SL(2)\)
charges.  It contains the bounded functional calculus of their physical
Casimir; the three components are GAUGE-bookkeeping on the unreduced space.

### 2.3 Existence, layer by layer

The following inventory uses “exists” in the sense of section 0.2.

| generator | Layer 0: finite-\(N\) even CAR | Layer 1: bilocal sequences | Layer 2: LL/PW |
|---|---|---|---|
| \(H_L,H_R\), physical-time translations | **EXISTS.** They are parity-even microscopic observables; their spectral measurements and unitaries are allowed. | **EXISTS.** Bounded functions and energy statistics are part of the layer by definition (`theory/lanes/syk-jt/observables-spec.md:32-37,63-67`). | **EXISTS.** \(H_X\) is affiliated with \(\mathcal A_X^{\rm JT}\).  LL instead use \(H_R-H_L\) to describe modular flow of \(\mathcal M_R^{\rm LL}\) (`refs/arxiv-2110.05497/causal_connectability_PRD.tex:887-891`); that does not show that either \(H_X\) is affiliated with the type-III one-sided algebra. |
| \(\epsilon\in\operatorname{span}\{1,t,t^2\}\), the three target-time Möbius directions | **NOT EXHIBITED as charges.** No parity-even finite-\(N\) proxy for \(Q^-,Q^0,Q^+\), other than the energy Casimir, is supplied. | **GAUGE-bookkeeping.** Equations (2.2)--(2.4) control the EFT saddle, but the collective map (t(u)) and the three components are explicitly excluded from the allowed-observable list (`theory/lanes/syk-jt/observables-spec.md:69-77`). | **EXISTS only before reduction as PW's \(J_a^X\); not as individual physical observables.** The total is a constraint, while \(H_X\) descends. |
| non-Möbius \(\epsilon\) | **ABSENT from the named sources.** **[physical argument]** The finite-coupling microscopic Hamiltonian explicitly breaks the conformal reparametrization rule. | **CORRELATOR ACTION ONLY.** Reparametrized bilocal kernels are controlled in the Schwarzian regime, but no convergent \(Q_N[\epsilon]\) or unitary implementer is given. | **ABSENT from the named constructions.** Neither \(\mathcal M_X^{\rm LL}\) nor \(\mathcal A_X^{\rm JT}\) is supplied with such affiliated generators. |

The difference between “correlator action” and “charge” is essential.  A
formula telling how a saddle reparametrizes \(G(u_1,u_2)\) defines a derivation
on a collection of correlation functions.  It does not by itself prove that
the derivation is closable, is spatially implemented by a self-adjoint
operator, preserves the chosen von Neumann algebra, or is the limit of
finite-\(N\) even observables.

The conclusion for the question “for which \(\epsilon\) does \(Q[\epsilon]\)
exist?” is therefore intentionally austere.  The physical-time generator
\(H_X\) exists operationally.  The three Möbius generators exist as
self-adjoint constraint operators before gauge reduction and as classical
Schwarzian ledger variables, but only their Casimir is presently in the
physical one-boundary algebra.  No non-Möbius \(Q[\epsilon]\) satisfying the
Layer-1 or Layer-2 definition is constructed in the named sources.

## 3. Algebra and extension

### 3.1 The algebra that is actually derived

Equation (2.5) is the only nonabelian charge bracket derived in the named
quantum source.  Generators on opposite unreduced boundaries commute, the
matter generators obey the same \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) relations, and their sum is
the diagonal constraint; PW give these ingredients at
`refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:486-505,636-673`.  On the physical algebra,
the total constraint has zero action.  The surviving Hamiltonian generates
the one-parameter automorphism

\[
 \alpha_t^X(A):=e^{itH_X}Ae^{-itH_X},\qquad A\in\mathcal A_X^{\rm JT},
\tag{3.1}
\]

and obeys \(\Phi_X(t)=\alpha_t^X(\Phi_X(0))\)
(`refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:817-826`).  Thus PW derive the ordinary finite-dimensional
\(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) constraint algebra and the physical time-translation
automorphism.  They do not derive a physical Witt/Virasoro charge algebra.

### 3.2 Where an extension could occur

Suppose, as an additional hypothesis, that every smooth \(\epsilon\) has a
self-adjoint charge \(Q[\epsilon]\), that all charges share a common invariant
Hilbert-space core \(\mathscr C\), and that their commutators act on a common
operator test algebra \(\mathcal D\).  A *central extension* of the
vector-field algebra means

\[
 [Q[\epsilon],Q[\eta]]\big|_{\mathscr C}
 =iQ[[\epsilon,\eta]_{\rm vf}]\big|_{\mathscr C}
  +iK(\epsilon,\eta)\mathbf 1\big|_{\mathscr C},
\tag{3.2}
\]

where \(K\) is a real antisymmetric Lie-algebra 2-cocycle and
\(\mathbf1\) is the identity operator.  Equivalently, exponentiated
implementers may multiply projectively.  The central term is not a term in the
Schwarzian action and cannot be read off merely from its coefficient \(C\); it
must be derived from the representation, its domains, and a normalization of
the charges.

MSY explicitly leave this open: “mentioned neither the algebra nor the central
charge” (`refs/arxiv-1606.01857/GravityReparametrizationsRevised.tex:1167-1172`).
Their nearby remark distinguishes the finite \(SL(2)\) charges present in two
dimensions from an infinite higher-dimensional family
(`refs/arxiv-1606.01857/GravityReparametrizationsRevised.tex:1160-1164`).  PW do not close the open
question.  They reformulate the quantum theory in terms of gauge-invariant
boundary algebras, within which the proposed non-Möbius \(Q[\epsilon]\)'s are
not among the generators.  This removes unphysical diagonal \(SL(2)\)
components; it neither constructs (3.2) nor proves \(K=0\).  The absence of a
Diff charge algebra from PW is therefore an unresolved antecedent, not a
vanishing-extension theorem.

### 3.3 Exact contrast with the one-dimensional chain

For the campaign's chain corner A, let \(G\) be the compact on-site internal
symmetry group, let \(V_\alpha(g)\) be its projective virtual implementer in a
fixed unbroken vacuum \(\alpha\), and define

\[
 N_\alpha:=\{g\in G:V_\alpha(g)\in\mathbb C^\times\mathbf1\},\qquad
 \mathcal A_{\rm eff}:=G/N_\alpha.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Here \(\mathbb C^\times\) is the multiplicative group of nonzero complex
numbers.

The group (3.3) is the effective state-level asymptotic symmetry, because
\(N_\alpha\) is exactly the trivially acting kernel
(`definitions.md:269-286`).  For \(g,h\in G\), let
\(\omega_\alpha(h,g)\) be a real phase modulo \(2\pi\).  If

\[
 V_\alpha(h)V_\alpha(g)=e^{i\omega_\alpha(h,g)}V_\alpha(hg),
\tag{3.4}
\]

then \(\omega_\alpha\) is the defined group 2-cocycle and its twisted group
algebra is the charge algebra on padded endpoint registers.  The campaign has
proved that representation and identified the state-level quotient; it also
states precisely why the global projective class need not appear as a
Lie-algebra central charge (`definitions.md:257-305`).  This is the sense in
which the chain's extension question is settled in its stated register.
This does not promote the endpoint generators to affiliated operators in a
GNS algebra: that realization remains a sketch
(`theory/lanes/reduction/r2r4-ward.md:173-180`).

The finiteness difference is geometric, not a discrepancy in counting.  At a
fixed time, spatial infinity of a (1+1)-dimensional chain is
\(S^0=\{-,+\}\).  Hence an asymptotic internal transformation is a map
\(S^0\to G\), namely a pair \((g_L,g_R)\in G_L\times G_R\), where
\(G_L\) and \(G_R\) are copies of \(G\) assigned to the two spatial ends.
Removal of the
common action leaves finite-dimensional relative data.  This reduction is
stated at `theory/lanes/reduction/r2r4-ward.md:169-180`.  In JT, by contrast,
the asymptotic coordinate being transformed is boundary *time*: an element is
a whole function \(\epsilon(\tau)\) on \(S^1\), or \(\epsilon(u)\) on the
Lorentzian line.  The resulting function algebra is infinite-dimensional.
Moreover, the chain extension (3.4) is a microscopic endpoint/SPT multiplier,
whereas the missing JT extension (3.2) would be a central extension of a
time-reparametrization charge algebra.  One cannot infer either from the
other.

## 4. Gap list as mathematical problems

### 4.1 Common test objects for the problems

For \(n\ge1\), define real circle vector fields

\[
 \epsilon_{n,c}(\tau):=\cos(n\tau),\qquad
 \epsilon_{n,s}(\tau):=\sin(n\tau),
\tag{4.1}
\]

and define \(\epsilon_0(\tau):=1\).  The modes with \(n=0,1\) span the Möbius
subalgebra; those with \(n\ge2\) are non-Möbius.  For a primary bilocal
\(B_\Delta(u,v)\) of scaling dimension \(\Delta>0\), define its infinitesimal
reparametrization by

\[
 \delta_\epsilon^{\rm biloc}B_\Delta(u,v)
 :=\left[\epsilon(u)\partial_u+\epsilon(v)\partial_v
 +\Delta\epsilon'(u)+\Delta\epsilon'(v)\right]B_\Delta(u,v).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Equation (4.2) is the infinitesimal form of the reparametrized matter kernel
derived by MSY at
`refs/arxiv-1606.01857/GravityReparametrizationsRevised.tex:645-675`.

Fix \(\delta>0\).  Define \(\mathcal D_{X,\delta}^{\rm JT}\) to be the
unital *-algebra generated by \(e^{itH_X}\) and by heat-kernel-regulated
matter insertions

\[
 \Phi_{X,\delta}(f):=e^{-\delta H_X}\Phi_X(f)e^{-\delta H_X},
\tag{4.3}
\]

where \(t\in\mathbb R\), \(f\) is a smooth compactly supported test function,
and \(\Phi_X(f):=\int dt\,f(t)\Phi_X(t)\).  PW use both real-time
and heat-kernel smearing at `refs/arxiv-2301.07257/Paper.tex:817-835`.  Define
\(\mathcal D_{LR,\delta}^{\rm JT}\) to be the *-algebra generated by the two
one-sided cores.  These are concrete candidate commutator domains; proving
their strong density and invariance under a proposed charge is part of each
problem, not an assumption hidden in the notation.
Define \(\mathcal D_{X,\delta}^{\rm biloc}\subset\mathcal D_{X,\delta}^{\rm JT}\)
to be the *-subalgebra generated by regulated
primary bilocals.  Extend (4.2) to this subalgebra by complex linearity, the
*-operation, and the Leibniz rule; proving that the extension respects all
operator relations and is closable is part of G3.

### G1. Determine the two-sided asymptotic group and its operational kernel

**Hypotheses.**  Take the Lorentzian two-boundary JT solution space with the
PW boundary conditions, the causal restriction
\(|T_L(t)-T_R(t')|<\pi\), and either (i) the no-wormhole physical algebra
\(\mathcal A_{LR}^{\rm JT}\), or (ii) a separately stated regulated wormhole
completion.  Fix an integer \(k\ge1\), give the boundary maps a specified
\(C^k\) topology, and impose
explicit falloff conditions at \(u_X\to\pm\infty\).

**Desired conclusion.**  Construct a topological group
\(\mathcal G_{\rm as}^{(2)}\subset\mathcal G_{\rm kin}^{\rm Lor}\) which
preserves the boundary and causal conditions, construct a strongly continuous
action \(g\mapsto\alpha_g\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal A_{LR}^{\rm JT})\),
and compute

\[
 K(\mathcal A_{LR}^{\rm JT})
 =\{g:\alpha_g=\operatorname{id}\}.
\tag{G1}
\]

In particular, prove or disprove that the identity component of this kernel is
exactly \(G_{0,\mathrm{diag}}\), and prove the analytic-continuation relation
between \(\mathcal G_{\rm as}^{(2)}\) and the single Euclidean orbit (1.2).

**Assessment.**  **Hard/conceptual.**  The Möbius kernel calculation is
mechanical once an action is given, but choosing the admissible falloffs and
showing that a general reparametrization induces an automorphism of the
physical algebra are not done in the sources.  **Negative outcome:** if every
non-Möbius transformation either violates the boundary conditions or acts
trivially after reduction, then the strong Diff version of corner A is absent,
although the finite \(SL(2)\)/Hamiltonian remnant remains.

### G2. Turn the three-component Schwarzian ledger into relational observables, or prove that this is impossible

**Hypotheses.**  Work in the physical PW Hilbert space with matter, restrict to
the balanced compact-source protocols of
`theory/lanes/syk-jt/t1-analytic.md:152-182,348-414`, and assume the
Schwarzian approximation to the large-\(N\) Layer-1 bilocals.  Fix the common
diagonal \(G_0\) gauge only as an intermediate coordinate choice.  Here a
*gauge section* means a choice of one representative from each diagonal
\(G_0\) orbit.

**Desired conclusion.**  Exhibit three self-adjoint relational operators
\(\widehat Q_{\rm rel}^a\), \(a\in\{-,0,+\}\), affiliated with
\(\mathcal A_{LR}^{\rm JT}\), and parity-even finite-\(N\) approximants
\(\widehat Q_{N,\rm rel}^a\in\mathfrak A_N\), such that:

1. their spectral statistics converge in every stated pulse protocol;
2. their unitaries preserve \(\mathcal D_{LR,\delta}^{\rm JT}\);
3. their action on mixed bilocals is the relative Möbius action; and
4. their early/late expectation-value ledger becomes (2.4) after the chosen
   gauge section is inserted, while the result itself is independent of that
   section.

An equally decisive solution is a no-go theorem: under the same hypotheses,
prove that no three such gauge-invariant operators exist and identify the
maximal observable quotient (for example, only the energy Casimir plus a
two-sided timeshift detector).

**Assessment.**  **Hard.**  Nonabelian right-minus-left components do not
automatically commute with the diagonal constraint, and PW's pure-JT algebra
shows that one-sided gauge invariance collapses the triple to its Casimir.
**Negative outcome:** if the no-go alternative holds even with matter and no
relational replacements exist, then the ledger used by
`theory/lanes/syk-jt/t1-analytic.md:73-118`
remains EFT bookkeeping rather than corner-A charge data.

### G3. Construct the first non-Möbius charge on an operational core

**Hypotheses.**  Take the \(N\to\infty\), low-energy SYK/JT scaling regime in
which Layer-1 bilocal moments converge.  Assume a *-preserving linear map
\(\iota\) from the Layer-1 bilocal core to
\(\mathcal D_{X,\delta}^{\rm biloc}\) that preserves every limiting joint
moment in the stated TFD protocols.  Choose the first cosine mode
\(\epsilon_{2,c}\) from (4.1).  Let \(r_N\to0\) be an explicitly proved error
scale that includes both finite-\(N\) and finite-coupling corrections; its form
must not be assumed from Schwarzian power counting.

**Desired conclusion.**  Exhibit a self-adjoint
\(Q[\epsilon_{2,c}]\) affiliated with a named algebra—either
\(\mathcal A_R^{\rm JT}\) for a genuinely one-boundary action or
\(\mathcal A_{LR}^{\rm JT}\) for an explicitly relational action—and
parity-even \(Q_N[\epsilon_{2,c}]\in\mathfrak A_N\).  Also exhibit a dense
invariant Hilbert-space core \(\mathscr C_2\) such that, as a quadratic-form
identity on \(\mathscr C_2\),

\[
 i[Q[\epsilon_{2,c}],A]
 =\delta_{\epsilon_{2,c}}^{\rm biloc}A,
 \qquad A\in\mathcal D_{X,\delta}^{\rm biloc},
\tag{G3a}
\]

with the evident two-sided replacement of the core when needed, and such that
all regulated commutator matrix elements of \(Q_N\) converge to (G3a) with
error bounded by \(C_{A,\delta}r_N\), where
\(C_{A,\delta}<\infty\) is independent of \(N\).  Prove essential
self-adjointness on \(\mathscr C_2\), or construct the charge directly from
its strongly continuous unitary group.  Finally, state whether it is
conserved, approximately conserved with a controlled breaking insertion, or
only a kinematic generator.

**Assessment.**  **Hard but maximally diagnostic.**  It is only one mode and
one regulated algebra, so no infinite-mode summability is needed; the hard
part is spatial implementation and the microscopic approximation.  **Negative
outcome:** a proof that (4.2) is an outer/nonimplementable derivation on every
candidate operational algebra, or that every approximant has an uncontrolled
Ward defect, removes the first generator beyond \(SL(2)\).

### G4. Close the full algebra and determine the extension

**Hypotheses.**  Suppose G3 has been solved for every real mode in (4.1), all
charges preserve one common operator test algebra \(\mathcal D\) and one
common dense Hilbert-space core \(\mathscr C\), their finite-\(N\)
approximants obey uniform energy bounds, and the map
\(\epsilon\mapsto Q[\epsilon]\) is continuous in a specified Sobolev topology
on vector fields.

**Desired conclusion.**  Prove (3.2) on \(\mathscr C\); determine the cocycle
\(K\) up to coboundary; prove that the corresponding projective unitary
representation exponentiates on the chosen cover of
\(\operatorname{Diff}_+(S^1)\); and show that the restriction to the Möbius
subalgebra agrees with PW's bracket (2.5) before reduction and with the
gauge-invariant content of (2.4) after reduction.  For a smooth
\(\epsilon\), define its hard-flux functional by

\[
 \mathcal J[\epsilon]
 :=\int_{u_i}^{u_f}du\;t'T_{tz}\epsilon(t),
\tag{G4a}
 \]

and require a Layer-1 allowed-observable realization of this expression.  Let
\(\omega_i,\omega_f\) be the normal incoming and outgoing protocol states,
assumed to have finite first moment for \(Q[\epsilon]\), and let
\(\mathcal E[\epsilon]\) be an explicitly derived finite-coupling breaking
term.  Prove the integrated Ward identity

\[
 \omega_f(Q[\epsilon])-\omega_i(Q[\epsilon])
 =-\mathcal J[\epsilon]+\mathcal E[\epsilon].
\tag{G4}
\]

For the three Möbius modes, prove that the relevant breaking term vanishes and
that (G4) reduces componentwise to the ledger (2.4).

**Assessment.**  **Hard analytic, then mechanical algebra.**  Once common
domains and commutators exist, checking the cocycle identity and changing it
by charge counterterms are mechanical.  Proving common domains,
exponentiation, and uniform mode bounds is not.  **Negative outcome:** if the
commutator cannot be defined on a common invariant core, “Virasoro-like” is
only a saddle-space analogy; if it closes with \(K=0\), corner A survives with
no central extension.

### G5. Relate the LL type-III algebra to the PW type-II algebra without changing the observable question

**Hypotheses.**  Assume the LL conjecture that
\(\mathcal M_R^{\rm LL}\) is a type-\(\mathrm{III}_1\) factor in the selected
large-\(N\) thermal representation.  Let \(\sigma_t\) be its modular
automorphism group with respect to \(\Omega_\beta\), and define the crossed
product \(\mathcal M_R^{\rm LL}\rtimes_\sigma\mathbb R\) to be the von Neumann
algebra generated by \(\mathcal M_R^{\rm LL}\) and unitaries implementing
\(\sigma_t\).

**Desired conclusion.**  Construct a normal faithful *-homomorphism from the LL algebra
and its Layer-1 bilocal core into \(\mathcal A_R^{\rm JT}\), and either prove a
state-preserving isomorphism

\[
 \mathcal M_R^{\rm LL}\rtimes_\sigma\mathbb R
 \cong\mathcal A_R^{\rm JT}
\tag{G5}
\]

in a precisely delimited Schwarzian sector, or state and prove the corrected
inclusion/corner relation.  Under that map identify which self-adjoint
operator is the boundary Hamiltonian, where its spectral projections live,
and whether every proposed \(Q[\epsilon]\) is inner, outer, or absent on each
side.

**Assessment.**  **Hard operator algebra.**  PW establish a high-energy
crossed-product description, not (G5) globally, while LL's factor type is
itself conjectural in the cited paper.  **Negative outcome:** inequivalent
representations or failure of a covariant inclusion would mean that a charge
constructed in one Layer-2 completion cannot be advertised as a charge in the
other without a new scaling theorem.

### G6. Control explicit breaking and formulate the refutation test

**Hypotheses.**  Let \(Q_N[\epsilon]\) be candidate Layer-0 approximants for a
non-Möbius smooth \(\epsilon\), let \(H_N:=H_{L,N}+H_{R,N}\) be the decoupled
two-copy Hamiltonian outside the compact pulse, and let
\(\mathcal B_N[\epsilon]\in\mathfrak A_N\) be an explicitly identified
parity-even breaking insertion (for example, the contribution of the UV term
discarded in the conformal collective action).  Fix a family of low-energy
TFD-sector vectors \(\Psi_N\in\mathcal H_N\) generated by a bounded number of
Layer-1 insertions, and use the error scale \(r_N\) defined in G3.

**Desired conclusion.**  Prove a Ward-defect estimate

\[
 \left|\langle\Psi_N,
 \big(i[H_N,Q_N[\epsilon]]-\mathcal B_N[\epsilon]\big)
 \Psi_N\rangle\right|
 \le C_{\epsilon,\Psi}\,r_N,
 \qquad r_N\longrightarrow0,
\tag{G6}
\]

uniformly for detector times in a stated conformal window, and show that
inserting \(\mathcal B_N[\epsilon]\) gives the same approximate Ward identity
as the bilocal variation (4.2).  Here \(C_{\epsilon,\Psi}<\infty\) must be
independent of \(N\).  Specify the order of the \(N\), coupling, and
late-time limits.

**Assessment.**  **Hard/model-analytic.**  Computing the formal breaking term
is mechanical; proving a uniform operational bound and the limit order is the
substantive work.  **Corner-A refutation criterion:** if G1 yields no
non-Möbius operational action and G2--G6 show that every candidate beyond the
finite constraint sector is either non-observable, pure gauge, or explicitly
broken with no controlled approximate Ward identity, then the strong
Diff-charge version of corner A is refuted.  What remains would be the exact
Hamiltonian, the diagonal \(SL(2)\) constraint, and a useful Schwarzian
pseudo-Goldstone response—not an operational asymptotic Diff charge algebra.

## 5. Verdict

Corner A currently has a precisely identified reparametrization soft orbit, a diagonal \(\widetilde{SL}(2,\mathbb R)\) gauge constraint, an ordinary pre-reduction \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) ledger, an operational boundary Hamiltonian, and mixed correlators that detect relative frame data.  It lacks a source-backed non-Möbius charge affiliated with either named Layer-2 algebra, finite-\(N\) even-CAR approximants for such charges, a common-domain charge algebra and extension, and a theorem relating the LL and PW completions.  The single cheapest decisive step is G3 for \(\epsilon_{2,c}=\cos 2\tau\): construct—or rule out—one regulated, operational first mode beyond \(SL(2)\) with a controlled finite-\(N\)/finite-coupling Ward defect.
