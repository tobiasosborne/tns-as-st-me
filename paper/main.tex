\begin{abstract}
The infrared triangle of gauge theory and gravity---soft theorem, asymptotic
symmetry, memory effect---has no lattice formulation. We give it one, in
quantum spin chains. We prove that a soft magnon of the Heisenberg ferromagnet
decouples with the universal phase $2k$; that the
symmetry behind this acts at spatial infinity as operators on matrix-product
bond space, with central extension the SPT index; and that a magnon crossing
a domain wall drags it by exactly two sites---a memory effect with its own
Adler zero. Bethe-ansatz, exact-diagonalization, and wavepacket numerics
verify each claim.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

A ferromagnetic spin chain contains two objects that can be pictured without
formalism: the \emph{magnon}, a single overturned spin spreading as a wave
with energy $\omega(k)=J(1-\cos k)$, and the \emph{domain wall}, the boundary
between a region of up spins and a region of down spins. This Letter concerns
what each does when the magnon's momentum $k$ is taken small, and the
symmetry bookkeeping that controls both. The whole result in one sentence: on
the lattice, a soft magnon decouples from scattering with the universal phase
$2k$; the symmetry responsible for this acts at the two ends of the chain as
explicit operators on the bond space of a matrix product state; and a magnon
that crosses a domain wall drags it backward by exactly two lattice sites, so
the wall keeps a quantized memory of every transmission.

In continuum field theory these three phenomena---soft theorems, asymptotic
symmetries, memory effects---form the \emph{infrared triangle}, three faces
of one Ward identity, established for photons and gravitons at null infinity
\cite{Strominger2017,Strominger2014}. Hamada and Sugishita extended the
triangle to Goldstone bosons of a spontaneously broken global symmetry
\cite{Hamada2017}; scalar and fracton versions followed
\cite{Campiglia2017,Perez2022,Perez2023}, together with local derivations
that avoid null infinity \cite{DeLuca2024} and a careful account of what
infrared finiteness requires \cite{Prabhu2022}. Each ingredient separately
also has a condensed-matter ancestor. Dyson showed in 1956 that ferromagnetic
magnons decouple at long wavelength \cite{Dyson1956}; the Adler zeros of
nonrelativistic Goldstone effective theories are by now systematic
\cite{WatanabeMurayama2012,NielsenChadha1976,Mojahed2021,Mojahed2022,Cheung2023,Gongyo2016};
and magnon-driven domain-wall motion is a working subject in magnonics, where
Lan and Xiao described the wall's displacement as a gravity-like two-body
collision \cite{Lan2021,Kim2014,Kim2017}. What has been missing is any
statement on the lattice: the infrared-structure literature and the
tensor-network literature do not intersect. This Letter supplies the lattice
statement, at finite spacing, with no continuum limit and no expansion
parameter, and verifies each corner against exact and numerical computation.

\emph{Setting.---}An infinite chain of spin-$s$ sites; a finite-range
Hamiltonian $H$; an on-site symmetry group $G$ acting by $u(g)$ at every
site; and a translation-invariant injective matrix product ground state with
tensor $A^{\sigma}_{ij}$ and bond (virtual) dimension $\chi$. One structural
fact is used throughout, the fundamental theorem of MPS symmetries
\cite{PerezGarcia2008,Cirac2020}: if the state is $G$-invariant, then acting
with $u(g)$ on the physical index of $A$ equals a phase times
$V(g)^{-1}A\,V(g)$, where $V(g)$ is a projective representation on the bond
space whose class $[\omega]\in H^{2}(G,U(1))$ is the symmetry-protected
topological (SPT) index of the phase \cite{Cheng2018,Else2019}. Excitations
are momentum superpositions of local tensor replacements---the tangent-space
ansatz \cite{Haegeman2011,Haegeman2013,Vanderstraeten2018}---whose
$S$-matrix is computable on the lattice
\cite{Vanderstraeten2013,Vanderstraeten2015}. All theorems below are proved
in the Supplemental Material; each carries a machine-checked numerical
certificate, and we quote the checked precision in place.

\emph{Asymptotic symmetry.---}Apply the symmetry to a finite region $R$
only: $U_R(g)=\prod_{x\in R}u(g)$. Using the intertwining relation once per
site, the $V$'s cancel pairwise throughout the interior of $R$, and the
whole operation collapses to two bond operators, $V(g)^{-1}$ on the bond
entering $R$ and $V(g)$ on the bond leaving it. Everything a truncated
symmetry does, it does at its two ends. Sending the ends to $\pm\infty$
turns this into an asymptotic-symmetry statement, and three facts hold.
(i)~On states, a half-infinite symmetry string acts \emph{exactly} as a
single bond insertion; as an operator sequence it converges strongly if and
only if $V(g)$ is a phase, so whenever the symmetry acts nontrivially on the
bond space there is no strongly convergent implementer at all---the lattice
counterpart of a large gauge transformation, distinguished from a global
symmetry by its failure to converge. (ii)~The bond implementers compose as
the twisted group algebra $\mathbb{C}_{\omega}[G]$: the SPT class is
precisely the central extension of the asymptotic charge algebra. (iii)~If
the symmetry is spontaneously broken, the half-infinite string leaves the
ground-state sector altogether: it converges (in expectation values) to a
\emph{domain-wall state}. Kinks are what broken asymptotic symmetries
create, and the kink superselection sectors are labelled by ordered pairs of
vacua. On the lattice, an asymptotic symmetry is not a fall-off condition;
it is a concrete algebra of $\chi\times\chi$ matrices. This reading of the
MPO-symmetry technology \cite{Williamson2014,GarreRubio2022} is, to our
knowledge, new.

\emph{The soft theorem.---}Now fix the concrete model: the spin-$\frac12$
Heisenberg ferromagnet $H=-J\sum_x(\mathbf{S}_x\!\cdot\!\mathbf{S}_{x+1}
-\tfrac14)$. Its fully polarized state is an exact product ground state
($\chi=1$), magnons are exact one-particle eigenstates with
$\omega(k)=J(1-\cos k)$ and velocity $v(k)=J\sin k$, and $SU(2)$ is broken
to $U(1)$, so the magnon is a type-B Goldstone mode
\cite{WatanabeMurayama2012}. Write $S_{12}(k_s,k_h)=\ec^{\ic\delta}$ for the
exact two-magnon scattering amplitude in the convention where the incoming
particle carrying $k_h$ is the faster one.

\begin{theorem}[soft magnon theorem, two-body]\label{thm:soft}
Fix a compact $I\Subset(0,\pi)$. For $k_h\in I$ and $k_s\to 0$,
\begin{equation}
\delta(k_s,k_h)\;=\;2\,k_s\;+\;\frac{v(k_h)}{\omega(k_h)}\,k_s^{2}
\;+\;O(k_s^{3})
\label{eq:soft}
\end{equation}
uniformly on $I$; reversing which particle is soft flips the sign of the
linear term and nothing else. Equivalently, the connected amplitude
factorizes, $M_2(k_s,k_h)=2\ic k_s\,M_1(k_h)+R$ with
$\|R\|\le C_I\,k_s^{2}\,\|M_1\|$.
\end{theorem}

The zeroth order, $S_{12}\to1$, is Dyson's decoupling \cite{Dyson1956}. The
content of the theorem is the next two orders. The linear coefficient is the
pure number $2$: independent of the hard momentum, of the coupling, of
everything. Hard-leg data enters first at second order, through the single
invariant $v_h/\omega_h$. The $2$ is not a scattering length---the
ferromagnet's scattering length vanishes \cite{Gongyo2016}---it is a
\emph{displacement}: $d\delta/dk_s=2$ states that the soft packet exits the
collision shifted by two lattice sites. We derive it without solving the
model, from two ingredients the symmetry supplies: the operator identity
$[H,Q_k]=(\ec^{\ic k}-1)J_k$ obeyed by the deformed broken charge
$Q_k=\sum_x \ec^{\ic kx}S^-_x$ and its current $J_k$, and the single contact
term of the exchange interaction. The residue that fixes the coefficient is
a current matrix element, $\braket{k_h|Q_0^{\dagger}J_0|k_h}=2\ic\,v(k_h)$:
charge times velocity, the lattice image of the continuum soft-current
analysis \cite{Hamada2017,Mojahed2021}. The closed Bethe form of $S_{12}$
enters only as verification; the hypotheses are symmetry, injectivity, and
finite range, not integrability.

Three independent checks agree (Fig.~\ref{fig:soft-bethe}). The closed-form
expansion reproduces Eq.~\eqref{eq:soft} exactly. Exact diagonalization on
rings of $18$--$24$ sites reproduces the two-magnon eigenvectors to
$10^{-15}$ and the two coefficients to $10^{-10}$. And real-time wavepacket
collisions \cite{VanDamme2019} measure the soft packet's displacement
directly: it matches $-d\delta/dk_s$ computed from the exact amplitude to
parts in $10^{5}$ at clean kinematics, for both signs of $k_s$.

We conjecture the general statement---Conjecture~S: for scattering states
with $n$ hard legs on any injective MPS vacuum with broken continuous
symmetry, $M_{n+1}=2\ic k_s M_n+o(k_s)$ with the same coefficient. What
separates theorem from conjecture is stated precisely in the Supplemental
Material as two lemmas: uniform regularity of current form factors under
wavepacket smearing, and an LSZ-type reduction showing the charge descendant
dominates the soft amplitude. We have proved neither in general; both hold
explicitly in the two-body sector.

\emph{Memory.---}Memory needs a wall, so change the model minimally: the
easy-axis XXZ ferromagnet, anisotropy $\Delta>1$, with its two product vacua
$\ket{\cdots\uparrow\uparrow\cdots}$, $\ket{\cdots\downarrow\downarrow\cdots}$
and a domain wall between them. Send in a magnon wavepacket with central
momentum $k_0$ and ask where the wall sits after the collision
(Fig.~\ref{fig:memory}). Read the wall position from a window $W$ around it,
$\mathfrak{X}_W=\mathrm{const}+\frac{1}{2s}\sum_{x\in W}(S^z_x+s)$. This
observable changes only through the $U(1)$ currents on the two boundary
bonds of $W$---an exact operator identity---so its total change over the
event is the zero-frequency weight of the boundary current. Memory is the DC
component of a current at the edge of the observation window: the lattice
image of the continuum statement that memory is stored in the potential at
the boundary \cite{Strominger2014,DeLuca2024}. Charge bookkeeping then gives
the memory law.

\begin{theorem}[memory law]\label{thm:memory}
Assume the state before and after the collision decomposes into a wall in
$W$ plus free magnon content outside it (hypothesis H-AD, Supplemental
Material). Then
\begin{equation}
\delta x\;=\;-\frac{1}{s}\braket{N_T}
\;=\;-\frac{1}{s}\int\!\frac{dk}{2\pi}\,|\varphi(k)|^{2}\,T(k),
\label{eq:memory}
\end{equation}
where $T$ is the wall's transmission probability and $\varphi$ the packet:
each transmitted magnon displaces the wall by exactly $-1/s$ sites---two
sites for spin~$\frac12$---and each reflected one by zero.
\end{theorem}

The displacement is quantized. It does not depend on $k_0$, on $\Delta$, on
the packet shape, or on any scattering phase; those enter only through
$T(k)$. Sparse-sector numerics on chains of up to $200$ sites confirm
$\delta x=-2\braket{N_T}$ to $0.004$ sites, with six-figure convergence in
system size. The transmission itself we obtain in closed form in the
dominant domain-wall sector, where the kink--magnon problem reduces to a
side-coupled (Fano) level:
$t(k)=[1+\ic J^{2}/4\omega(k)v(k)]^{-1}$ with
$\omega(k)=J(\Delta-\cos k)$, matching the measured reflection over
$\Delta\in[1.5,12]$ to a few percent with no fitted parameter. As
$k_0\to0$,
\begin{equation}
T(k_0)=16(\Delta-1)^{2}k_0^{2}+O(k_0^{4}):
\end{equation}
a soft magnon is totally reflected and leaves no memory. The memory effect
has an Adler zero of its own. One warning earned by this analysis: the
naive transcription of the continuum memory formula---displacement equals
the DC limit of the soft factor---is \emph{false} here; the wall's quantum
$1/s$ is a charge, not a phase, and the soft data enters only through
$T$. And one observation we can state but not yet derive: the two $2$'s of
this Letter---the soft displacement $d\delta/dk_s=2$ and the memory quantum
$1/s=2$---are numerically the same and dimensionally the same object, the
$U(1)$ charge transported past an observer in units of the vacuum spin
density. We conjecture they are one invariant; the spin-$1$ chain, where
the memory quantum becomes one site, will falsify or confirm this.

Finally, the third edge closes at the level of bookkeeping: $\delta x$
records which ordered pair of vacua the chain interpolates between before
and after the event, which is exactly the data labelling the orbit of the
broken asymptotic symmetry. Measuring the memory identifies the asymptotic
transformation that acted.

\emph{The SPT index in soft amplitudes.---}Does the central extension
$[\omega]$ of the asymptotic charge algebra show up in scattering? Our
preliminary analysis points to a dichotomy, which we state as a conjecture:
in the bulk, $[\omega]$ cancels from soft amplitudes at every order, because
only the adjoint action of $V(g)$ enters there; at an open edge or a
symmetry twist a single unpaired $V(g)$ survives, and the soft response then
detects $[\omega]$---through degeneracies and selection rules rather than
through modified expansion coefficients. This is consistent with the
continuum principle that anomalies constrain soft limits
\cite{Huang2015,Copetti2024} and with the anomalous-edge analysis of MPO
symmetries \cite{Williamson2014,GarreRubio2022,GarreRubio2024}; the bulk
half is within reach of the methods above, and we expect to settle it.

\emph{Outlook.---}Three directions are concrete. The spin-$1$ chain tests
the two-$2$'s conjecture, since the memory quantum halves while the soft
phase can be computed independently. Higher dimensions replace bond
matrices by the virtual symmetries of projected entangled-pair states,
where the same interior cancellation applies. And magnonic and cold-atom
platforms already resolve single-site wall displacements and host
gravitational-wave analogues \cite{Lan2021,Chojnacki2023}; a two-site kick
per transmitted magnon is an observable, not a metaphor. The asymmetry of
our opening---magnon and wall as unrelated objects---has disappeared: the
wall is the broken asymptotic symmetry made visible, and the magnon's soft
phase and the wall's memory are the same conserved charge read out in two
ways. The infrared structure of gauge theory and gravity, long tied to null
infinity, fits on a chain of spins, where every step of it can be checked.
